This practice involves cybersecurity specialists attempting to exploit identified or potential weaknesses in an organization's digital assets. The objective is to evaluate the robustness of security controls and uncover exploitable flaws, such as misconfigurations, software bugs, or weak authentication mechanisms, from an adversarial perspective. Unlike a vulnerability scan, which merely identifies potential weaknesses, penetration testing actively attempts to bypass security measures to demonstrate the feasibility and impact of an attack.

Upon completion, testers provide a comprehensive report detailing all discovered vulnerabilities, their severity, the methods used to exploit them, and practical recommendations for remediation. Organizations often conduct penetration tests as part of their regular security audits, to meet regulatory compliance requirements, or before deploying new systems. For instance, a company might commission a penetration test on its new e-commerce platform to ensure customer data is adequately protected before launch. This process is common in industries subject to strict data protection regulations and for any entity managing sensitive information or critical infrastructure.
