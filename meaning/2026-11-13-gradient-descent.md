This method works by repeatedly moving in the direction of the steepest descent of the function. Imagine you are standing on a hillside in the fog and want to reach the lowest point. You would feel around for the steepest downward slope from your current position and take a step in that direction. Then, you would repeat this process, always moving towards the steepest downhill path.

The "gradient" refers to the slope of the function at a given point, indicating the direction and steepness of the curve. "Descent" means moving downwards. By taking small steps in the negative direction of the gradient, the algorithm progressively approaches a local minimum. The size of these steps is controlled by a parameter called the "learning rate."

For example, to train a machine learning model, the algorithm adjusts its internal parameters to minimize an error function.

This technique is widely employed in machine learning, deep learning, and various fields of mathematics and engineering for optimization tasks.
