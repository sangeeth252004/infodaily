NEW DELHI – The Union Cabinet of India has given its approval to a new draft bill aimed at preventing malpractices in public examinations, introducing stringent penalties that include imprisonment of up to 10 years and a fine of up to Rs 10 crore. This legislative development addresses long-standing concerns regarding the integrity and fairness of recruitment and admission tests conducted across the nation.

The proposed legislation, reportedly named "The Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Bill," seeks to establish a robust legal framework to deter and punish individuals and organizations involved in exam paper leaks, cheating, impersonation, and other unfair means. The move follows numerous incidents where widespread malpractices have led to the cancellation of examinations, causing distress to millions of aspirants and eroding public trust in the examination system.

The bill's primary objective is to safeguard the interests of genuine candidates and ensure that the selection process for government jobs and academic admissions remains merit-based. It is designed to cover examinations conducted by central government agencies, including but not limited to the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), Staff Selection Commission (SSC), Railway Recruitment Boards (RRBs), and various other central departments and ministries.

Key provisions reportedly outlined in the bill include:

*   **Severe Penalties for Organized Crime:** Individuals or groups found to be organizing or facilitating paper leaks and other forms of organized cheating could face a maximum imprisonment term of 10 years and a fine extending up to Rs 10 crore.
*   **Consequences for Examination Service Providers:** The bill is expected to hold examination service providers, such as testing agencies and their employees, accountable for security breaches or lapses that lead to unfair practices.
*   **Protection for Whistleblowers:** Provisions may be included to protect individuals who report incidents of malpractices, encouraging transparency and accountability within the system.
*   **Scope of Offenses:** Beyond paper leaks, the legislation is anticipated to cover a broad range of unfair means, including tampering with OMR sheets, manipulation of results, creating fake examination materials, and unauthorized access to examination systems.
*   **Jurisdiction:** The law is intended to apply uniformly to all public examinations conducted by central government bodies, ensuring a consistent approach to tackling malpractices nationwide.

This legislative initiative underscores the government's commitment to upholding transparency and fairness in the public examination system. By imposing tougher legal consequences, the administration aims to create a stronger deterrent against those who seek to undermine the credibility of these vital assessments, thereby securing the future prospects of deserving candidates.

Following its clearance by the Union Cabinet, the Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Bill is now slated for introduction in Parliament. Its eventual passage will mark a significant step towards reinforcing the sanctity and reliability of public examinations across India.
