NEW DELHI – Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan tendered his resignation from the Cabinet on Wednesday, July 31, 2024, amidst widespread student protests and mounting public pressure regarding alleged irregularities in competitive examinations, including the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (NEET-UG) and the UGC-National Eligibility Test (UGC-NET). The President of India formally accepted Mr. Pradhan's resignation shortly thereafter.

The resignation marks a significant development following weeks of intense scrutiny over the conduct of examinations administered by the National Testing Agency (NTA), an autonomous organization under the Ministry of Education. Student bodies, opposition parties, and civil society groups across the country had called for accountability and reforms after a series of controversies eroded trust in the national examination system.

The core of the student unrest stemmed from two major examinations:

*   **NEET-UG 2024:** The undergraduate medical entrance examination, conducted on May 5, saw allegations of paper leaks, inflated scores, and the arbitrary award of "grace marks" to several candidates. Over 2.3 million students appeared for the examination. The Supreme Court of India initiated proceedings and ordered a re-test for 1,563 candidates who received grace marks. Several arrests were also made in connection with alleged paper leaks in states like Bihar and Gujarat.
*   **UGC-NET June 2024:** This examination, for junior research fellowship and assistant professorship, was conducted on June 18. Just one day later, the Ministry of Education announced its cancellation, citing reports from the National Cyber Crime Threat Analytics Unit suggesting the integrity of the examination may have been compromised. Approximately 900,000 candidates were affected by this cancellation.

In response to the growing crisis, the Ministry of Education had taken several measures under Mr. Pradhan's tenure. These included the formation of a high-level committee of experts to review the functioning of the NTA and recommend improvements in examination processes. Additionally, the government operationalized the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, which aims to curb malpractices and irregularities in public examinations, prescribing stringent penalties for offenders.

Despite these measures, protests escalated, with student organizations demanding a thorough investigation, re-examination for NEET-UG, and greater transparency from the NTA. Opposition parties had also consistently called for Mr. Pradhan's resignation, asserting a failure of leadership in addressing the concerns of millions of students and their families.

Following Mr. Pradhan's resignation, the Ministry of Education is expected to announce an interim arrangement or a new Union Minister to take charge of the portfolio. The government faces the immediate challenge of restoring public confidence in the integrity of its national examination systems and ensuring the swift and fair resolution of the ongoing investigations into alleged examination malpractices. The high-level committee's recommendations for NTA reforms are anticipated to play a crucial role in shaping the future of competitive examinations in India.
