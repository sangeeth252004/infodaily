New Delhi, India – Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan has stepped down from his position following weeks of widespread protests and public criticism regarding alleged irregularities in national examinations. The development comes as the Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP) announced the cessation of its nationwide demonstrations after receiving explicit assurances from the Central Government for decisive action and comprehensive systemic reforms aimed at safeguarding the integrity of the examination process.

Mr. Pradhan’s departure from the cabinet, confirmed by an official announcement from the Prime Minister’s Office on June 25, 2024, follows intense scrutiny over the conduct of critical national entrance tests. These include, notably, the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (Undergraduate) (NEET-UG) and the University Grants Commission – National Eligibility Test (UGC-NET). Student bodies, civil society organizations, and political opposition had consistently demanded accountability and a complete overhaul of the examination system.

The CJP-led protests, which commenced on June 18, 2024, mobilized thousands of students and concerned citizens across major cities. The organization highlighted systemic vulnerabilities that allegedly led to paper leaks and compromised results, eroding public trust in educational institutions. CJP’s primary demands included a thorough investigation into all irregularities, stringent penalties for those involved in malpractices, and the implementation of robust mechanisms to prevent future occurrences.

In response to the escalating protests and growing public discontent, the Central Government engaged in discussions with protest leaders, culminating in a series of assurances announced on June 24, 2024. These commitments address key concerns raised by the protesting groups and signal a proactive approach from the government to restore confidence in the examination framework.

Key assurances and actions announced by the Centre include:

*   **High-Level Committee Formation:** The establishment of an expert committee, to be headed by a retired Supreme Court judge, tasked with reviewing the entire examination administration process, from question paper setting to result declaration. The committee is expected to submit its recommendations within three months.
*   **Enforcement of New Legislation:** Immediate implementation of the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, which seeks to curb malpractices and irregularities in public examinations. This law includes provisions for imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines of up to ₹1 crore for those involved in paper leaks and other unfair means.
*   **Re-evaluation and Re-conduct:** A decision to re-conduct the UGC-NET examination for all registered candidates due to compromised integrity. For NEET-UG, specific batches of candidates whose results were under scrutiny for grace marks will have the option to reappear for the test.
*   **Establishment of National Testing Agency Review Panel:** An independent panel will be formed to review the functioning and standard operating procedures of the National Testing Agency (NTA), the autonomous body responsible for conducting these national examinations, to enhance its transparency and efficiency.

A CJP spokesperson stated that while the organization views these assurances as a positive first step, they will closely monitor the implementation of the promised reforms. The spokesperson emphasized the importance of sustained vigilance to ensure that the government's commitments translate into tangible improvements in the integrity of the examination system.

The stepping down of Minister Pradhan and the government's rapid response underscore the significant pressure exerted by the public and civil society on issues of educational equity and integrity. Further details on the composition of the expert committees and the specific timelines for implementing the proposed reforms are expected to be announced in the coming weeks. The government aims to regain the trust of millions of students and their families by demonstrating a firm commitment to fairness and transparency in national examinations.
