The Best Before Company has issued a recall for five specific novelty chocolate products across Britain due to the undeclaration of milk and soya on their packaging. This action, prompted by alerts from the Food Standards Agency (FSA) and Food Standards Scotland (FSS), aims to mitigate potential health risks for consumers with allergies or intolerances to these ingredients. The recall notice applies to all batch codes and best before dates of the affected items.

The undeclared allergens pose a significant health concern for individuals with milk or soya allergies, who could experience adverse reactions ranging from mild symptoms to severe anaphylaxis if they consume the products. For the general public, the chocolates present no immediate health risk. The Best Before Company, based in Southall, London, initiated the recall as a precautionary measure to ensure consumer safety and compliance with food labelling regulations.

The FSA and FSS are government bodies responsible for food safety and food hygiene in England, Wales, Northern Ireland, and Scotland, respectively. Their role involves issuing alerts when food products are found to be unsafe, defective, or mislabelled. In this instance, the failure to declare milk and soya, which are among the 14 major allergens requiring clear identification on food labels under UK law, led to the immediate advisory. Accurate allergen labelling is critical for consumers to make informed purchasing decisions, especially those managing dietary restrictions dueenced by allergies.

The recalled products include brightly coloured chocolates shaped as various items. Consumers who have purchased any of the following items are advised not to consume them:

*   **Milk Chocolate Cars**
*   **Milk Chocolate Hearts**
*   **Milk Chocolate Butterflies**
*   **Milk Chocolate Ladybirds**
*   **Milk Chocolate Dinosaurs**

Customers who have bought these chocolates and have an allergy or intolerance to milk or soya are advised to return the product to their point of purchase for a full refund. Proof of purchase, such as a receipt, may not be required for the return process, though consumers are encouraged to verify this with their retailer.

The Food Standards Agency has disseminated the recall information through its official channels and continues to monitor the situation. This incident underscores the ongoing importance of stringent quality control and accurate labelling within the food industry to protect public health. Both regulatory bodies maintain that companies have a legal and ethical responsibility to ensure all potential allergens are clearly listed on product packaging. The Best Before Company has expressed its regret for the oversight and is cooperating with authorities to ensure all affected products are removed from sale and appropriate corrective actions are implemented to prevent future occurrences.
