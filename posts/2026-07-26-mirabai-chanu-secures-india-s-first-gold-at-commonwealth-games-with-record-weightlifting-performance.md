Saikhom Mirabai Chanu secured India's first gold medal at the 21st Commonwealth Games in Gold Coast, Australia, on April 5, 2018. Competing in the Women's 48kg weightlifting category, Chanu delivered a dominant performance, setting new Commonwealth Games records in snatch, clean and jerk, and total lift with an aggregate of 196kg. Her victory marked a significant moment for the Indian contingent at the multi-sport event.

The Indian lifter showcased exceptional strength and technique throughout the competition. In the snatch segment, Chanu successfully lifted 80kg on her first attempt, followed by 84kg, and then a Games-record 86kg on her third lift, surpassing her own national record. This performance established a substantial lead early in the event. She continued her record-breaking streak in the clean and jerk section, executing lifts of 103kg, 107kg, and ultimately 110kg, each setting a new Commonwealth Games benchmark. Her final clean and jerk lift of 110kg, combined with her 86kg snatch, culminated in a total lift of 196kg, eclipsing the previous Games record by a considerable margin.

Chanu's achievement underscored her consistent performance and dedication to the sport. The win in Gold Coast followed her silver medal performance at the 2014 Glasgow Commonwealth Games, demonstrating a progression in her competitive career. Her gold medal provided an early boost to India's overall medal tally, setting a positive tone for the team in Australia. The event took place at the Carrara Sports and Leisure Centre, attracting considerable attention from sports enthusiasts and media globally.

This victory held national significance, as Chanu's disciplined training and resilience culminated in a world-class performance on an international stage. Her win was celebrated across India, highlighting the potential of Indian athletes in global sporting events. The record-breaking lifts also cemented her position as one of the premier weightlifters in her category worldwide, just months after she had become the first Indian woman in over two decades to win a gold medal at the World Weightlifting Championships in 2017.
