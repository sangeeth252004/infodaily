A Supreme Court judge recently drew attention to the critical issue of delayed bail, underscoring its profound implications for personal liberty and the efficiency of the criminal justice system. The remarks were made during a lecture delivered at the National Law Institute University (NLIU), where the judge highlighted instances of individuals being detained for actions not explicitly prohibited by law.

During the address, the judge used a vivid illustration to make the point, stating, "No law bars eating chicken over Ganga." This example served to emphasize how individuals might face prolonged incarceration or legal action based on perceptions rather than clear legal statutes, thereby challenging the fundamental principles of due process and the right to liberty. The critique focused on how the current bail system often leads to unnecessary pre-trial detention, contributing to systemic issues within the judiciary.

The observations from the Supreme Court bench member reflect a broader concern regarding the application of Article 21 of the Constitution, which guarantees the right to life and personal liberty. Delayed bail processes have been consistently identified as a significant factor in prison overcrowding, with a substantial portion of the inmate population comprising under-trial prisoners awaiting resolution of their bail applications or trials. Such delays can often lead to the de facto punishment of individuals even before any conviction, contradicting the foundational principle of "innocent until proven guilty."

Key points raised by the judge implicitly included:
*   The necessity for judicial officers to rigorously examine the legal basis for detention and ensure that bail is not arbitrarily denied.
*   The importance of adhering to legal principles over social or cultural biases when considering matters of personal liberty.
*   The impact of prolonged pre-trial detention on an individual's life, including their employment, family, and mental health.
*   The systemic burden placed on the justice system by a large number of under-trial prisoners, affecting overall judicial efficiency.

The judge's comments are anticipated to reignite discussions within legal circles, among policymakers, and across the judiciary regarding the urgent need for bail reform. Calls for greater judicial accountability in bail decisions and a more streamlined process for granting liberty, especially in cases where specific legal prohibitions are absent, are expected to intensify. The remarks serve as a critical reminder of the judiciary's role in safeguarding constitutional rights and ensuring that the justice system operates effectively and humanely.
