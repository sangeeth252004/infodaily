New Delhi and Washington have significantly advanced their strategic collaboration across defence and civil nuclear sectors, with a pronounced emphasis on enhancing critical supply chains and leveraging artificial intelligence. These developments follow the recent conclusion of the 11th India-US Dialogue on Civil Nuclear Energy and comprehensive meetings of the India-US Defence Policy Group (DPG).

The intensified cooperation underscores a shared commitment to fortifying the India-US Comprehensive Global Strategic Partnership. Discussions during these dialogues focused on a range of initiatives aimed at fostering co-development, co-production, and bolstering resilience in critical technology and manufacturing ecosystems. Both nations acknowledged the imperative to secure supply chains, particularly in defence manufacturing, and to explore joint advancements in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and other emerging technologies.

In the defence domain, the two nations reviewed progress on the "Roadmap for US-India Defence Industrial Cooperation," which was established in June 2023. This roadmap aims to accelerate technological collaboration and industrial partnerships. Key areas of focus included:
*   Advancing the India-US Initiative on Critical and Emerging Technologies (iCET), which seeks to expand collaboration in areas like AI, quantum computing, semiconductors, and advanced telecommunications.
*   Strengthening defence industrial supply chain resilience, aiming to reduce dependencies and ensure robust access to critical components for both countries.
*   Identifying specific projects for co-development and co-production, reflecting a move beyond traditional buyer-seller relationships towards deeper integration.
*   Enhancing interoperability through expanded joint military exercises, increased information sharing, and collaboration in maritime domain awareness.
*   Continuing progress on existing major defence deals, such as the acquisition of GE F414 jet engines for India's indigenous Tejas aircraft and the procurement of MQ-9B Remotely Piloted Aircraft.

On the civil nuclear energy front, the dialogue highlighted renewed efforts to collaborate on energy security and climate change mitigation goals. Both sides expressed interest in exploring opportunities for private sector involvement in nuclear energy projects and stressed the importance of advancing nuclear safety, security, and non-proliferation efforts. Specific areas of discussion included:
*   The exploration of Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) as a viable option for meeting growing energy demands and achieving decarbonization targets.
*   India's participation as an observer in the Generation IV International Forum (GIF), signaling its commitment to advanced nuclear energy systems.
*   Sharing best practices and expertise in regulatory frameworks and waste management for civil nuclear power plants.

These high-level engagements signify a deepening of the bilateral relationship, extending beyond traditional security concerns to encompass critical technological and energy independence imperatives. The collaborative framework is designed to not only serve the strategic interests of India and the United States but also to contribute to regional stability and address global challenges collectively.

Going forward, both nations are expected to continue their structured dialogues and implement the agreed-upon frameworks. The focus will remain on translating policy discussions into concrete projects and partnerships that enhance capabilities, ensure technological leadership, and strengthen the resilience of critical infrastructure and supply chains in the evolving global landscape.
