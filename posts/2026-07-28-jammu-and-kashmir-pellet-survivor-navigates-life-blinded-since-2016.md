Shopian, Jammu and Kashmir – Insha Malik, a resident of Sedow village in Shopian, continues to navigate life with permanent blindness after sustaining severe injuries from pellet guns in 2016. Then 14 years old, Malik became one of the numerous victims of pellet injuries during widespread protests across the Kashmir Valley. Her story underscores the profound and lasting impact of such incidents on individuals and their families in the region.

The incident occurred on July 12, 2016, when Malik was near her home and was hit by pellets fired during civilian unrest. The pellets struck her face, primarily affecting her eyes and leading to irreversible damage. Despite undergoing multiple surgeries and receiving treatment at institutions like the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) in Delhi, her vision could not be restored. The medical prognosis confirmed complete blindness, fundamentally altering the trajectory of her life.

Malik's journey since 2016 has been marked by significant personal challenges and a determined pursuit of education. She recounts the emotional and physical pain of the incident, stating, "It hurts to remember." Her father, Bashir Ahmad Malik, a daily wager, faced considerable financial strain to fund her extensive medical treatments, often relying on community support and donations. This financial burden compounded the emotional distress for the family.

Despite the profound disability, Malik has exhibited considerable resilience. She resumed her studies through adaptive methods and is currently pursuing a Master of Arts degree in Urdu. This academic pursuit represents her commitment to rebuilding her life and achieving independence. Her daily routine involves overcoming obstacles related to her blindness, from navigating her surroundings to accessing educational materials.

*   **Incident Date:** July 12, 2016
*   **Age at Incident:** 14 years old
*   **Location:** Sedow village, Shopian, Jammu and Kashmir
*   **Injuries:** Severe pellet injuries to the face, resulting in permanent bilateral blindness.
*   **Medical Treatment:** Multiple surgeries, including at AIIMS, Delhi.
*   **Current Status:** Pursuing MA in Urdu.

The use of pellet guns by security forces for crowd control has been a contentious issue in Jammu and Kashmir. Reports from various human rights organizations and medical professionals have documented a pattern of severe injuries, including permanent blindness, among civilians caught in confrontations. Victims often face lengthy rehabilitation processes, significant medical expenses, and lifelong disability, impacting their ability to work and participate fully in society.

Malik's case is emblematic of the broader humanitarian concerns associated with the use of such weapons. Her aspirations extend beyond her current studies, as she hopes to secure a job that allows her to live independently and contribute to her family. The path forward for her, like many others with similar injuries in the region, remains challenging, requiring continued support and adaptation to a world without sight. Her story highlights the ongoing search for normalcy and opportunity amidst enduring physical and emotional scars.
