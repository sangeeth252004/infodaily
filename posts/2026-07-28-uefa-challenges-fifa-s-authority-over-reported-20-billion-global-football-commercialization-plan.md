UEFA has issued a robust challenge to FIFA's reported $20 billion plan to overhaul and commercialize global football tournaments, with UEFA President Aleksander Čeferin asserting that the rights to such competitions are not FIFA's to sell. The statement, made during a recent UEFA Congress, highlights significant tension between the sport's two most powerful governing bodies regarding the future financial structure of international football.

The proposed $20 billion package, reportedly spearheaded by FIFA President Gianni Infantino and backed by investors including SoftBank and unnamed Saudi Arabian entities, is understood to involve creating new global competitions or significantly expanding existing ones. The plan aims to generate substantial revenue streams, with the funding allocated to participating federations and for football development initiatives worldwide. Central to the proposal are concepts for a "New Global World Cup" and a "New Global Nations League."

UEFA's opposition centers on the fundamental question of ownership and commercialization rights for international football assets. Čeferin explicitly stated, "It is not FIFA's to sell. None of us are the owners of football. We are just custodians of the game." This position underscores UEFA's belief that FIFA does not hold unilateral authority to commercialize or restructure competitions without broad consensus from continental confederations and other key stakeholders. The European governing body indicated its refusal to participate in the proposed new ventures under the current terms.

Key details surrounding the dispute include:

*   **Financial Proposal:** A reported $20 billion fund, primarily from outside investors, intended to secure commercial rights for new or expanded global tournaments.
*   **Proposed Competitions:** The plan includes discussions for a "New Global World Cup" and a "New Global Nations League," signaling a significant restructuring of the international football calendar.
*   **Investor Involvement:** Media reports suggest SoftBank and Saudi Arabian interests are among the potential backers for the multi-billion dollar project.
*   **UEFA's Stance:** UEFA maintains that no single entity owns football's commercial rights and that any such substantial changes require collective agreement among all confederations and national associations.
*   **Governance Conflict:** The disagreement represents a continuation of power struggles between FIFA and UEFA over control and revenue distribution within global football.

The implications of this standoff could significantly impact the future landscape of international football tournaments, their commercial models, and the relationship between FIFA and its confederations. No immediate resolution has been announced, and the proposal remains subject to ongoing internal discussions within FIFA and consultations with various football stakeholders. The debate highlights the complex governance challenges facing football as it navigates increasing commercial interest and global expansion efforts.
