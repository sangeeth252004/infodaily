Argentine international midfielder Leandro Paredes is facing three assault charges stemming from alleged incidents that occurred following the 2022 FIFA World Cup final in Qatar. Concurrently, the world football governing body, FIFA, has initiated disciplinary proceedings against the Argentine Football Association (AFA) regarding the display of a "Las Malvinas Son Argentinas" banner during the team's post-tournament celebrations. Both developments highlight ongoing scrutiny of events surrounding Argentina's World Cup victory.

The charges against Paredes, who was a key member of Argentina's triumphant squad, relate to specific allegations of assault. Details concerning the exact nature of these incidents or the identities of the complainants have not been publicly disclosed, beyond their connection to the aftermath of the World Cup final. Legal proceedings are reportedly underway, which will determine the validity of the claims and any potential consequences for the player. At the time of the World Cup, Paredes was playing for Juventus on loan from Paris Saint-Germain, and has since moved to Galatasaray.

Separately, FIFA's disciplinary committee has opened a case against the AFA regarding a banner bearing the slogan "Las Malvinas Son Argentinas" that was displayed by players during celebrations after their victory over France. The banner refers to the Falkland Islands, which Argentina claims as its own territory. This issue remains a politically sensitive matter between Argentina and the United Kingdom.

FIFA regulations strictly prohibit the display of political messages or symbols during matches and official events. Article 16 of the FIFA Disciplinary Code outlines sanctions for "order and security at matches," which includes offences related to political or ideological messages.
*   **Case against AFA:** Allegations include violating Article 11 (Offensive behaviour and violation of the principles of fair play) and Article 16 (Order and security at matches) of the FIFA Disciplinary Code.
*   **Nature of dispute:** The Malvinas (Falkland Islands) have been under British control since 1833, but Argentina asserts sovereignty, leading to the Falklands War in 1982. FIFA's stance aims to keep political statements separate from sporting events.

The disciplinary action against the AFA could result in various sanctions, including fines, stadium closures, or other penalties as determined by FIFA's Disciplinary Committee. The AFA will be required to present its defence to the allegations.

Both the legal process involving Leandro Paredes and FIFA's investigation into the AFA are ongoing. Further updates are anticipated as the respective legal and disciplinary bodies continue their work to reach conclusions on these matters. The outcomes will underscore the responsibilities of players and national federations, even amidst moments of historic sporting achievement.
