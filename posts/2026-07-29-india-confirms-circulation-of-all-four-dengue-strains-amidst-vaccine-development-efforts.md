New Delhi, India – The Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) has confirmed the simultaneous circulation of all four serotypes of the dengue virus across India. This development coincides with the advanced stages of indigenous dengue vaccine development, with a potential rollout anticipated within the next year. Dr. Rajiv Bahl, Director General of the ICMR, affirmed the presence of DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, and DENV-4 strains, highlighting the complex public health challenge faced by the nation.

The concurrent presence of all four dengue serotypes is a critical concern for public health authorities. Infection with one serotype typically provides lifelong immunity against that specific strain, but only temporary and partial immunity against the other three. Subsequent infection with a different serotype can lead to a phenomenon known as Antibody-Dependent Enhancement (ADE), which increases the risk of developing severe forms of the disease, including Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (DHF) and Dengue Shock Syndrome (DSS). This makes a quadrivalent vaccine, capable of protecting against all four strains, essential for effective disease prevention.

Several pharmaceutical companies are actively involved in developing dengue vaccines for India. Panacea Biotec, an Indian firm, has completed its Phase-2 clinical trials for a dengue vaccine candidate. The company projects that its vaccine could be available for public use by 2025, pending regulatory approvals and successful completion of further trials if required.

Concurrently, the Serum Institute of India (SII) is also advancing its own dengue vaccine candidate. SII's vaccine is currently undergoing Phase-3 clinical trials, a crucial stage that assesses efficacy and safety in a larger population before seeking marketing authorization. The progress of these indigenous vaccines offers hope for a comprehensive preventive strategy against the mosquito-borne disease.

Globally, Takeda Pharmaceutical's Qdenga vaccine has already received approvals in several countries, including those in Europe and parts of Asia. However, Qdenga has not yet been approved for use in India. The availability of multiple vaccine candidates, both domestic and international, underscores the urgent global and national efforts to combat dengue, which continues to pose a significant health burden.

Key aspects of the current dengue situation and vaccine landscape include:
*   **All four serotypes confirmed:** DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, and DENV-4 are circulating nationally.
*   **Risk of severe disease:** Concurrent circulation increases the likelihood of secondary infections, raising the risk of severe dengue due to ADE.
*   **Indigenous vaccine progress:** Panacea Biotec aiming for 2025 rollout post Phase-2, SII in Phase-3 trials.
*   **Need for quadrivalent vaccine:** A vaccine covering all four strains is crucial for comprehensive protection.
*   **ICMR's role:** The ICMR continues its surveillance efforts, monitoring the prevalence and distribution of dengue strains across the country to inform public health strategies.

The anticipated rollout of a domestic dengue vaccine represents a significant step towards managing the disease in India. Public health advisories continue to emphasize mosquito control measures and early detection as primary methods of prevention while awaiting the widespread availability of a vaccine. Continued research, surveillance, and public health education remain vital in the ongoing efforts to mitigate the impact of dengue.
