Pakistani Minister Khawaja Asif recently made a statement justifying the use of force against protesters in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK), describing them as "enemies like India." The remarks were delivered following reports of the killing of over 30 individuals during protests in the region.

The minister's comments have drawn attention to the escalating situation in PoK, where demonstrations have reportedly been ongoing. Asif, a prominent figure in the Pakistani government, publicly addressed the casualties, framing the actions taken against the protesters within a national security context. His statement explicitly linked the protesters to external adversaries, specifically mentioning India, a move that analysts suggest could further inflame regional sensitivities.

The protests in PoK have reportedly centered on various grievances, including economic hardship, shortages of essential goods, and demands for basic rights. Local reports have indicated widespread public discontent, leading to confrontations between demonstrators and law enforcement agencies. The specific context surrounding the killing of the 30 individuals was not detailed in the minister's public remarks, beyond the general justification provided.

*   **Minister's Statement:** Khawaja Asif's direct quote, "'They're enemies like India'," served as the core of his justification for actions against the protesters.
*   **Casualty Figures:** Reports indicate over 30 protesters have been killed amidst the unrest in PoK, preceding the minister's public comments.
*   **Geographical Focus:** The events and the minister's statements pertain specifically to Pakistan-occupied Kashmir.
*   **Government Stance:** The remarks represent a senior Pakistani official's perspective on the handling of internal dissent within the administered territory.

Such statements from a high-ranking government official regarding the use of force and the characterization of protesters carry significant implications for regional stability and human rights discourse.
