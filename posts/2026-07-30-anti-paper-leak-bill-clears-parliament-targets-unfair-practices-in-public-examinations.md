The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Bill, 2024, commonly referred to as the Anti-paper leak bill, has successfully cleared both houses of the Indian Parliament. The Lok Sabha passed the legislation on February 6, 2024, followed by its clearance in the Rajya Sabha on February 9, 2024. Prime Minister Narendra Modi affirmed that the new law aims to "build a credible exam system," underscoring the government's commitment to ensuring integrity in the nation's competitive examinations.

This legislative development comes amid growing concerns over recurring instances of question paper leaks and other unfair practices that have undermined the sanctity of public examinations, impacting millions of students across the country. The bill seeks to deter individuals, groups, and organized entities involved in such malpractices, thereby protecting the interests of genuine candidates and restoring public faith in the examination process. It is designed to target perpetrators of organized crime in this sector, rather than penalizing individual examinees.

The new bill introduces stringent provisions and enhanced penalties for those found guilty of engaging in or abetting unfair means in public examinations. Key aspects of the legislation include:

*   **Penalties:** Individuals involved in organized examination crime face imprisonment ranging from three to ten years, along with fines of up to ₹1 crore. Other offenses under the bill carry a minimum imprisonment of three years, extending up to five years, and a fine of ₹10 lakh.
*   **Definition of Unfair Means:** The bill broadly defines "unfair means" to include leaking question papers or answer keys, accessing question papers or OMR sheets without authorization, tampering with answer sheets, facilitating fraudulent online examinations, and manipulating exam results or merit lists.
*   **Applicability:** The law covers a wide array of public examinations conducted by central government bodies. This includes examinations overseen by the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), the Staff Selection Commission (SSC), railway recruitment boards, banking recruitment examinations, and tests administered by the National Testing Agency (NTA).
*   **Investigation Powers:** Offences under this bill will be investigated by a designated central agency, such as the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI), or any other agency authorized by the central government. All offenses are classified as cognizable and non-bailable.

The government maintains that the bill's focus is on preventing large-scale organized cheating and paper leaks, which cause significant distress to students and disrupt examination schedules. The objective is to foster an environment of transparency, fairness, and equal opportunity for all aspirants. The Prime Minister's statement highlights the broader vision of creating a robust and trustworthy examination framework for the future.

Following its passage by both houses of Parliament, the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Bill, 2024, now awaits presidential assent to officially become law. Its implementation is expected to provide a strong legal framework to combat examination malpractices and bolster confidence in India's extensive public examination system.
