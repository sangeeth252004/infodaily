Prime Minister Narendra Modi recently convened a high-level meeting in New Delhi to review the safety of Indian seafarers and assess the implications of the ongoing Middle East conflict on India's critical supply chains. The meeting, held on May 12, 2024, focused specifically on the escalating threats to commercial shipping in the Red Sea region, primarily from Houthi attacks, and their broader economic impact on the nation.

The Prime Minister emphasized India's unwavering commitment to protecting its seafarers, who constitute a significant portion of the global maritime workforce. He directed senior officials to ensure maximum safety and well-being for Indian nationals employed on merchant vessels navigating through conflict-affected zones. The review underscored the strategic importance of maritime routes for India's trade and energy security, with disruptions in key passages like the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait posing substantial challenges.

The Middle East conflict, particularly the sustained drone and missile attacks by Yemen's Houthi group on commercial vessels in the Red Sea, has compelled numerous shipping companies to reroute their vessels around the Cape of Good Hope. This diversion significantly extends transit times and increases operational costs, impacting global trade flows. For India, a major importer of crude oil, fertilizers, and other essential commodities, and a significant exporter of manufactured goods, these disruptions have tangible economic consequences.

Key points reviewed during the meeting included:
*   **Seafarer Safety:** Discussions centered on intelligence sharing, emergency response protocols, and diplomatic efforts to secure the release and safe return of any Indian seafarers affected by incidents. The Indian Navy has maintained a proactive presence in the Arabian Sea and the Gulf of Aden, undertaking anti-piracy operations and providing assistance to distressed merchant vessels.
*   **Supply Chain Resilience:** Officials analyzed the vulnerability of various supply chains, focusing on potential bottlenecks, increased freight and insurance costs, and their potential inflationary impact on domestic markets. Strategies to mitigate these effects, including exploring alternative logistics solutions and enhancing strategic reserves, were likely part of the agenda.
*   **Economic Impact:** The extended shipping routes are leading to higher import bills for India due to increased fuel consumption and insurance premiums. Export competitiveness could also be affected as Indian goods face longer delivery times and higher transportation costs, potentially impacting global market share.

India has a substantial maritime workforce, with over 200,000 Indian seafarers employed globally, making their safety a paramount concern for the government. The Red Sea, a vital artery for international trade, is particularly crucial for India's commerce with Europe, the Mediterranean, and parts of Africa. Annually, a significant percentage of India's EXIM (Export-Import) trade, including approximately two-thirds of its crude oil imports, traverses these maritime lanes.

The meeting highlighted the need for a coordinated approach involving various ministries, including the Ministry of External Affairs, Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, Ministry of Defence, and Ministry of Commerce and Industry. This multi-ministerial strategy aims to safeguard India's national interests, protect its citizens abroad, and maintain the stability of its economy amidst geopolitical volatility.

Looking ahead, the Indian government is expected to continue closely monitoring the evolving situation in the Middle East and the Red Sea. Diplomatic engagements with international partners and maritime organizations will likely intensify to advocate for freedom of navigation and the safety of commercial shipping. Further policy adjustments and contingency planning will remain ongoing as India seeks to navigate these complex geopolitical and economic challenges.
