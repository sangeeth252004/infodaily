A cloudburst struck Honjar village in the Dacchan area of Jammu and Kashmir's Kishtwar district on Wednesday, July 24, 2024, resulting in damage to several structures and agricultural land. Concurrently, heavy rainfall across the region led to the temporary suspension of the annual Amarnath Yatra, impacting thousands of pilgrims on both the Baltal and Pahalgam routes. Authorities initiated immediate rescue operations and advised pilgrims to halt their progress until weather conditions improved and tracks were deemed safe.

The cloudburst, which occurred in the mountainous terrain of Kishtwar, caused significant disruption in Honjar village. Reports from the local administration confirmed that multiple residential houses sustained damage, alongside a vital bridge and swaths of agricultural land. While the material damage was substantial, officials confirmed there were no immediate reports of fatalities or injuries, attributing this to swift local response and possible prior warnings in vulnerable areas. Rescue teams from the State Disaster Response Force (SDRF), local police, and the Indian Army were deployed to assess the situation, clear debris, and provide assistance to affected residents.

Simultaneously, the Amarnath Yatra, a revered pilgrimage to the holy cave shrine in the Himalayas, faced a temporary halt. Heavy rainfall rendered the trekking routes, particularly the challenging stretches along the Baltal and Pahalgam axes, slippery and hazardous. Authorities made the decision to suspend the movement of pilgrims from both base camps to ensure their safety.

Key details regarding the Yatra's suspension include:
*   **Affected Routes:** The pilgrimage was halted on both the traditional 48-kilometer Pahalgam track and the shorter 14-kilometer Baltal route.
*   **Pilgrim Numbers:** Over 6,000 pilgrims were reportedly stationed at the Baltal base camp, with an additional 4,000 at the Nunwan base camp near Pahalgam when the suspension order was issued.
*   **Reason for Halt:** Continuous heavy rainfall throughout the day, which increased the risk of landslides and made the mountainous terrain precarious for trekking.
*   **Authority Action:** Shri Amarnathji Shrine Board (SASB) officials, in coordination with district administrations and security forces, issued advisories urging pilgrims to remain at designated camps and avoid proceeding towards the cave shrine until further notice.

District administrations in Ganderbal and Anantnag, overseeing the Baltal and Pahalgam routes respectively, monitored the weather situation closely. Officials communicated that the Yatra would resume only after weather conditions stabilize and the tracks are thoroughly inspected and cleared for safe passage. The priority remains the safety and well-being of the thousands of devotees undertaking the arduous journey. Efforts are underway to restore normalcy in the cloudburst-affected areas of Kishtwar and to facilitate the safe resumption of the Amarnath Yatra once conditions permit.
