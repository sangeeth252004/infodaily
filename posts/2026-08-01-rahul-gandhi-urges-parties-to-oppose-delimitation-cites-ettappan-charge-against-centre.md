Rahul Gandhi, a prominent leader of the Indian National Congress, recently issued a strong appeal to all political parties to unite and defeat the upcoming delimitation process in Parliament, labeling the Centre's approach as an "Ettappan" act. Speaking publicly, Gandhi alleged that the government's potential plans for redrawing electoral constituencies could lead to a significant disadvantage for southern states that have successfully controlled their population growth. His remarks underscore a growing concern among some political factions regarding the future balance of power in the Lok Sabha.

The term "Ettappan" carries historical significance in Tamil Nadu, referring to a chieftain from Ettayapuram who is widely perceived as a betrayer for allegedly aiding the British against the freedom fighter Veerapandiya Kattabomman. By invoking this term, Mr. Gandhi implied a perceived act of betrayal or malicious intent on the part of the central government, suggesting that the delimitation exercise could undermine the principles of fair representation and federalism.

Delimitation is the process of redrawing boundaries of Lok Sabha and state assembly constituencies to ensure that each constituency has a roughly equal population. In India, the last major delimitation exercise was conducted based on the 2001 Census, but a constitutional amendment in 2002 froze the adjustment of seats for Lok Sabha and state assemblies until the first census conducted after the year 2026. This freeze was implemented to encourage states, particularly those in the north, to prioritize population control measures without the immediate fear of losing political representation.

However, with the freeze set to expire, the prospect of a new delimitation based on the 2021 Census or a future census is raising significant concerns. States like Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Karnataka have achieved notable success in stabilizing their populations over recent decades. Conversely, several northern states have experienced higher population growth rates. If the parliamentary seats are reallocated strictly based on the latest population figures, southern states fear a reduction in their number of Lok Sabha seats, thereby diminishing their political voice in the national legislature.

*   **Key Concerns Highlighted by Gandhi:**
    *   **Reduced Representation:** Southern states, despite their economic contributions and population control efforts, could see their parliamentary seats decrease.
    *   **Federal Imbalance:** A shift in seat allocation could alter the federal power balance, potentially marginalizing regions with stable populations.
    *   **Disincentive for Population Control:** The proposed delimitation mechanism could inadvertently penalize states for effective governance in population management.

Mr. Gandhi emphasized that such a move would be fundamentally undemocratic and an attack on the foundational principles of India's diverse federal structure. He appealed to leaders across the political spectrum, regardless of their party affiliations, to recognize the gravity of the situation and unite to prevent what he described as an unjust reallocation of parliamentary power. The Congress leader's call is for a concerted effort within the Parliament to ensure that any future delimitation process respects existing constitutional provisions and addresses the concerns of all states equitably.

The debate surrounding delimitation is expected to intensify as the 2026 deadline approaches. Political parties are likely to engage in further discussions and strategizing regarding potential legislative amendments or alternative frameworks for seat allocation that can address population disparities without penalizing states for successful development and population control policies. The outcome of these deliberations will have long-term implications for India's parliamentary democracy and its federal character.
