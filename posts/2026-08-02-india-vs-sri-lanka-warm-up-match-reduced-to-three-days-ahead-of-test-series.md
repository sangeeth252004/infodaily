The warm-up fixture scheduled between the Indian and Sri Lankan cricket teams has been officially reduced from a four-day encounter to a three-day match. This adjustment to the preparatory schedule was confirmed by the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) and will directly precede the two-match Test series, which is a part of the ongoing ICC World Test Championship (WTC) 2021-23 cycle.

Originally planned as a four-day engagement, the practice match is now set to take place from February 25 to February 27 at the KSCA Cricket Ground in Alur, Bengaluru. The decision to shorten the match duration means both squads will have one less day of match practice to acclimatize, test strategies, and assess player form before the highly anticipated Test series commences.

The Test series holds significant importance for both nations, particularly concerning their standings in the ICC World Test Championship. Each match contributes valuable points towards qualifying for the WTC Final. India, currently placed in a competitive position, will be looking to secure maximum points from this home series, while Sri Lanka aims to improve its standing in the championship table.

The reduction in the warm-up period could influence team management's approach to player rotation and workload management. Warm-up matches typically serve multiple purposes:
*   Allowing players returning from injury or a break to regain match fitness.
*   Providing an opportunity for fringe players to stake a claim for a spot in the playing XI.
*   Offering the main squad a chance to practice in local conditions against competitive opposition.
*   Testing new combinations or strategies ahead of the main series.

With one day less, coaches and captains will need to adapt their preparation plans, potentially limiting opportunities for some players to get extended time in the middle or for the team to fine-tune specific aspects of their game. Both teams recently concluded T20 International series, meaning many players will transition directly from white-ball cricket to the longer Test format, making effective warm-ups crucial.

The two-match Test series itinerary remains unchanged. The first Test is scheduled to be played at the PCA IS Bindra Stadium in Mohali from March 4 to March 8. Following this, the teams will travel to Bengaluru for the second and final Test, which will be a Day/Night fixture played with a pink ball at the M. Chinnaswamy Stadium from March 12 to March 16. This second Test will mark a significant occasion as it will be India's fourth Day/Night Test match at home.

The decision regarding the warm-up duration ensures that both teams will proceed directly into the Test series with their adjusted practice schedules, focusing on translating their preparations into competitive performances in the WTC matches.
