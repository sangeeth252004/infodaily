India's manufacturing sector experienced a significant slowdown in its growth rate during May, reaching its slowest pace in nearly five years, according to data released by HSBC and compiled by S&P Global. Despite this deceleration, the HSBC India Manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) remained firmly above the critical 50-point threshold, indicating continued expansion within the sector.

The PMI, a composite single-figure indicator of manufacturing performance, registered a notable decrease compared to previous months. A reading above 50 signifies expansion, while a reading below 50 indicates contraction. The latest figures show that while manufacturing output continues to grow, the speed of this expansion has moderated significantly from earlier peaks. This suggests a tempering of the robust growth observed in recent periods.

The moderation in the headline PMI figure was primarily driven by slower increases across several key components of the index. Businesses reported a less pronounced rise in new orders, which in turn led to a softer expansion in production volumes. Firms also noted a reduced pace of input buying and inventory accumulation compared to the preceding months.

Key findings from the May report include:

*   **New Orders:** Growth in new orders, both domestic and export-oriented, slowed. While new business inflows continued to rise, the rate of increase was the weakest since mid-2020.
*   **Production:** Consequently, manufacturing output expanded at a softer pace, reflecting the cooling demand environment. This was the slowest rise in production recorded since early 2020.
*   **Employment:** Despite the overall slowdown in growth, the employment sub-index indicated marginal job creation within the manufacturing sector, continuing a trend of modest increases.
*   **Input Costs:** Inflationary pressures on input costs eased slightly but remained elevated. Manufacturers reported higher prices for raw materials and other inputs, leading to a continued increase in their cost burdens.
*   **Output Prices:** Firms adjusted their selling prices upwards in response to higher input costs, though the rate of increase in output prices also moderated.
*   **Business Confidence:** Manufacturers maintained an optimistic outlook for future production, albeit with some reduction in overall business confidence compared to the previous assessment period.

The slowdown in India's manufacturing growth rate, while significant, should be viewed within the context of sustained expansion. The sector has maintained a growth trajectory for an extended period, and the current moderation reflects a rebalancing rather than a contraction. Analysts will closely monitor future PMI reports and other economic indicators to assess the trajectory of India's industrial output and its broader economic implications in the coming months. The Reserve Bank of India, among other policy makers, typically considers such data in its economic assessments and monetary policy decisions.
