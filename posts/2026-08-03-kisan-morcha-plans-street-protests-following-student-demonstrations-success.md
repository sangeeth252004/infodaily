NEW DELHI – The Kisan Morcha, a prominent collective of farmers' organizations, has announced its intention to organize street protests, reportedly drawing inspiration from the recent success of student demonstrations in the country. This development signals a potential renewal of large-scale farmer agitation, reminiscent of previous movements that have significantly impacted national discourse and policy.

The decision to escalate farmer grievances to public protests comes as student-led movements have recently garnered attention and achieved specific outcomes. While specific details regarding the student protests that served as inspiration were not immediately specified by the Kisan Morcha, the announcement underscores a perceived effectiveness of direct public action in compelling official responses. Leaders of the Kisan Morcha have indicated that the strategy aims to exert similar pressure to address long-standing demands pertaining to agricultural policies and farmer welfare.

Farmers' organizations in India have a history of mobilizing significant public demonstrations to highlight their concerns. Key issues often raised by such groups include:

*   **Minimum Support Price (MSP):** Demands for a legally guaranteed MSP for all crops, calculated based on the C2+50% formula (cost of production plus 50%).
*   **Loan Waivers:** Calls for comprehensive debt relief for farmers, addressing the pervasive issue of rural indebtedness.
*   **Crop Insurance:** Improvements in existing crop insurance schemes to ensure timely and adequate compensation for losses due to natural calamities.
*   **Land Rights:** Protection of farmers' land rights and opposition to policies perceived as detrimental to agricultural land ownership.

While the exact dates, locations, and specific demands for the upcoming protests are still under planning, the announcement suggests a strategic shift towards renewed activism. The Kisan Morcha's statement indicates a period of internal consultation and mobilization is underway to consolidate support and outline a detailed plan of action.

The previous widespread farmer protests, particularly those observed between 2020 and 2021, demonstrated the capacity of these movements to sustain long-term demonstrations and influence legislative processes. The government's response to the upcoming planned protests will be closely watched, as it navigates the balance between addressing farmer grievances and maintaining public order. Further announcements from the Kisan Morcha are anticipated to provide more concrete details regarding the scale and scope of these planned demonstrations in the coming weeks.
