NEW DELHI – The Supreme Court of India has significantly expanded the ambit of Section 498A of the Indian Penal Code (IPC), a provision addressing cruelty by a husband or his relatives, to now encompass women in live-in relationships. This landmark ruling allows female live-in partners to file complaints of cruelty against their male partners and their relatives, a protection previously limited to legally married couples.

The judgment, delivered by a bench of Justices B R Gavai and Sandeep Mehta, clarified that the term "husband" under Section 498A can, in certain circumstances, include a male live-in partner. This decision came while dismissing an appeal filed by a man challenging a lower court's decision, underscoring the judiciary's evolving interpretation of family law in response to changing societal norms. The Court highlighted that the social stigma associated with live-in relationships has diminished, necessitating an updated legal framework for protection.

This expansion aligns Section 498A with the spirit of the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA). The PWDVA already recognizes and provides statutory protection to women in "domestic relationships," which includes relationships "in the nature of marriage," thereby covering live-in arrangements. The Supreme Court emphasized that since a live-in relationship is statutorily recognized under the PWDVA, a "purposive interpretation" of Section 498A is required to ensure consistent legal protection across related statutes.

Key aspects of the ruling and its implications include:

*   **Expanded Definition of "Husband":** The judgment clarifies that the term "husband" in Section 498A can be interpreted to include a male live-in partner, particularly where the relationship is "in the nature of marriage."
*   **Protection Against Relatives:** Crucially, the ruling extends the ability to file complaints not only against the live-in partner but also against "his relatives," mirroring the provisions available to married women under Section 498A.
*   **Alignment with PWDVA:** The decision aims to harmonize the application of Section 498A with the broader protections offered by the Domestic Violence Act, ensuring women in non-traditional relationships receive comparable legal safeguards against cruelty.
*   **Judicial Recognition of Societal Change:** The Court acknowledged the dynamic nature of societal relationships, noting that the legislative intent behind Section 498A was to combat cruelty against women, a concern not exclusive to marital bonds.

This judgment marks a significant step towards reinforcing the legal safety net for women in diverse relationship structures. It underscores the judiciary's proactive approach in adapting laws to contemporary social realities, ensuring that legal provisions intended to protect vulnerable individuals are applied effectively across various forms of domestic partnerships. The ruling is expected to influence how cases involving cruelty in live-in relationships are prosecuted and adjudicated across India, offering more robust legal recourse for women experiencing abuse outside of conventional marriages.
