Guatemala’s Fuego volcano, located approximately 35 kilometers southwest of Guatemala City, commenced an eruptive phase in late June 2024, prompting the National Disaster Reduction Coordinator (CONRED) to issue a preventative "yellow alert." The eruption has generated ash plumes reaching significant altitudes and incandescent lava flows down the volcano's flanks, leading to ashfall in several nearby communities.

Activity intensified, with authorities reporting the ejection of ash and gases. Plumes of volcanic material were observed rising up to 5,000 meters above sea level. This particulate matter subsequently drifted, causing ashfall in residential areas situated near the volcano, including Santa Sofia, El Porvenir, and La Reunion. While no immediate evacuations were ordered, CONRED has advised residents to remain vigilant and follow official guidance.

The preventative yellow alert signifies a state of monitoring and preparedness across municipal, departmental, and national emergency response units. This measure allows for the pre-positioning of resources and the activation of communication channels to disseminate critical information. Authorities are closely monitoring the intensity and trajectory of the ashfall, as well as the progression of lava flows, which have extended several hundred meters down the volcano's ravines.

The Fuego volcano is recognized as one of Central America's most active stratovolcanoes, making regular eruptions a characteristic of its geological profile. Its proximity to densely populated areas necessitates continuous surveillance. A previous major eruption in June 2018 resulted in over 200 fatalities and widespread destruction, underscoring the potential hazards associated with its activity and the importance of timely public safety measures.

Current risks include the potential for increased ashfall affecting air quality and visibility, as well as the formation of lahars (volcanic mudslides). Lahars can be particularly dangerous, especially during periods of heavy rainfall, as they can travel rapidly down river valleys, posing a threat to communities far from the volcano itself. La Aurora International Airport in Guatemala City is under observation, though its operations have not been impacted as of the initial reporting.

CONRED continues to assess the situation, providing updates to the public and coordinating with local authorities. Residents in affected areas have been advised to wear protective masks to mitigate inhalation of volcanic ash, cover water sources, and stay informed through official channels. The monitoring will continue as the eruptive phase evolves, with authorities prepared to escalate response measures if the situation intensifies or poses a greater risk to human settlements.
