Chandigarh, India – The Punjab Legislative Assembly has recently passed a unanimous resolution opposing the central government's E20 ethanol blending policy. The resolution, adopted in Chandigarh, directly challenged the accelerated implementation timeline for the policy and included allegations that the Centre was acting under "US pressure" in its push for E20.

The E20 policy mandates the blending of 20% ethanol with petrol, a core component of India's national strategy to reduce crude oil imports, enhance energy independence, and lower carbon emissions. However, lawmakers in Punjab expressed significant reservations, primarily highlighting the potential for severe groundwater depletion within the state. Representatives argued that increasing ethanol production, particularly from water-intensive crops such as maize and paddy, would place unsustainable demands on Punjab's already strained water resources.

During the Assembly proceedings, legislators questioned the "hurry" behind the policy's implementation, noting the target date for E20 was advanced from 2030 to 2025. They contended that this rapid acceleration lacked adequate consultation with state governments and failed to consider regional agricultural and environmental specificities. The claim of "US pressure" was articulated as a factor influencing the policy's swift adoption, suggesting external rather than purely domestic drivers.

Further concerns voiced by the Punjab Assembly included the potential economic implications for farmers. While ethanol production could offer new market avenues for agricultural produce, the shift towards water-intensive feedstocks might also increase cultivation costs and financial risks for a sector crucial to Punjab's economy. Additionally, members raised worries about food security, asserting that diverting food grains for fuel production could potentially impact food availability and price stability within the state.

India's national ethanol blending program aims to achieve a substantial reduction in its crude oil import bill, which stood at approximately $157 billion in the fiscal year 2022-23. The government has encouraged the use of diverse feedstocks for ethanol production, including sugarcane, maize, and damaged food grains, as part of its broader biofuel initiatives. The E20 mandate represents a significant step towards these national energy objectives.

Key objections formally articulated by the Punjab Assembly include:
*   Allegations of "US pressure" influencing the policy's accelerated timeline.
*   Concerns over severe groundwater depletion due to increased demand for water-intensive crops.
*   Lack of comprehensive consultation with state governments regarding policy implementation.
*   Potential financial strain on farmers and implications for state-level food security.

The resolution serves as a formal and united representation of Punjab's stance to the central government. As of the time of reporting, the Centre is yet to issue an official response to the specific allegations and concerns raised by the Punjab Assembly. This development underscores ongoing federal-state dynamics in national policy implementation and indicates potential areas for further dialogue or contention regarding India's energy, environmental, and agricultural strategies.
