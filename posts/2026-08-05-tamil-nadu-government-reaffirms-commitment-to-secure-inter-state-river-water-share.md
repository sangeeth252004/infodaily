The Tamil Nadu government has recently reaffirmed its steadfast commitment to securing the state’s legitimate share of inter-state river waters. This pledge underscores the government’s priority in addressing critical water resource management challenges, particularly those impacting agriculture, drinking water supply, and the overall socio-economic development of the state. The announcement comes amidst ongoing efforts to resolve long-standing disputes with neighboring states over shared river systems.

Chief Minister M.K. Stalin, along with senior officials from the Water Resources Department, has consistently emphasized the necessity of ensuring equitable access to water, citing its indispensable role in sustaining livelihoods and fostering industrial growth. The state's position is rooted in legal precedents, including various tribunal awards and Supreme Court judgments that delineate water allocations among riparian states. Securing these entitlements is paramount for agricultural productivity in regions like the Cauvery Delta, which relies heavily on river flows for irrigation, and for meeting the potable water needs of urban and rural populations.

The primary inter-state river water disputes involving Tamil Nadu include:

*   **Cauvery River:** A protracted dispute with Karnataka regarding the sharing of Cauvery river waters. The Supreme Court, in its February 2018 judgment, largely upheld the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal (CWDT) final award, allocating 177.25 tmcft (thousand million cubic feet) of water to Tamil Nadu from Karnataka. Enforcement and seasonal shortfalls remain key areas of contention.
*   **Mullaperiyar Dam:** An ongoing issue with Kerala concerning the safety and operational control of the Mullaperiyar dam. The dam, located in Kerala but operated by Tamil Nadu, is crucial for irrigating five southern districts of Tamil Nadu. Legal battles have focused on increasing the water storage level to its stipulated capacity.
*   **Palar River:** Disputes with Andhra Pradesh over the construction of check dams and other structures on the Palar river, which Tamil Nadu argues impede its historic water flow and affect groundwater recharge in its northern districts.

The Tamil Nadu government has indicated its intention to pursue all available legal and administrative avenues to protect the state’s water interests. This includes continuous engagement with the central government, advocating for the formation of necessary regulatory bodies, and meticulously presenting its case before the Supreme Court and relevant water disputes tribunals. Officials state that the government’s approach will be based on scientific data, established legal frameworks, and principles of inter-state equity. The objective is to ensure that the state receives its designated share without undue interruption, thereby safeguarding agricultural output, which directly impacts the state’s economy and food security.

Looking ahead, the state government is expected to intensify its efforts in monitoring water flows, advocating for compliance with existing awards, and initiating further legal proceedings where necessary. The focus will remain on proactive measures to protect Tamil Nadu’s water rights and to ensure sustainable water management practices across the state, anticipating continued engagement with neighboring states and central authorities to resolve these complex and critical issues.
