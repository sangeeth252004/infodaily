India has officially assigned new names to 27 geographical locations within Arunachal Pradesh, a state it administers as its own but which China claims as 'Zangnan,' or South Tibet. This move by New Delhi directly counters Beijing's earlier attempts to rename places in the same disputed Himalayan region, asserting India's sovereignty over the territory.

The action by the Indian government comes amidst an ongoing diplomatic exchange concerning the sensitive border region. Arunachal Pradesh, situated in India's northeastern frontier, has been a long-standing point of contention between the two nuclear-armed nations. China routinely refers to the state as 'Zangnan' and has historically issued its own lists of standardized names for locations within it.

India’s Ministry of External Affairs has consistently rejected China's claims and renaming efforts, reiterating that Arunachal Pradesh is an integral and inalienable part of India. New Delhi views Beijing's renaming actions as an attempt to assert its territorial claims through administrative means. This latest initiative by India mirrors the strategies employed by China in what some analysts describe as a "map war" over the region.

China has previously released multiple lists of renamed places in Arunachal Pradesh. Its most recent list, issued in March 2024, included 30 additional places, marking the fourth such list from Beijing since 2017. These actions have consistently drawn strong condemnation from India, which maintains that "invented names" do not alter the reality of the territory.

The 27 locations renamed by India encompass various geographical and administrative features across Arunachal Pradesh. This comprehensive renaming exercise underscores India’s commitment to reinforcing its territorial claims through official documentation and mapping.

The sites designated with new official names by India include:
*   8 villages
*   4 towns
*   4 rivers
*   3 mountain peaks
*   3 administrative areas
*   2 lakes
*   2 passes
*   1 glacier

This strategic move by India serves to officially cement its administrative authority and challenge China's narrative regarding the disputed region. While both nations continue to engage in diplomatic dialogue regarding the border dispute, actions such as the renaming of geographical sites reflect the entrenched positions of both countries. The ongoing assertion of sovereignty through administrative and naming actions indicates that the resolution of the India-China border dispute remains a significant geopolitical challenge.
