MacKenzie Scott, philanthropist and author, has announced a $50 million donation to the Native Forward Scholars Fund. The gift, disclosed in early June 2024, designates $10 million for immediate scholarship distribution to Indigenous students and allocates the remaining $40 million to establish a permanent endowment, designed to provide sustained financial stability for the organization's long-term mission.

This substantial contribution marks one of the largest single donations received by Native Forward, an organization dedicated to empowering Native American, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian students through scholarship support and leadership development. The organization, founded in 1969, has grown to become the largest non-governmental scholarship provider to Native students in the United States, having awarded over $350 million and supported more than 20,000 students from over 500 tribal nations across all 50 states. The donation is expected to significantly enhance Native Forward's capacity to address the educational disparities faced by Indigenous communities.

The strategic decision by Native Forward to direct $40 million into a permanent endowment underscores a commitment to enduring support for future generations of Indigenous scholars. An endowment provides a perpetual source of income, with investment returns utilized to fund scholarships and operational costs, thereby reducing reliance on year-to-year fundraising. This long-term financial planning is crucial for an organization whose mission addresses systemic issues in educational access and equity. Native Forward emphasized that the endowment will be managed under Indigenous leadership, ensuring that resource allocation remains aligned with community priorities and self-determination principles.

Ms. Scott's philanthropic approach, often described as "Yielding to Each Other," involves significant unrestricted giving to organizations identified for their effective work and direct impact within their communities. This model grants recipient organizations the autonomy to allocate funds where they are most needed, reflecting trust in their leadership and mission. The gift to Native Forward aligns with this philosophy, empowering an Indigenous-led institution to strengthen its foundational support for education. The investment arrives as Indigenous populations continue to navigate unique challenges in accessing and completing higher education, with data indicating persistent gaps in graduation rates and academic resources compared to other demographic groups.

The $10 million allocated for immediate scholarships will be disbursed in upcoming academic cycles, directly aiding students with tuition, fees, books, and living expenses, thereby mitigating financial barriers to higher education. This direct infusion of funds is expected to benefit a significant number of students immediately. The establishment of the $40 million permanent endowment is projected to generate sustainable revenue streams, which will enable Native Forward to expand its scholarship programs, enhance student services, and invest in strategic initiatives for decades. This dual approach addresses both immediate needs and long-term sustainability, reinforcing Native Forward's role in cultivating Indigenous leaders and strengthening tribal nations through educational achievement.

With this substantial financial boost, Native Forward is positioned to broaden its reach and deepen its impact on Indigenous education. The organization will continue its work in identifying promising Native students and connecting them with the resources necessary to achieve academic and professional success, further solidifying its legacy as a cornerstone of Indigenous educational advancement.
