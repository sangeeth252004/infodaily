The Shiromani Akali Dal (SAD) recently announced its support for the Women's Reservation Bill, which has already received parliamentary assent, and parliamentary bills concerning the reorganisation and reservation in Jammu and Kashmir. This declaration from the Sikh-centric political party followed a meeting between SAD President Sukhbir Singh Badal and Prime Minister Narendra Modi, sparking significant political speculation within national political circles in New Delhi.

The meeting between the two leaders marked their first direct engagement since the SAD's withdrawal from the National Democratic Alliance (NDA) in September 2020 over contentious farm laws. This interaction immediately fueled discussions regarding a potential rapprochement between the former allies. Mr. Badal subsequently clarified that his meeting with Prime Minister Modi was primarily personal, aimed at expressing gratitude for the Prime Minister's concern regarding his critically ill mother-in-law, former Union Minister Harsimrat Kaur Badal. Despite this clarification, the party's subsequent public stance on key legislative matters held considerable political weight.

Regarding the Women's Reservation Bill, officially known as the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, the SAD reiterated its long-standing advocacy for women's empowerment. The party emphasized its consistent support for a 33% reservation for women in legislative bodies.

*   The Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam mandates 33% reservation for women in the Lok Sabha (lower house of Parliament) and state legislative assemblies.
*   Its implementation is contingent upon a future census and a subsequent delimitation exercise, which will redraw constituency boundaries.

On the legislative proposals concerning Jammu and Kashmir, SAD voiced its support for the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill, 2023, and the Jammu and Kashmir Reservation (Amendment) Bill, 2023, which were under parliamentary consideration at the time. While supporting these measures, the party also called for the swift restoration of full statehood to Jammu and Kashmir and the urgent holding of assembly elections in the region.

*   The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation (Amendment) Bill seeks to increase the number of assembly seats in the union territory and to provide for the nomination of Kashmiri migrants and displaced persons from Pakistan-occupied Kashmir to the legislative assembly.
*   The Jammu and Kashmir Reservation (Amendment) Bill aims to modify the nomenclature for "weak and underprivileged classes" to "other backward classes" in the region's reservation laws.

The public affirmation of support by the SAD for these significant pieces of legislation provides cross-party backing for key government initiatives. While the meeting between Mr. Badal and Prime Minister Modi was described as personal, the timing of SAD's legislative announcements closely following this interaction is a notable development in the Indian political landscape. It highlights a convergence of positions on national issues, even amidst a history of political divergence.

Political observers will continue to monitor the dynamics between the SAD and the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). With general elections approaching, any further alignments or shifts in political strategy will be closely watched to discern the long-term implications of these recent developments beyond support for specific parliamentary bills.
