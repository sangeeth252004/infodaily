The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) is reportedly planning to eliminate the 60-day grace period currently afforded to H-1B visa holders, a measure that allows these foreign skilled workers to remain in the country for up to 60 days after their employment ends to seek new opportunities. This potential policy shift, first reported in connection with revisions to nonimmigrant worker regulations, could compel H-1B visa holders to depart the United States immediately upon job termination, profoundly impacting thousands of professionals, particularly in the technology sector, and the companies employing them.

The existing 60-day grace period, implemented in 2017 under the Obama administration, was designed to provide H-1B workers with a window to find new sponsored employment without having to immediately leave the country, thus promoting "portability" of their visas. The reported proposal seeks to revoke this provision, requiring H-1B visa holders to exit the U.S. if their employment ceases, regardless of their progress in finding a new sponsor. American entrepreneur Doug Schwalbe, who operates a consulting firm, characterized the development by stating, "I'm shocked but honestly not surprised," reflecting a sentiment among some stakeholders regarding potential changes in U.S. immigration policy.

The reported rationale behind such a move often includes addressing concerns over the potential displacement of American workers and alleged wage depression in certain sectors. Critics of the current grace period argue it allows foreign workers to remain in the U.S. for an extended period without active employment, potentially competing for jobs. Conversely, proponents highlight that the grace period helps U.S. companies retain skilled talent by allowing workers a reasonable transition time, thereby reducing disruption to projects and maintaining continuity in specialized roles.

Key details of the reported proposal include:
*   **Current Regulation:** The 60-day grace period was established in 2017, allowing H-1B visa holders to remain in the U.S. for up to 60 consecutive days or until their I-94 authorized period of stay expires, whichever is shorter, after their employment ends.
*   **Proposed Change:** The plan would remove this grace period, mandating immediate departure from the U.S. upon cessation of employment for H-1B visa holders.
*   **Departmental Action:** The Department of Homeland Security is expected to publish a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) detailing these changes, initiating a formal public comment period.
*   **Impact on Workers:** H-1B visa holders facing job loss would have significantly less time to secure new employment or finalize arrangements to leave the country, potentially leading to increased uncertainty and expedited departures.
*   **Impact on Employers:** U.S. companies would face challenges in retaining skilled foreign talent during economic downturns or restructuring, as workers would need to leave the country promptly.

The anticipated publication of the Notice of Proposed Rulemaking by the DHS will initiate a period for public comments, during which individuals, organizations, and businesses can submit feedback on the proposed changes. The final rule will be determined after reviewing these comments and undergoing further departmental consideration. This development represents a potential significant alteration to U.S. immigration regulations concerning high-skilled nonimmigrant workers.
