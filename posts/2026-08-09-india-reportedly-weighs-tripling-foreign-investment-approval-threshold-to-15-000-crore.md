The Indian government is reportedly considering a significant increase in the threshold for foreign direct investment (FDI) proposals requiring explicit government approval. According to recent media reports from November 2023, the Centre is weighing a proposal to raise this limit from the current ₹5,000 crore to ₹15,000 crore. This potential 200% increase aims to streamline the investment process, reduce regulatory burdens, and enhance India's appeal as a destination for foreign capital.

Under the current FDI policy, investments exceeding ₹5,000 crore are channeled through the 'government route,' necessitating vetting and approval from relevant ministries and departments. Proposals below this amount typically fall under the 'automatic route,' allowing investors to proceed without prior government sanction, subject to specific sector-wise conditions. The proposed hike to ₹15,000 crore would reclassify a larger volume of substantial investments, shifting them from the more time-consuming government approval process to the automatic route.

The primary objective behind this deliberation is to improve the ease of doing business in India. By increasing the threshold, the government intends to minimize bureaucratic delays and expedite project implementation, thereby making the investment climate more attractive for large-scale foreign enterprises. This move aligns with broader government initiatives aimed at boosting economic growth and increasing India's share of global FDI inflows. It is anticipated that fewer proposals will require direct government scrutiny, allowing ministries to focus on strategically critical or sensitive investments.

Key details of the proposed change include:
*   **Current FDI Approval Threshold:** ₹5,000 crore
*   **Proposed FDI Approval Threshold:** ₹15,000 crore
*   **Percentage Increase:** 200%
*   **Reported Aim:** To streamline investment, reduce regulatory burden, and attract more foreign capital.
*   **Impact:** A greater number of large FDI proposals would potentially qualify for the automatic route.

Should this proposal gain Cabinet approval and be formally implemented, it could significantly alter the landscape for foreign investors in India. Businesses planning substantial investments might find the regulatory pathway less cumbersome, potentially accelerating their entry and expansion plans. This policy adjustment would signal the government's commitment to creating a more investor-friendly environment, particularly for those looking to deploy significant capital into the Indian economy across various sectors.

While discussions regarding this threshold adjustment are ongoing, any formal change would require official announcement and implementation by the government. The move is expected to be closely watched by international investors and business communities keen on understanding India's evolving stance on foreign investment facilitation.
