The Indian Navy and the United States Navy have commenced a five-day joint specialized diving and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) exercise in Kochi, India. The collaborative drills, which began on Tuesday, June 25, 2024, aim to significantly enhance interoperability and facilitate the sharing of best practices in complex underwater operations and ordnance neutralization between the two maritime forces.

This joint engagement underscores the growing strategic partnership between India and the United States in the maritime domain. Such exercises are crucial for building mutual trust, developing a common understanding of operational procedures, and fostering a unified approach to addressing evolving regional security challenges. Both navies regularly confront threats ranging from underwater mines to improvised explosive devices, making proficiency in EOD and salvage operations vital for safeguarding maritime routes, critical infrastructure, and personnel. The exercise provides a focused platform for personnel from both nations to exchange expertise on advanced techniques, equipment, and safety protocols essential for these high-risk operations.

Personnel from the Indian Navy's Diving School, based in Kochi, are actively participating alongside their counterparts from the US Navy's Mobile Diving Salvage Unit One (MDSU-1). The training modules are specifically designed to simulate a variety of real-world scenarios, testing the endurance, precision, and coordination of the participating teams. These encompass underwater search and recovery operations for lost equipment or vessels, focusing on systematic grid searches and advanced sonar deployment. Participants are also engaging in mine countermeasure (MCM) operations, which involve identifying, classifying, and neutralizing various types of naval mines, a critical skill for keeping vital shipping lanes open. Deep-sea diving techniques, including saturation diving principles and emergency decompression procedures, are being practiced to prepare divers for extreme environments. Furthermore, the Explosive Ordnance Disposal component focuses on the safe handling, disarming, and disposal of unexploded ordnance (UXO) found both underwater and in coastal areas, leveraging both manual techniques and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs). These exercises are underpinned by rigorous safety protocols and continuous threat assessment.

This joint engagement is part of a broader framework of defense cooperation between India and the United States, aimed at enhancing regional security and stability in the Indo-Pacific. The Indian Ocean Region, a vital global trade route, faces evolving security challenges, including the proliferation of naval mines, piracy, and the need for robust underwater salvage capabilities following maritime incidents. Collaborative exercises like this strengthen the collective ability of partner nations to respond effectively to these threats, ensuring freedom of navigation and maritime commerce. For India, engaging with advanced naval forces like the US Navy provides valuable exposure to cutting-edge technologies, operational methodologies, and logistical coordination strategies. Similarly, the US Navy benefits from understanding the unique operating environments and expertise of the Indian Navy, particularly within the complex maritime geography of the Indo-Pacific theatre. These sustained interactions contribute directly to a more resilient, secure, and stable maritime environment.

Upon completion of the five-day exercise, both navies are expected to have significantly enhanced their combined capabilities in specialized diving and EOD. This engagement is anticipated to pave the way for future collaborative initiatives, reinforcing the commitment of India and the United States to maintaining a free, open, and secure Indo-Pacific. The exercise represents another step in the deepening military-to-military cooperation aimed at addressing common maritime security interests and preparing for future challenges.
