The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) has initiated disciplinary proceedings against a number of its active cricketers for participating in an unsanctioned Twenty20 (T20) league in Zambia. The players, who reportedly played in the 'Zambia Cricket League' (ZCL) without obtaining the mandatory No Objection Certificates (NOCs) from the PCB, now face potential bans of up to two years.

This development underscores the PCB's stringent policy on player participation in international cricketing events, which mandates official approval for all foreign engagements. The cricketers' involvement in the ZCL, which was not sanctioned by the International Cricket Council (ICC) or the PCB, constitutes a direct violation of these established regulations.

Key details regarding the incident include:
*   **Unsanctioned League:** The Zambia Cricket League (ZCL) operated without the necessary approvals from either the ICC or the PCB.
*   **NOC Requirement:** Under PCB regulations, all registered players are required to obtain a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the board before participating in any foreign league or competition. This process ensures player welfare, adherence to cricketing schedules, and compliance with anti-corruption protocols.
*   **Violation:** The cricketers' participation without prior PCB approval is considered a breach of the board's operational guidelines and potentially the PCB's Anti-Corruption Code, specifically clauses related to playing in unsanctioned events.
*   **Potential Sanction:** The penalty for such a violation can include a ban from all forms of cricket for up to two years, alongside financial penalties.

Among the cricketers reportedly involved are prominent names such as Sohail Khan, Imran Khan Senior, Asad Shafiq, Zohaib Khan, and Mohammad Irfan. These players have represented Pakistan at various international levels, and the potential bans could significantly impact their careers and the domestic cricketing landscape.

The PCB maintains a firm stance on upholding its regulatory framework to ensure discipline and professionalism among its players. The board's Anti-Corruption Unit (ACU) is reportedly involved in the initial inquiries to gather all relevant information pertaining to the players' involvement and the nature of the league.

Following these initial inquiries, the PCB is expected to issue show-cause notices to the cricketers involved, requiring them to explain their participation in the unsanctioned league. Depending on their responses and the findings of the investigation, formal charges may be laid, leading to disciplinary hearings. Any final decision on sanctions will be communicated after these processes are completed. The players will have the right to appeal any decision made by the PCB's disciplinary committee. This situation serves as a stark reminder to cricketers of the mandatory compliance with board regulations concerning their professional engagements outside national duty.
