New Delhi – The Indian government has announced a halt to the wider adoption of ethanol-blended diesel (EBD) after it failed to clear crucial safety tests. The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG) confirmed this week that initial trials revealed significant concerns regarding engine compatibility and long-term performance, effectively ruling out its immediate rollout as a standard fuel across the nation.

The decision marks a significant setback for India's ambitious push towards diversifying its energy basket and reducing reliance on crude oil imports. While ethanol blending in petrol has seen successful implementation, with E20 petrol now available nationwide, extending this initiative to diesel was seen as a logical next step. The move aimed to enhance fuel security, mitigate environmental impact through reduced emissions, and support the domestic agricultural sector through increased ethanol production.

According to preliminary reports submitted by leading testing agencies, including the Automotive Research Association of India (ARAI) and the Indian Institute of Petroleum (IIP), trials for EBD formulations – specifically an E5D blend (5% ethanol with diesel) – revealed several issues. Tests conducted over varying driving cycles and environmental conditions indicated potential adverse effects on existing diesel engine components.

Key findings from the trials include:
*   Accelerated wear on fuel injection systems and high-pressure pumps in tested engines.
*   Corrosion and degradation of certain fuel line materials and seals not currently designed for ethanol exposure.
*   Increased potential for water absorption within the fuel blend, leading to phase separation and microbial contamination in both storage tanks and vehicle fuel systems.
*   Concerns were also noted regarding cold flow properties and the potential for filter blocking in cooler climatic conditions.

These findings suggest that the current fleet of diesel vehicles in India, as well as existing fuel infrastructure, is not fully compatible with ethanol blends without substantial modifications. This presents a considerable challenge for automakers, who would need to redesign engine components, and for refiners and distributors, who would require significant investment in research and development to adapt vehicle technology and supply chains. For consumers, the ruling means continued reliance on conventional diesel, with the prospect of a more sustainable blend delayed.

Officials from the MoPNG stated that while the current EBD formulations have not met the required safety and performance standards, research into alternative blends and engine adaptations will continue. The government remains committed to exploring bio-fuel options as part of its long-term energy strategy, but any future rollout of ethanol-blended diesel will require rigorous re-testing and certification to ensure long-term vehicle integrity, operational efficiency, and consumer safety. A revised timeline for renewed trials or a refined strategy has not yet been announced.
