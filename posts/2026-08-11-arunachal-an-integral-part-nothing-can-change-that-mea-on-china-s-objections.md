NEW DELHI – India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) has firmly reiterated that Arunachal Pradesh is an integral and inalienable part of India, asserting that "nothing can change that." The statement came in response to persistent objections from China regarding India's sovereignty over the northeastern state, particularly following high-level visits by Indian officials.

MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal stated that India's position on Arunachal Pradesh has been clear and consistent, emphasizing the state's status as an undisputed territory of India. This affirmation underscores India's resolute stance against China's historical and ongoing claims over the region, which Beijing refers to as 'Zangnan' or 'South Tibet.'

The latest reaffirmation from New Delhi follows China's renewed objections to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit to Arunachal Pradesh in March 2024, where he inaugurated the Sela Tunnel project. China's foreign ministry spokesperson, Wang Wenbin, had termed the visit "illegal" and stated that India had "no right to develop" in the area. India has consistently rejected such objections, viewing them as attempts to unilaterally alter the status quo.

The dispute over Arunachal Pradesh forms a significant part of the broader India-China border issue, which spans 3,488 kilometers along the Line of Actual Control (LAC). Both nations have held multiple rounds of diplomatic and military talks to de-escalate tensions and resolve outstanding issues, particularly following clashes in the Galwan Valley in June 2020. However, fundamental disagreements over territorial claims, including Arunachal Pradesh, persist.

Key details regarding the India-China dispute over Arunachal Pradesh include:
*   **Historical Context:** China claims approximately 90,000 square kilometers of territory in Arunachal Pradesh, stating it is part of southern Tibet. India rejects this, asserting that the state is an integral part of its territory, defined by the McMahon Line, a boundary drawn during the British era.
*   **High-Level Visits:** China regularly objects to visits by Indian political leaders, including presidents, prime ministers, and ministers, to Arunachal Pradesh, considering such visits a violation of its territorial integrity. India maintains that its leaders have the right to visit any part of its sovereign territory.
*   **Renaming of Places:** China has, on multiple occasions, unilaterally renamed places within Arunachal Pradesh. India has strongly condemned these actions, labeling them as absurd and an attempt to legitimize unfounded territorial claims.
*   **Infrastructure Development:** India has significantly increased its infrastructure development projects in Arunachal Pradesh, including roads, bridges, and tunnels like the Sela Tunnel, to enhance connectivity and military preparedness in the border regions. China often views these developments as provocative.

India maintains that its development activities within its own territory are legitimate and aimed at improving the lives of its citizens. The MEA's consistent messaging signals no shift in India's sovereign claims over Arunachal Pradesh. The ongoing diplomatic exchanges highlight the enduring complexity of the India-China relationship and the unresolved nature of their boundary dispute. No immediate resolution to the territorial claims is anticipated, with both nations maintaining their respective positions firmly on the matter.
