Typhoon Dolphin made landfall in eastern China, prompting widespread emergency responses and significant disruption across several provinces. The severe weather event has led to the cancellation of hundreds of flights, the evacuation of hundreds of thousands of residents from vulnerable coastal areas, and extensive flooding. Authorities have issued high-level warnings as the typhoon continues its trajectory through densely populated regions.

The typhoon's arrival brought powerful winds exceeding 120 kilometers per hour and torrential rainfall to key economic zones and coastal cities. Government agencies initiated pre-emptive measures, including the closure of ports, suspension of public transportation services, and deployment of emergency personnel. The primary aim has been to mitigate risks to human life and critical infrastructure as the storm system advanced.

The impact has been particularly acute on transportation networks and residential areas. Key details include:
*   **Flight Cancellations:** Over 500 flights were cancelled at major airports, including those in Shanghai and Hangzhou, affecting tens of thousands of passengers. Airlines implemented flexible rebooking policies as departure boards displayed numerous delays and cancellations.
*   **Mass Evacuations:** Provincial disaster management departments oversaw the evacuation of an estimated 300,000 residents from low-lying coastal districts and areas prone to landslides. Temporary shelters have been activated to accommodate those displaced.
*   **Widespread Flooding:** Heavy rainfall has caused rivers to swell and urban drainage systems to be overwhelmed, leading to significant flooding in cities across Zhejiang, Jiangsu, and Fujian provinces. Agricultural land has also been submerged.
*   **Infrastructure Damage:** Preliminary reports indicate localized power outages and minor damage to communication infrastructure in some affected areas. Coastal defenses, including seawalls, have been tested by the storm surge.

Emergency response teams, comprising military personnel, police, and civilian volunteers, are actively engaged in rescue operations and damage assessment. Meteorological services continue to track Typhoon Dolphin's movement, advising residents in its path to remain vigilant and follow official guidance. As the typhoon is projected to weaken while moving further inland, authorities are already preparing for recovery and clean-up operations, with a focus on restoring essential services and assessing long-term economic impacts.
