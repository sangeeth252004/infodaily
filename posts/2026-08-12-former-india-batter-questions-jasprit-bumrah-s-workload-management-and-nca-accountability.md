Gautam Gambhir, a former India opening batter and World Cup winner, has publicly questioned the workload management decisions surrounding premier fast bowler Jasprit Bumrah and the accountability framework of the National Cricket Academy (NCA). These comments underscore ongoing discussions within Indian cricket circles regarding player fitness, rehabilitation protocols, and the demands of a rigorous international schedule.

Gambhir's primary concern centers on the decision to have Bumrah participate across all three formats – Test, One-Day International (ODI), and Twenty20 International (T20I) cricket – soon after his return from a prolonged injury layoff. Bumrah had been sidelined for over 10 months due with a stress fracture, an injury that kept him out of significant tournaments, including the Asia Cup and the T20 World Cup in 2022. Gambhir stated, "I don't know who made the decision, whether it was Bumrah himself, the captain, the coach, or the selectors, but it raises questions." He emphasized that a player of Bumrah's caliber, crucial to India's bowling attack, requires careful management to ensure career longevity and prevent recurrence of injuries.

The former cricketer also directed scrutiny towards the National Cricket Academy (NCA), also known as the Centre of Excellence (CoE), in Bengaluru. Gambhir questioned the academy's role and accountability, particularly when players frequently sustain injuries or experience setbacks in their rehabilitation. He asserted that if cricketers are continuously breaking down or returning prematurely from injuries, the NCA should be held responsible for the effectiveness of its programs and player recovery protocols. The NCA is tasked with player rehabilitation, fitness assessments, and preparing athletes for national duty.

Bumrah's return to competitive cricket has seen him regain form, notably leading the pace attack in recent international series. However, his injury history, including the stress fracture and previous back issues, has brought player workload and the efficacy of rehabilitation processes to the forefront of cricketing discourse in India. The demands of modern international cricket, with frequent tours and multi-format commitments, place significant strain on fast bowlers in particular.

These statements highlight a prevailing concern among former players and analysts regarding safeguarding key talent through judicious workload management and robust rehabilitation mechanisms, potentially prompting further internal discussions within the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) and the NCA regarding their operational methodologies.
