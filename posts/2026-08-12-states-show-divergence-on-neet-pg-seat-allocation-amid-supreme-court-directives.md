State governments across India are demonstrating divergent approaches to seat allocation norms for postgraduate medical admissions through NEET-PG, with specific variations concerning "in-service" doctor reservations and domicile criteria. This non-uniformity persists despite ongoing observations and directives from the Supreme Court of India, which has consistently advocated for a more streamlined and merit-based national admission process, particularly for the All India Quota (AIQ). The differing rules are leading to inconsistencies in the current admission cycle for Doctor of Medicine (MD) and Master of Surgery (MS) courses.

The Supreme Court has, on multiple occasions, emphasized the importance of national uniformity in postgraduate medical admissions, especially for the 50% AIQ seats centrally managed by the Medical Counselling Committee (MCC) under the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS). The apex court has notably ruled against the creation of separate quotas for "in-service" doctors within the AIQ, asserting that rules for these seats must be uniform nationwide, free from state-specific reservations or domicile requirements. This stance aims to ensure equal opportunity for candidates across the country to access seats based solely on national merit.

However, the remaining 50% of seats, comprising the State Quota, are managed by individual state authorities, where varied rules are applied. Several states continue to implement reservations for their "in-service" medical officers within this state quota. The percentages for these reservations and the specific criteria for defining "in-service" status can differ significantly from one state to another. This leads to a complex admission landscape where candidates face different eligibility and reservation conditions depending on the state they are applying to, even after national merit-based examinations like NEET-PG.

Key aspects of the divergence include:

*   **"In-service" Reservations:** While the Supreme Court has clarified that 'in-service' reservations cannot be applied to AIQ seats, many states maintain such provisions within their 50% state quota. The percentages allocated for these reservations vary, with some states offering significant proportions of their seats to in-service candidates.
*   **Domicile Requirements:** States also enforce different domicile criteria for their state quota seats, which can further complicate the application process for candidates who have studied or resided in multiple regions.
*   **Interpretation of Rules:** Discrepancies arise from varying interpretations of Supreme Court judgments and National Medical Commission (NMC) guidelines by state counselling authorities, affecting how seats are categorized, converted, or allocated.
*   **Impact on Seat Matrix:** The varied application of these norms can affect the overall availability and distribution of seats, potentially leading to an uneven playing field for aspiring postgraduate medical students across different states.

The ongoing counselling processes for the current academic year are thus navigating these state-specific nuances, which can result in legal challenges and procedural delays. While the Supreme Court's pronouncements aim to foster a cohesive national framework for medical education, the persistent variations at the state level underscore a continued tension between national standardization and regional policy autonomy. The situation highlights an ongoing need for clearer, universally enforceable guidelines or enhanced coordination between central and state authorities to ensure equitable and transparent admissions for future NEET-PG cycles.
