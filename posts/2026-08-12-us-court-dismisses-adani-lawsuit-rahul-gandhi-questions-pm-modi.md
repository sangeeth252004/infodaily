A United States District Court has dismissed a class-action lawsuit filed against the Adani Group, a major Indian conglomerate. The dismissal, announced recently, quickly drew sharp criticism from Indian opposition leader Rahul Gandhi, who publicly questioned Prime Minister Narendra Modi's alleged connections to the business group, stating, "Much more to this than meets the eye." The legal development concerns allegations of misrepresentation and stock manipulation that followed a critical report earlier this year.

The lawsuit, filed in the District Court for the Northern District of California, sought damages against the Adani Group following claims made in a report by Hindenburg Research. The short-seller's report, published in January 2023, accused the Adani Group of extensive stock manipulation and accounting fraud, allegations the conglomerate has consistently denied. According to court documents, the US court dismissed the class-action complaint primarily on procedural grounds, citing a lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. This signifies that the court determined it did not have the legal authority to hear the case, rather than making a ruling on the merits of the allegations themselves.

Following the announcement, Rahul Gandhi, a prominent leader of the Indian National Congress party, leveraged the development to intensify his long-standing critique of the Adani Group and its perceived proximity to the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and Prime Minister Modi. Speaking to the media, Mr. Gandhi questioned the implications of the dismissal, stating, "The Prime Minister should explain what is going on. This is not a simple case of a court dismissing a case; there is much more to this." He has previously called for a Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) investigation into the Adani Group's affairs, alleging undue favouritism.

The Adani Group, in an official company announcement, welcomed the court's decision, interpreting it as a vindication. The conglomerate stated that the dismissal further affirms its position that the allegations were baseless and politically motivated. They reiterated their commitment to transparency and adherence to all regulatory standards. The Adani Group's legal team emphasized that the court's finding of a lack of jurisdiction effectively shuts down the avenue for these specific claims to be heard in a US court.

Supporting details:
*   The class-action lawsuit was initiated by US-based investors who claimed to have suffered financial losses due to the alleged fraudulent activities outlined in the Hindenburg Research report.
*   Hindenburg Research's report in January 2023 triggered a significant sell-off in Adani Group's shares, wiping out billions of dollars in market value and prompting intense scrutiny from investors and regulators globally.
*   Rahul Gandhi has repeatedly raised questions about the Adani Group in the Indian Parliament, linking the business house's growth to alleged government patronage under Prime Minister Modi.

The dismissal of the US lawsuit marks a significant legal development for the Adani Group, potentially easing some of the international legal pressures it has faced since early 2023. However, the political fallout in India appears set to continue, with opposition parties like the Congress maintaining their scrutiny of the conglomerate and its relationship with the government. The Adani Group continues to navigate ongoing regulatory reviews and efforts to restore investor confidence in the wake of the Hindenburg allegations, while the political discourse around corporate governance and transparency in India remains a key focus.
