New Delhi – A Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports has highlighted a critical discrepancy between the introduction of new curriculum designs and the availability of updated textbooks for students in classes 9 through 12. The findings, detailed in a report recently tabled in Parliament, point to an "inexplicable" delay in providing essential learning materials consistent with the National Curriculum Framework (NCF) mandated under the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020.

The committee's report, titled "Review of Implementation of New Education Policy, 2020," specifically noted that while the new curriculum framework has been implemented for these senior secondary classes, the corresponding textbooks from the National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) are not yet adequately available. This situation leaves students reliant on older editions, supplementary reference guides, or publications from private publishers, potentially resulting in inconsistencies in their learning and preparation for examinations.

This shortage is particularly significant as classes 9-12 constitute a foundational stage for students' academic and career trajectories, directly influencing their performance in board examinations and future educational pathways. The absence of standardized, updated NCERT textbooks, traditionally regarded as authoritative and syllabus-aligned, poses a considerable disadvantage to students. It also complicates the efforts of educators to effectively teach in accordance with the revised curriculum guidelines, potentially undermining the broader objectives of the NEP 2020 to foster a comprehensive and contemporary learning environment.

Key observations and recommendations outlined by the parliamentary panel include:
*   **Committee Leadership:** The report was presented under the direction of MP Vivek Thakur, who chairs the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports.
*   **Identified Gap:** The panel observed a clear disparity where new curriculum designs have been put into practice, but the core learning resources—the textbooks—have not followed in a timely manner.
*   **Student Consequences:** Students are compelled to utilize outdated materials or unverified resources, risking incomplete or misaligned preparation for high-stakes academic assessments.
*   **Call for Urgency:** The committee strongly urged the Ministry of Education and NCERT to prioritize and expedite the development, printing, and distribution of the new textbooks for classes 9, 10, 11, and 12.
*   **Improved Coordination:** Emphasis was placed on enhancing coordination between curriculum developers and textbook publishers to ensure the prompt delivery of educational materials.

The committee further underscored the paramount importance of timely textbook availability as a cornerstone for the successful implementation of any educational reform. It advocated for a synchronized approach where curriculum updates are immediately supported by accessible and updated learning resources. Failure to do so, the panel cautioned, could lead to academic disruption and an unequal playing field for students across different regions.

Moving forward, the Ministry of Education and NCERT are expected to respond to these critical observations. The parliamentary panel's report serves as a formal recommendation for immediate action to rectify the identified gap, ensuring that students in classes 9-12 have access to the appropriate and current educational materials essential for success under the new National Education Policy framework. The swift resolution of this issue is crucial for maintaining academic standards and ensuring equitable educational opportunities nationwide.
