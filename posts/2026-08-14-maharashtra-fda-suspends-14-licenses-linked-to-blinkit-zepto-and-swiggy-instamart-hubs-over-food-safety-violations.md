The Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has suspended 14 food licenses pertaining to warehouses and distribution hubs operated by quick commerce platforms, including Blinkit (Grofers India Pvt Ltd), Zepto (KiranaKart Technologies Pvt Ltd), and Swiggy Instamart (Bundl Technologies Pvt Ltd). The suspensions followed an extensive inspection drive that revealed multiple violations of the Food Safety and Standards Act (FSSA), 2006.

The regulatory action was initiated after ongoing inspections conducted by the FDA over the past eighteen months identified persistent lapses in hygiene and food handling practices across various quick commerce facilities in Maharashtra. These violations included the presence of cockroaches and mold within premises, improper storage conditions for food products, and the alleged handling of expired food items.

Key details from the FDA's findings leading to the suspensions include:
*   **Unsanitary Conditions:** Inspectors documented instances of unhygienic premises, including the presence of cockroaches and mold in areas where food products were stored or handled.
*   **Expired Products:** Several facilities were found to be storing expired food products. FDA officials highlighted concerns regarding some expired items potentially being repackaged or relabeled, raising significant public health concerns.
*   **Improper Storage and Pest Control:** Violations included a lack of adequate pest control records, failure to maintain appropriate temperature records for perishable food items, and general lapses in maintaining a clean and safe environment for food storage.
*   **Cumulative Enforcement:** Over the duration of the FDA's inspection drive leading up to these suspensions, food products valued at approximately Rs 5.86 lakh were either seized or destroyed from various locations associated with the three quick commerce companies.

The suspensions underscore the increasing regulatory scrutiny on the rapidly expanding quick commerce sector, particularly regarding food safety and hygiene standards. The FDA’s actions aim to ensure compliance with the FSSA, 2006, which mandates strict guidelines for the storage, handling, and distribution of food products to safeguard consumer health.

For the affected quick commerce entities, the license suspensions necessitate immediate corrective measures. These companies are required to address all identified violations, rectify the unsanitary conditions, and implement robust food safety protocols. Following these rectifications, the facilities will be subject to re-inspection by the Maharashtra FDA. Licenses can only be reinstated upon verification that all compliance issues have been satisfactorily resolved.

No immediate official comment was available from the quick commerce firms regarding the license suspensions at the time of reporting. The situation highlights the ongoing challenge for fast-delivery services to balance rapid operational expansion with stringent regulatory adherence to public health standards.
