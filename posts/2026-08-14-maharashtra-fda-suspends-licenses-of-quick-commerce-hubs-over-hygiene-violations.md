The Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has suspended 14 licenses belonging to storage facilities linked to prominent quick-commerce platforms Blinkit (a subsidiary of Zomato), Zepto, and Instamart (operated by Swiggy). The regulatory action, taken in the week preceding August 21, 2024, followed inspections conducted between April and June 2024 across key cities including Mumbai, Pune, and Thane, revealing significant breaches of food safety and hygiene standards.

The suspensions underscore a heightened regulatory focus on the operational practices of the rapidly expanding quick-commerce sector, particularly concerning public health and consumer safety. FDA officials cited multiple violations of the Food Safety and Standards Act (FSSA), 2006, at these distribution and storage hubs, necessitating immediate corrective measures.

Key findings from the FDA inspections included:
*   **Unhygienic Conditions:** Inspectors documented the presence of cockroaches and rats within the storage facilities, indicating a lack of adequate pest control and overall sanitation.
*   **Expired Food Products:** Substantial quantities of expired food items were found stored alongside sellable goods, posing potential health risks to consumers.
*   **Improper Storage:** Several facilities failed to maintain prescribed temperature controls for perishable food products, compromising their quality and safety.
*   **Lack of Documentation:** Issues were noted regarding the absence of clear manufacturing and packaging dates on some products, as well as the lack of valid medical fitness certificates for employees handling food items.

The FDA seized expired products valued at approximately ₹12.4 lakh across the affected facilities. This included items worth ₹6.2 lakh from Blinkit-linked hubs, ₹1.7 lakh from Zepto facilities, and ₹4.5 lakh from Instamart operations.

Detailing the suspensions, the Maharashtra FDA's actions specifically impacted:
*   **Blinkit (Zomato):** Six licenses suspended across its storage facilities in Mumbai, Thane, and Pune.
*   **Instamart (Swiggy):** Six licenses suspended for its hubs located in Mumbai and Pune.
*   **Zepto:** Two licenses suspended, affecting its facilities in Mumbai and Pune.

Shailesh Adhao, Joint Commissioner (Food) at the Maharashtra FDA, emphasized the administration's commitment to consumer health and safety. Adhao stated that the FDA would continue to take stringent action against any entity found violating food safety norms, irrespective of their market position. The inspections are part of an ongoing statewide drive to ensure compliance with food safety regulations.

Representatives from Zomato, Swiggy, and Zepto did not provide immediate comments when contacted regarding the license suspensions.

The suspension of licenses mandates that these facilities cease operations until they address the identified deficiencies and secure re-approval from the FDA. This regulatory action highlights the critical importance for quick-commerce companies to uphold rigorous hygiene and food safety standards throughout their supply chains to ensure consumer trust and compliance with national food safety laws. The FDA's ongoing oversight suggests that similar inspections and actions may continue as the quick-commerce sector expands.
