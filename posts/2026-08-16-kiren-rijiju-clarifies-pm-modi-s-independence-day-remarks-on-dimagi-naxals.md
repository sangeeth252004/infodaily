Union Minister Kiren Rijiju has clarified comments made by Prime Minister Narendra Modi during his recent Independence Day address, specifically refuting claims that the Prime Minister labeled opposition leaders as "dimagi naxals" (intellectual Naxals). Mr. Rijiju stated that PM Modi's remarks were aimed at broader societal challenges and disruptive forces, not at specific political opponents.

The clarification by Mr. Rijiju, currently the Minister of Earth Sciences, comes amidst ongoing political debate and interpretations of the Prime Minister's August 15 speech from the Red Fort. During the address, Prime Minister Modi emphasized the need to combat "three evils" — corruption, nepotism, and appeasement — which he identified as detrimental to the nation's progress. While the Prime Minister did not explicitly use the term "dimagi naxals" in this particular speech, the phrase has been part of Indian political discourse previously, often associated with critics of the government or those perceived as promoting anti-establishment ideologies.

Mr. Rijiju, speaking to media outlets, asserted that "The Prime Minister never called opposition leaders 'dimagi naxals'." He stressed that the Prime Minister's intent was to highlight elements that impede national development and social harmony. This statement aims to de-escalate interpretations suggesting a direct attack on opposition parties by equating them with Maoist sympathizers, a contentious term in Indian politics. The term "urban naxals" gained prominence in the country's political lexicon around 2018, often used to describe individuals or groups perceived as intellectual sympathizers of Naxalite-Maoist insurgencies, operating within urban environments and sometimes accused of instigating unrest.

The Prime Minister's Independence Day speech on August 15, 2024, marked his eleventh address from the Red Fort. In his remarks, he urged citizens to strive for a developed India by 2047, focusing on collective responsibility and overcoming divisive forces. The address also touched upon India's growing global stature and economic resilience. The subsequent interpretations and Mr. Rijiju's clarification highlight the sensitive nature of political rhetoric and its reception in a diverse democratic landscape.

Key points from the context surrounding the clarification include:
*   **Prime Minister's Independence Day Speech:** Delivered on August 15, 2024, from the Red Fort, New Delhi.
*   **Core Message:** Focused on fighting corruption, nepotism, and appeasement for a developed India by 2047.
*   **Specific Terminology:** The term "dimagi naxals" was not explicitly used by PM Modi in this particular speech but emerged in public discourse as an interpretation of his broader criticism of certain anti-development ideologies.
*   **Kiren Rijiju's Role:** Union Minister of Earth Sciences, providing clarification on behalf of the government.
*   **Background of "Urban Naxal" Term:** Has been used in Indian political discourse to characterize individuals perceived as intellectual backers of Naxalite ideology.

The clarification by Mr. Rijiju serves to manage the narrative surrounding the Prime Minister's speech, particularly concerning its perceived implications for political opposition. It underscores the government's position that the Prime Minister's criticisms were directed at broader systemic issues rather than specific political figures or parties. The political landscape is expected to continue debating the nuances of such statements as parties prepare for upcoming elections and parliamentary sessions.
