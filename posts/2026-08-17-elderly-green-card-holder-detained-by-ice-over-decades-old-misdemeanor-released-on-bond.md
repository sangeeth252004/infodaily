Baljit Kaur, an 80-year-old Indian-origin woman and a U.S. Green Card holder, was detained by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) upon her arrival at San Antonio International Airport, Texas, on May 10, 2024. Mrs. Kaur, who has maintained her status as a lawful permanent resident in the United States for three decades, was returning from a visit to India when she was taken into custody. After an 18-day period of detention, she was subsequently released on bond, with her daughter, Monica Singh, an immigration attorney, actively representing her in the ongoing legal proceedings.

Mrs. Kaur's detention, despite her long-standing residency since 1994, reportedly stemmed from the discovery of a misdemeanor conviction dating back nearly two decades. During her processing at the airport, an immigration officer identified a 2006 conviction for attempted retail theft in Georgia. This past legal infraction, classified as a misdemeanor, was deemed by ICE officials to potentially render her inadmissible or deportable under current immigration statutes. This determination was made even though Mrs. Kaur had traveled internationally multiple times since the 2006 incident without encountering any prior issues regarding her immigration status.

Monica Singh, Mrs. Kaur's daughter and an immigration attorney, has publicly confirmed her mother's detention and is spearheading her legal defense. The family has asserted that Mrs. Kaur had no prior knowledge or indication that the nearly 18-year-old misdemeanor, described as a minor shoplifting offense, would imperil her lawful permanent resident status. This case underscores the intricate nature of U.S. immigration law, where even minor and aged criminal convictions can lead to significant and unforeseen consequences for Green Card holders, particularly when re-entering the country after international travel.

Supporting details from the case include:
*   Mrs. Kaur was held at the South Texas ICE Processing Center in Pearsall, Texas, for a period of 18 days, from her detention on May 10, 2024, until her release.
*   Her family voiced considerable concern regarding her health and well-being during her time in detention, highlighting her advanced age and pre-existing medical conditions, which include arthritis, high blood pressure, diabetes, and a history of a heart attack, necessitating consistent medical care.
*   An immigration judge ultimately ordered her release from custody upon the posting of a $10,000 bond.
*   Mrs. Kaur's legal battle continues, with her next court hearing scheduled for August 2025. Her daughter, Monica Singh, will continue to represent her interests in these proceedings aimed at clarifying and securing her immigration standing.
