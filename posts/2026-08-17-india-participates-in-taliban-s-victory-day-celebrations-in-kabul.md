India's diplomatic mission in Kabul participated in the "Victory Day" celebrations held by the Taliban administration on August 31, 2023. The event, which marked the second anniversary of the withdrawal of United States and NATO forces from Afghanistan, saw the Charge d'Affaires of the Indian Embassy in Kabul among the attendees. This participation signals a notable development in New Delhi's pragmatic engagement with the interim Taliban government.

The "Victory Day" commemorates August 31, 2021, when the last foreign troops departed Afghanistan, leading to the Taliban's re-establishment of control over the country. For the Taliban, the day signifies the end of a two-decade conflict and a victory over foreign intervention. India's presence at these celebrations has been interpreted by observers as a strategic move reflecting its evolving policy towards Afghanistan, despite New Delhi not having formally recognized the Taliban government.

India reopened its embassy in Kabul with a "technical mission" in June 2022, following a period of withdrawal after the Taliban takeover. Since then, India has maintained a limited diplomatic presence, focusing on humanitarian aid and monitoring the situation, particularly concerning regional security and cross-border terrorism. The recent participation at the 'Victory Day' event represents an incremental upgrade in India's level of engagement, extending beyond mere technical interaction.

Key aspects of India's engagement with Afghanistan include:
*   **Humanitarian Aid:** India has consistently provided humanitarian assistance to Afghanistan, including wheat, medicines, and vaccines, delivered through various channels.
*   **Infrastructure Projects:** India has ongoing investments in Afghanistan's infrastructure, such as the Salma Dam (Afghan-India Friendship Dam) and the Afghan Parliament building, which it continues to monitor.
*   **Security Concerns:** A primary driver for India's engagement is to monitor the security situation, particularly regarding the activities of Pakistan-based terror groups and their potential impact on regional stability and India's interests.
*   **Protecting Interests:** India seeks to safeguard its strategic and economic interests in Afghanistan and ensure the welfare of Afghan minorities, including Hindus and Sikhs.

Despite the recent diplomatic overture, India's official stance on formal recognition of the Taliban government remains contingent on several conditions. These include the formation of an inclusive government, respect for women's rights and education, and assurances that Afghan soil will not be used for terrorism against other countries. The engagement is largely seen as practical and needs-based, aiming to keep communication channels open and maintain a degree of influence in the country.

Looking ahead, India is expected to continue its pragmatic approach, balancing its stated conditions for recognition with the practical necessities of engaging with the de facto authorities in Kabul. This strategy aims to protect India's security interests, facilitate humanitarian efforts, and preserve its long-term stake in Afghanistan's stability, while carefully navigating the complex geopolitical landscape of the region.
