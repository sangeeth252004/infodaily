Over 30 U.S. states, led by California and New York, filed a federal lawsuit against Meta Platforms Inc., the parent company of Instagram and Facebook, on Tuesday, October 24, 2023. The widespread legal action accuses Meta of knowingly designing Instagram features that exploit young users' psychology for prolonged engagement, leading to mental and physical harm in teenagers, and of systematically misleading the public about these risks.

The lawsuits, filed in federal court in Oakland, California, and various state courts, collectively represent a significant legal challenge to Meta's business practices concerning its younger demographic. Attorneys general from states including Colorado, Florida, Kentucky, Massachusetts, Michigan, and Washington are among those participating in the multi-state action. They assert that Meta's platforms, particularly Instagram, contribute to a youth mental health crisis.

The legal complaints detail a range of specific allegations:
*   **Addictive Design Features:** Meta is accused of intentionally engineering its platforms with features such as endless scrolling, pervasive notifications, and algorithmic recommendations that are designed to promote compulsive use among young people.
*   **Harmful Psychological Impact:** The lawsuits claim these design choices contribute to increased rates of anxiety, depression, eating disorders, body dysmorphia, and sleep deprivation among adolescent users.
*   **Concealment of Internal Research:** A central accusation is that Meta possessed internal research demonstrating these negative impacts on young users but chose to conceal this information from the public, parents, and regulators. This directly underpins the plaintiffs' assertion that "they knew" about the harms.
*   **Violation of Consumer Protection Laws:** The states allege Meta violated state consumer protection laws through its deceptive practices and its failure to adequately warn users and parents about the risks associated with its platforms.
*   **COPPA Violations:** Some complaints also allege that Meta violated the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by collecting data from users under the age of 13 without verifiable parental consent.

The attorneys general involved have emphasized the severity of the alleged public health crisis, stating that Meta prioritized profit over the well-being of its young users. The lawsuits seek comprehensive injunctive relief to compel Meta to cease its alleged harmful practices and implement design changes that protect young people. They also demand civil penalties and restitution for the damages caused by Meta's conduct.

In response to previous concerns and inquiries, Meta has stated that it has invested in tools and resources to support young people and their families online. The company highlights features such as parental supervision tools, age verification measures, and resources for mental health support. Meta has also stated its commitment to working with policymakers and experts to address youth well-being on its platforms.

The legal proceedings are anticipated to be lengthy and complex. The outcome of these lawsuits could have significant implications for the regulation of social media companies, potentially leading to new industry standards for platform design, age-appropriate content, and data privacy for young users across the United States.
