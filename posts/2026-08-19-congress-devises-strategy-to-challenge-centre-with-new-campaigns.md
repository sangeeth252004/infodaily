The Indian National Congress recently held a crucial strategy meeting in New Delhi to outline its approach for challenging the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)-led central government. Top party leaders, including Congress President Mallikarjun Kharge, former President Rahul Gandhi, General Secretary (Organisation) K.C. Venugopal, and General Secretary in-charge of Communications Jairam Ramesh, attended the discussions. The primary objective was to devise a multi-pronged strategy to corner the Centre on key national issues, including unemployment, alleged financial improprieties, and perceived threats to constitutional values.

The deliberations centered on the launch of several targeted campaigns designed to resonate with various segments of the electorate. Among the prominent initiatives discussed were "Chhatron ki Goonj," "Vande Mataram," and "Chanda Chori." These campaigns aim to highlight specific grievances and counter the government's narrative ahead of upcoming elections.

Key campaign details discussed include:

*   **"Chhatron ki Goonj" (Voice of Students):** This initiative focuses on the escalating issue of youth unemployment and other challenges faced by students across the country. The Congress party plans to mobilize students and youth groups, amplify their concerns regarding job creation, educational reforms, and competitive examination irregularities, aiming to position itself as a vocal advocate for their future.
*   **"Chanda Chori" (Theft of Donations):** This campaign is designed to target alleged financial irregularities and a perceived lack of transparency in political funding. Central to this will be scrutinizing the controversial electoral bonds scheme, which the Congress claims has fostered crony capitalism and provided an opaque channel for corporate donations to the ruling party. The party intends to frame this as an issue of public trust and accountability.
*   **"Vande Mataram":** The Congress aims to reclaim and redefine the narrative around nationalism. This campaign seeks to counter what the party views as the BJP's exclusive claim on patriotic sentiment, by promoting an inclusive form of nationalism rooted in constitutional principles and the diversity of India, rather than a narrow ideological interpretation.

Beyond these specific campaigns, the meeting addressed a broader spectrum of national issues. Discussions touched upon concerns regarding the reliability of Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs), challenges to federalism, and the alleged misuse of central investigative agencies against political opponents. Other critical matters, such as the situation in Manipur, border disputes in Ladakh, and persistent inflation, were also deliberated as potential avenues for challenging the government.

The party's overarching strategy involves a comprehensive outreach program targeting specific demographic groups. Plans were discussed to engage effectively with students, farmers, women, Other Backward Classes (OBCs), Dalits, and tribal communities to build a broad-based opposition. The leadership emphasized the need for a unified approach to communicate its vision for governance and highlight the perceived shortcomings of the current administration.

While specific timelines for the launch and implementation of these campaigns were not detailed in the available information, the consensus from the meeting signals an intensified push by the Congress party to articulate its opposition stance. Further announcements regarding the rollout of these initiatives and subsequent phases of their strategy are anticipated as the party prepares for upcoming political contests.
