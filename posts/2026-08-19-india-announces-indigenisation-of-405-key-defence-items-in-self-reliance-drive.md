The Indian Ministry of Defence (MoD) has announced a significant push towards domestic manufacturing, identifying 405 key defence items for indigenisation. This initiative aims to reduce reliance on foreign imports across various critical military platforms, including the Advanced Light Helicopter (ALH), Tejas Light Combat Aircraft, main battle tanks, and naval warships. The announcement underscores India's ongoing commitment to the 'Aatmanirbhar Bharat' (Self-Reliant India) initiative, specifically strengthening the defence industrial base.

This latest list, designated for Defence Public Sector Undertakings (DPSUs), represents an addition to previous Positive Indigenisation Lists (PILs) for both the armed forces and DPSUs. The primary objective is to progressively substitute imported components, sub-systems, and aggregates with domestically manufactured alternatives. This strategic move is anticipated to bolster India's self-reliance in defence production, foster indigenous research and development, and generate economic opportunities within the country.

The newly identified items span a wide array of categories, crucial for the operational readiness and technological advancement of the Indian armed forces. These items are currently imported and their local production will involve DPSUs, private sector industries, including Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs), and startups. The indigenisation process for these 405 items is set to be achieved within specific timelines, aiming to ensure a seamless transition and continuous supply chain.

Key aspects of this indigenisation drive include:
*   **Expanded Scope:** The 405 items cover a diverse range of high-value components and sub-systems essential for various military platforms.
*   **Strategic Platforms:** Indigenisation efforts are specifically targeting critical components for frontline assets such as:
    *   Advanced Light Helicopters (ALH), enhancing their operational sustainability.
    *   Tejas Light Combat Aircraft, supporting its ongoing upgrades and future variants.
    *   Main Battle Tanks, focusing on power packs, armament systems, and other vital parts.
    *   Naval Warships, addressing propulsion systems, sensors, and weapon components.
*   **Economic Impact:** The initiative is expected to stimulate domestic defence manufacturing, promote job creation, and attract investments in advanced technologies.
*   **Technological Advancement:** By necessitating indigenous design and development, the move aims to foster innovation and build a robust defence technology ecosystem in India.

The Ministry of Defence has been actively pursuing indigenisation through various measures, including the issuance of multiple Positive Indigenisation Lists that restrict the import of specific items beyond a certain timeframe. These lists serve as a directive to the defence industry to develop and produce these items domestically. The current announcement builds upon these efforts, reinforcing the government's long-term vision for India to emerge as a global defence manufacturing hub.

Looking ahead, the MoD is expected to continuously monitor the progress of indigenisation for these items, providing necessary support and policy interventions to facilitate domestic production. This systematic approach is designed to ensure that India's defence capabilities are increasingly rooted in indigenous strengths, thereby enhancing national security and strategic autonomy in the coming years.
