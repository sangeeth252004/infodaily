The United Arab Emirates (UAE) experienced a series of missile and drone attacks in early 2022, primarily targeting its capital, Abu Dhabi. These incidents, attributed to Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen, prompted immediate defensive responses from the UAE and a significant review of its regional security postures, including the termination of a maritime security cooperation agreement with Iran. The attacks underscored escalating regional tensions and the complex dynamics of the ongoing conflict in Yemen.

The series of attacks commenced in January 2022, marking a direct escalation of hostilities targeting the UAE mainland. Multiple ballistic missiles and drones were intercepted over Abu Dhabi, with some debris falling without causing significant casualties or damage. The incidents involved advanced weaponry, prompting a robust defensive posture from the UAE military, which utilized its air defense systems to neutralize incoming threats.

Key details of the attacks and the UAE's response include:
*   **January 17, 2022:** A Houthi-claimed attack targeted an industrial area in Musaffah, Abu Dhabi, resulting in three fatalities and six injuries. The incident involved drone and missile strikes, with the UAE Ministry of Defence reporting the destruction of the attacking platforms.
*   **January 24, 2022:** Two ballistic missiles were launched towards Abu Dhabi and intercepted by UAE air defenses. The attack coincided with Israeli President Isaac Herzog's visit to the UAE, highlighting the strategic timing of the aggressions.
*   **February 7, 2022:** A further missile attack was launched by Houthis, with UAE defenses intercepting and destroying the incoming ballistic missiles. This event marked the third such attack on the UAE within weeks.

In response to these security challenges, the UAE undertook several actions. Diplomatically, the nation engaged with international partners to condemn the attacks and garner support for regional stability. Security cooperation with allies, including the United States, was reinforced. Economically and strategically, a significant step was taken with the cancellation of a 2017 maritime security cooperation agreement with Iran. This pact had aimed to foster cooperation on maritime safety and counter-piracy efforts. The decision to terminate the agreement reflected a reassessment of security priorities in light of the ongoing threats and perceived regional destabilization efforts.

The termination of the maritime pact with Iran signals a recalibration of the UAE's approach to engagement with Tehran amidst rising security concerns. While the UAE had previously pursued a path of de-escalation and dialogue with Iran, the missile attacks necessitated a firm response aimed at safeguarding national security. The implications of these developments extend to regional trade, shipping lanes, and broader geopolitical relations in the Gulf. The immediate aftermath has seen continued vigilance from UAE defense forces and a sustained diplomatic effort to address the root causes of regional instability, particularly the conflict in Yemen. The international community continues to monitor the situation, urging de-escalation and adherence to international law.
