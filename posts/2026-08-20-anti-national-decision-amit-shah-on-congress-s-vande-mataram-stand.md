Union Home Minister Amit Shah has strongly criticized the Indian National Congress for its continued practice of reciting only the first two stanzas of the national song, 'Vande Mataram'. Mr. Shah, a senior leader of the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), publicly labeled the Congress party's position as an "anti-national decision," reigniting a long-standing debate concerning national symbols and cultural heritage in India. The remarks were made during a recent public address, drawing significant attention to the historical and political interpretations of the revered song.

Mr. Shah emphasized the profound historical significance of 'Vande Mataram,' highlighting its role as a powerful rallying cry during India's independence movement against British rule. He asserted that the song, in its entirety, embodies the spirit of national pride and sacrifice. "The Congress party's decision to limit the recitation to only two stanzas disrespects the sacrifices of countless freedom fighters who embraced 'Vande Mataram' as their anthem," Mr. Shah stated, linking the selective recitation to a broader disregard for the nation's foundational values and symbols. He urged the Congress to reconsider its stance and embrace the full rendition of the song, which he described as an integral part of India's identity.

The Indian National Congress has historically defended its practice of reciting only the first two stanzas, citing objections raised by certain communities to later stanzas during the pre-independence era. This stance dates back to discussions within the Congress Working Committee in the late 1930s, which acknowledged sensitivities around certain interpretations of the complete song. While the initial two stanzas are widely accepted and universally revered, subsequent stanzas have historically faced scrutiny over their religious undertones, particularly by some Muslim leaders and organizations who found them problematic. This historical context has shaped the Congress party's approach to its recitation at public events and party gatherings.

This issue frequently emerges as a point of contention in India's political landscape, particularly between the BJP and the Congress. The BJP, often advocating for a more unified and robust expression of nationalism, typically champions the complete and unconditional embrace of national symbols like 'Vande Mataram'. In contrast, the Congress often navigates these issues with an emphasis on inclusive interpretations that accommodate diverse community sentiments. The current exchange underscores the ideological divergence between the two major political parties on matters of national identity, cultural assimilation, and historical narratives.

The debate surrounding 'Vande Mataram' is not merely about a song; it reflects deeper political and cultural divides within the country. While the Constitution of India designates 'Jana Gana Mana' as the National Anthem, 'Vande Mataram' holds the status of the National Song, recognized for its pivotal role in the freedom struggle. Mr. Shah's latest remarks are expected to intensify political discourse, especially as national elections approach, with both parties likely to use such issues to consolidate their respective ideological bases and appeal to different segments of the electorate. The controversy highlights the ongoing tension in India over how national symbols should be interpreted and honored in a diverse society.
