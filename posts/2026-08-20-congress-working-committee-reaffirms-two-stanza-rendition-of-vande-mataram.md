During a recent meeting, the Congress Working Committee (CWC), the highest decision-making body of the Indian National Congress, reiterated its firm stance on the rendition of 'Vande Mataram'. The committee resolved to continue the practice of singing only the first two stanzas of the revered national song during all party events and gatherings, a position consistent with its historical approach to the hymn.

This reaffirmation by the CWC comes amidst ongoing national discourse regarding cultural symbols and their interpretation. The decision underscores the party's continued adherence to a specific protocol for 'Vande Mataram', a song deeply embedded in India's independence movement but which has also been subject to varying interpretations over time. The Congress party's leadership engaged in discussions surrounding the full composition versus its abbreviated form before settling on the reaffirmed position.

'Vande Mataram', penned by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, holds significant historical weight as a rallying cry during the struggle for Indian independence from British rule. It was first sung in a political session by Rabindranath Tagore at the 1896 session of the Indian National Congress. While the complete poem consists of multiple stanzas, the first two stanzas are widely recognized and sung, holding the status of India's national song. The distinction between the national anthem ('Jana Gana Mana') and the national song ('Vande Mataram') is also a key aspect of this discussion, with both holding distinct but equally revered places in the nation's heritage.

Key details regarding the CWC's decision and the historical context include:

*   **Decision Focus:** The CWC explicitly affirmed that only the initial two stanzas of 'Vande Mataram' are to be sung at Congress party functions.
*   **Historical Precedent:** This stance aligns with the Congress party's long-standing tradition and resolution dating back decades, which acknowledged the song's importance while also addressing sensitivities associated with later verses for various communities.
*   **Authorship:** 'Vande Mataram' was composed by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay as part of his 1882 novel, 'Anandamath'.
*   **National Significance:** While 'Jana Gana Mana' is the National Anthem of India, 'Vande Mataram' is recognized as the National Song, possessing an equivalent status in terms of national respect.
*   **Party's Rationale:** The CWC's decision reflects a historical consensus within the party to balance the song's patriotic fervor with an inclusive approach that respects diverse cultural and religious sentiments within the country.

The CWC's directive provides clear guidance for party members and event organizers on the established protocol for 'Vande Mataram'. This decision reinforces the party's internal policy on the matter, ensuring uniformity in its public and private engagements. As political discussions around national identity and cultural symbols continue in India, such reaffirmations by major political bodies like the CWC highlight their considered positions on these sensitive subjects, shaping the future conduct of party activities nationwide.
