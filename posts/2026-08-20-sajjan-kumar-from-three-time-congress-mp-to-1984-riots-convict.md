Sajjan Kumar, a prominent former Member of Parliament (MP) from the Indian National Congress party, is currently serving a life sentence following his conviction in a case related to the 1984 anti-Sikh riots. The Delhi High Court convicted Kumar in December 2018, overturning an earlier acquittal and marking a significant development in the pursuit of justice for the victims of the widespread violence that erupted in India's capital after the assassination of then-Prime Minister Indira Gandhi.

Born in 1945, Sajjan Kumar embarked on a political career that saw him rise through the ranks of the Congress party. He was elected to the Lok Sabha, the lower house of the Indian Parliament, three times from the Outer Delhi constituency. His electoral victories were in 1980, 1984, and 1991, establishing him as an influential political figure in parts of Delhi. Beyond his parliamentary roles, Kumar also served as a Member of the Legislative Assembly (MLA) in Delhi, further solidifying his grassroots presence and political base within the region.

The widespread anti-Sikh violence commenced on October 31, 1984, immediately after the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi by her Sikh bodyguards. Over several days, thousands of Sikhs were killed, and their properties destroyed, primarily in Delhi and surrounding areas. Investigations and eyewitness accounts subsequently implicated several political figures, including Sajjan Kumar, in instigating mobs and overseeing the violence.

The legal process concerning the 1984 riots cases, particularly those involving political figures, has been protracted and complex. Multiple inquiry commissions were established over the decades to investigate the riots.

Key developments in Sajjan Kumar's legal journey include:
*   **Nanavati Commission (2005):** This commission, set up by the Indian government, submitted a report recommending further investigation against Sajjan Kumar for his alleged role in the riots.
*   **CBI Charges:** Following the commission's recommendations, the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) filed charge sheets against Kumar in various cases related to the riots. One prominent case pertained to the killing of five members of a Sikh family in Raj Nagar, Palam Colony, Delhi, and the destruction of a gurdwara on November 1, 1984.
*   **Trial Court Acquittal (2013):** In this specific case, a trial court acquitted Sajjan Kumar in April 2013, citing a lack of sufficient evidence.
*   **Delhi High Court Conviction (2018):** The acquittal was challenged by the victims and the CBI. On December 17, 2018, the Delhi High Court overturned the trial court's verdict. The High Court convicted Sajjan Kumar of criminal conspiracy to commit murder, promoting enmity between different groups, and various other charges under the Indian Penal Code. The court sentenced him to life imprisonment, noting that "political patronage" had shielded perpetrators for decades.
*   **Supreme Court Appeals:** Sajjan Kumar surrendered before a Delhi court on December 31, 2018, to begin serving his sentence. He subsequently appealed his conviction in the Supreme Court of India. The apex court has consistently denied him interim bail during the pendency of his appeal, maintaining his incarceration.

Sajjan Kumar's conviction represented a significant milestone for human rights advocates and victims' families who had pursued justice for over three decades. It underscored the principle of accountability for mass violence, even for high-profile political figures, and highlighted the persistence required in protracted legal battles. As of the latest reports, Sajjan Kumar remains incarcerated, and his appeal continues to be heard before the Supreme Court of India, determining the final legal outcome of his case.
