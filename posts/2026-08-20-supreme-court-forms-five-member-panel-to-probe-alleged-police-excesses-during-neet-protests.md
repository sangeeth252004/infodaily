The Supreme Court of India has established a five-member committee to investigate allegations of excessive force used by police during recent nationwide protests against the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET). The apex court's decision, announced recently, comes amid widespread reports and complaints regarding the use of disproportionate force against students and other protestors demonstrating against alleged irregularities in the medical entrance examination.

The formation of the panel underscores growing concerns over police conduct during public demonstrations and aims to ensure accountability for any instances of misconduct. Protests erupted across various cities following the declaration of NEET-UG 2024 results on June 4, with students and parents raising serious questions about issues such as grace marks awarded to certain candidates, paper leaks, and alleged irregularities in the examination process. These demonstrations, which included sit-ins, marches, and public gatherings, frequently led to confrontations with law enforcement agencies.

The newly constituted committee is tasked with a comprehensive examination of specific incidents where police action has been questioned. Its mandate includes:
*   **Investigating specific complaints:** Examining individual allegations of excessive force, lathi-charge, arbitrary detention, or other forms of police misconduct.
*   **Assessing proportionality:** Evaluating whether the level of force used by police personnel was proportionate to the threat or situation presented by the protestors.
*   **Identifying responsible parties:** Pinpointing officials or units responsible for any proven instances of excessive force.
*   **Recommending remedial measures:** Proposing actions to be taken against erring officials and suggesting guidelines to prevent similar incidents in future public demonstrations.
*   **Ensuring accountability:** Working towards a framework that holds law enforcement accountable while upholding citizens' fundamental right to peaceful protest.

The Court's intervention highlights the judiciary's role in safeguarding fundamental rights, including the right to freedom of speech and peaceful assembly, even in contentious situations. Allegations against the police have ranged from unprovoked assaults on peaceful protestors to the use of water cannons and tear gas in situations deemed by protestors to be unwarranted. Civil society groups and human rights organizations have also called for an independent inquiry into these incidents.

The panel is expected to operate with a degree of autonomy to ensure an impartial investigation. Details regarding the panel members and a specific timeframe for submitting its findings are anticipated to be announced shortly. Its recommendations could have significant implications for police training protocols, crowd management strategies, and mechanisms for addressing grievances related to state action during public unrest. The outcome of this investigation will be closely watched by the public, legal fraternity, and human rights advocates, potentially setting precedents for future engagements between law enforcement and protestors in India.
