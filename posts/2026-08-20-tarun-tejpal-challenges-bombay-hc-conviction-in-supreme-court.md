Tarun Tejpal, the former editor-in-chief of Tehelka magazine, has recently filed a Special Leave Petition (SLP) in the Supreme Court of India, challenging the Bombay High Court's August 2024 judgment. The High Court had overturned his earlier acquittal by a trial court, convicting him in a 2013 sexual assault case and sentencing him to a 10-year rigorous imprisonment term. The appeal seeks to set aside the High Court's verdict and reinstate the trial court’s decision.

The Bombay High Court, on August 1, 2024, reversed the May 2021 judgment by a Goa trial court that had acquitted Tejpal of all charges. The High Court, after a thorough review of the evidence and legal arguments, found him guilty of sexually assaulting a former colleague during an event in Goa in November 2013. The charges against Tejpal included various sections of the Indian Penal Code (IPC) related to sexual harassment, assault, and wrongful restraint.

The case originated from an incident reported in November 2013 at a five-star hotel in Goa during Tehelka’s 'Thinkfest' event. A junior colleague accused Tejpal of sexual assault. Following the complaint, Tejpal stepped down from his position at Tehelka. The Goa Police registered a First Information Report (FIR), leading to his arrest and subsequent chargesheet filing. The trial commenced in 2017.

Key details of the case's progression include:
*   **November 2013:** Incident occurs in Goa; complaint filed.
*   **December 2013:** Tarun Tejpal arrested by Goa Police.
*   **September 2017:** Charges framed against Tejpal under IPC Sections 341 (wrongful restraint), 342 (wrongful confinement), 354 (assault or criminal force to woman with intent to outrage her modesty), 354-A (sexual harassment), 354-B (assault or use of criminal force to woman with intent to disrobe), 376(2)(f) (person in position of authority), and 376(2)(k) (person in a position of control).
*   **May 21, 2021:** The Additional Sessions Court in Goa acquits Tejpal of all charges, citing a lack of corroborative evidence.
*   **August 1, 2024:** The Bombay High Court's Goa bench overturns the trial court’s acquittal, convicting Tejpal and imposing a 10-year rigorous imprisonment term along with a fine of Rs 10 lakh.

Tejpal's legal team has now approached the apex court, arguing that the Bombay High Court erred in its assessment of the evidence and the interpretation of legal principles. The Supreme Court will now review the appeal, a process that involves examining the grounds on which the High Court’s judgment is challenged. The filing of an SLP is a standard legal recourse available to individuals seeking to challenge judgments from High Courts.

The Supreme Court's decision to admit the SLP for hearing will mark the next stage in this high-profile legal battle. The outcome will have implications for the legal precedent surrounding sexual assault cases and the appellate process in India. The date for the Supreme Court's first hearing on the matter is yet to be announced.
