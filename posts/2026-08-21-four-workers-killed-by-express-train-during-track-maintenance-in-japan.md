Four railway maintenance workers were killed early Sunday morning after being struck by an express train on the Tsukuba Express line in Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. The incident, which occurred around 5:30 AM Japan Standard Time (JST) near Tsukuba city, has prompted a full investigation by local authorities and the railway operating company.

The workers, who were reportedly engaged in weed control tasks on the tracks, were hit by a six-car express train operated by the Metropolitan Intercity Railway Company. The train was traveling from Tsukuba to Akihabara in Tokyo and was carrying approximately 20 passengers at the time of the collision. There were no reported injuries among the train's passengers or crew. Train services on the affected line were immediately suspended following the incident, leading to significant disruption for commuters.

Investigations are underway to determine the precise circumstances that led to the fatal collision. Authorities, including local police and the Japan Transport Safety Board, are focusing on several key areas:

*   **Track Access Protocols:** Examining why the maintenance crew was present on operational tracks during scheduled service hours.
*   **Safety Procedures:** Reviewing the safety measures in place, including warning systems, communication protocols, and lookout provisions for workers.
*   **Visibility and Warning:** Assessing whether the workers were adequately alerted to the approaching train or if visibility was a factor at the early morning hour.

The Metropolitan Intercity Railway Company has released a statement expressing profound regret over the tragic loss of life and pledged full cooperation with the ongoing investigation. The company reiterated its commitment to railway safety and indicated that a thorough review of its operational and maintenance procedures will be conducted.

This incident highlights critical concerns regarding railway worker safety, particularly for those performing essential maintenance tasks on active lines. The outcome of the investigation is expected to inform potential revisions to safety standards and operational protocols across Japan's extensive railway network. As of Sunday, train services on parts of the Tsukuba Express line remained suspended, with alternative transportation arrangements being made for passengers. Further updates are anticipated as the investigation progresses.
