New Delhi, India – India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) has affirmed that recent action taken outside the Pakistan High Commission in New Delhi was a "proportionate response," drawing parallels with incidents involving Indian diplomatic missions abroad. The statement by MEA Spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal on Thursday addressed the removal of alleged illegal construction, underscoring that the action was consistent with municipal regulations.

The MEA's clarification follows the intervention by local authorities to dismantle what were described as unauthorized structures on public land adjacent to the Pakistan High Commission premises. While specific dates for the municipal action were not publicly detailed by the MEA, the statement confirmed the government's stance on the matter, framing it within the broader context of diplomatic reciprocity and adherence to host country laws. The Indian government maintains that the action was carried out by relevant municipal bodies in accordance with their established procedures regarding public land and building regulations.

This diplomatic exchange gains significance in light of previous incidents where Indian diplomatic facilities have faced challenges or alleged acts of vandalism in other countries. The MEA statement explicitly referenced these prior occurrences, suggesting a policy of reciprocal measures to ensure the integrity and security of its missions abroad.

Key aspects highlighted by the MEA include:
*   **Reciprocal Diplomacy:** The "proportionate response" phrasing directly links the action in New Delhi to incidents targeting Indian missions in other nations. This approach suggests a tit-for-tat dynamic in diplomatic dealings.
*   **Compliance with Local Laws:** The MEA emphasized that the action was a matter of municipal authorities enforcing local laws regarding illegal encroachments on public property. This frames the incident as an administrative rather than a purely political or diplomatic maneuver.
*   **Protection of Diplomatic Premises:** The Indian government has previously raised concerns about the security and inviolability of its diplomatic properties and personnel globally, particularly in situations involving protests or alleged vandalism.

While the MEA did not detail specific past incidents in its statement, India has previously voiced strong objections over security breaches and alleged acts of desecration at its High Commission in London and other diplomatic outposts. These incidents often involved protests that led to property damage or posed security risks to personnel. The current statement signals India's intent to apply similar scrutiny and enforcement where alleged infractions occur on its soil concerning foreign missions.

The incident is expected to be part of ongoing diplomatic communications between India and Pakistan, two nations with historically complex relations. While neither side has indicated an immediate escalation, the MEA's firm stance suggests a continued emphasis on upholding diplomatic principles and ensuring equitable treatment of missions based on the Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, while also maintaining national sovereignty over local law enforcement. Future developments will likely involve diplomatic notes and bilateral discussions regarding the interpretation and application of these principles.
