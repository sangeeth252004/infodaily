NEW DELHI – Congress leader Rahul Gandhi visited the Parliament Street Police Station on Tuesday, May 7, 2024, to formally lodge a complaint with the Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP). The complaint pertains to an allegedly digitally manipulated video involving Union Home Minister Amit Shah, which has been circulating on social media platforms. Gandhi's action underscores growing concerns over the proliferation of deepfakes and misinformation, particularly during the ongoing electoral period in India.

The complaint, filed by the Member of Parliament from Wayanad, focuses on a video that purports to show Home Minister Amit Shah making statements about reservations. The Congress party alleges that this video has been "doctored" and "fabricated" to misrepresent Shah's original remarks, creating a false narrative. This incident highlights the challenges posed by advanced digital manipulation technologies, such as deepfakes, in influencing public perception and potentially disrupting democratic processes.

*   **Nature of Allegation:** The complaint specifically targets the digital alteration of a video segment featuring Union Home Minister Amit Shah.
*   **Content in Question:** The manipulated content allegedly distorts Shah's comments regarding reservation policies, presenting them out of context or fabricating new statements.
*   **Broader Context:** The move follows recent actions by the Delhi Police's Intelligence Fusion & Strategic Operations (IFSO) unit, which has registered a First Information Report (FIR) and made arrests in connection with similar manipulated videos. These arrests have occurred in various states, including Assam and Telangana, signaling a coordinated effort by law enforcement to tackle online misinformation.
*   **Political Ramifications:** Several Congress leaders have also received notices from the authorities in connection with the circulation of these manipulated videos, prompting a strong response from the party regarding the fairness and impartiality of such investigations.

Gandhi's visit to the police station is seen as a direct counter-action and a means to formally highlight the party's concerns regarding the origin and spread of what it deems to be malicious propaganda. The Congress party maintains that the creation and dissemination of such deepfake content constitute a serious attempt to mislead voters and create communal discord.

The incident brings into sharp focus the imperative for robust mechanisms to verify digital content, especially during high-stakes elections. The increasing sophistication of AI-powered deepfake technology means that visually and audibly convincing fabricated content can be produced with relative ease, making it difficult for the public to distinguish between genuine and false information. As the electoral campaign intensifies, the integrity of information shared across digital platforms remains a critical challenge for political parties, voters, and regulatory bodies alike.

Delhi Police officials confirmed receipt of the complaint and stated that the matter would be investigated in accordance with relevant legal provisions. The outcome of the investigation is anticipated to provide further clarity on the authenticity of the video in question and the individuals responsible for its creation and circulation. This case is expected to serve as a significant precedent for addressing the misuse of digital manipulation technologies in the political sphere moving forward.
