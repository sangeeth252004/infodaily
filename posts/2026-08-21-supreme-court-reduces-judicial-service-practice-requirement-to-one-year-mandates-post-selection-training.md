The Supreme Court of India has announced a significant amendment to the eligibility criteria for candidates aspiring to join the judicial service. Under the new directive, the mandatory minimum period of practice at the Bar required for applicants has been reduced from three years to one year. This modification is coupled with a new requirement for selected candidates to undergo compulsory training and clerkship after their selection.

This revision marks a notable shift in the approach to judicial recruitment, potentially broadening the pool of eligible candidates, including younger law graduates. Previously, the three-year practice rule often posed a barrier, delaying entry for those without immediate access to extensive legal practice opportunities post-graduation. The move is viewed by some as an effort to streamline the entry process and attract a diverse range of talent to the judiciary, emphasizing academic merit alongside foundational practical exposure.

Under the revised framework, the focus has shifted towards structured professional development post-selection. The introduction of mandatory training and clerkship programs is designed to equip newly appointed judicial officers with essential practical skills, a deep understanding of legal procedures, and firsthand experience in courtroom dynamics. This post-selection regimen is intended to compensate for the reduced pre-selection practice experience, ensuring that new judges are adequately prepared for their roles. The specifics of these training modules, including their duration and curriculum, are expected to be elaborated upon by the relevant judicial authorities.

The decision reportedly stems from ongoing deliberations within the legal fraternity regarding judicial reforms and the need to strike a balance between practical experience and academic prowess. Discussions have highlighted the varying eligibility criteria across different states for judicial services and the potential benefits of a more uniform and accessible approach to recruitment.

Key changes introduced by the Supreme Court include:
*   The mandatory practice period at the Bar for judicial service candidates reduced from three years to one year.
*   Selected candidates will now undergo compulsory training and clerkship.
*   The reform aims to facilitate easier entry for qualified law graduates into the judicial system.
*   Emphasizes structured post-selection professional development as a crucial component of judicial preparation.

The implementation of these new rules will necessitate close coordination among the Supreme Court, various High Courts, and state public service commissions responsible for judicial appointments. Further guidelines detailing the post-selection training and clerkship programs are anticipated to ensure a smooth transition and effective preparation of future judicial officers. The impact of these changes on the overall quality, diversity, and composition of the Indian judiciary will be observed as new batches of judges are inducted under the revised criteria.
