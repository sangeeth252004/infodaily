In 1986, the U.S. government completed the acquisition of Santa Rosa Island, a 53,000-acre property off the coast of California, from a private ranching family for a sum approaching $30 million. This transaction integrated the expansive island into the existing Channel Islands National Park, significantly expanding its protected area. As part of the agreement, the former landowners retained approximately seven acres and secured a leaseback arrangement allowing them to continue operations on the island for an additional 25 years.

Santa Rosa Island is the second largest of California's Channel Islands, situated approximately 27 miles southwest of Santa Barbara. For over a century prior to the sale, the island had been privately owned and operated primarily as a cattle ranch by a single family. This long history of private stewardship included extensive ranching operations that shaped the island's landscape and economy. The decision to sell marked a pivotal shift for the island, transitioning its management from private agricultural enterprise to public conservation.

The 1986 purchase price of nearly $30 million underscored the substantial value and scale of Santa Rosa Island. Comprising 53,000 acres, the acquisition represented a significant expansion for the National Park System in Southern California. Key terms of the agreement provided for a phased transition. While ownership transferred to the federal government, the sellers retained a small parcel of land, approximately seven acres, and were granted the right to maintain their presence and ranching activities under a special use permit for a period spanning 25 years. This arrangement was designed to allow a gradual cessation of private operations and a smooth transition to park management and public access.

The integration of Santa Rosa Island into the Channel Islands National Park system was driven by federal conservation objectives. The park, established in 1980, aims to protect the unique natural and cultural resources of the five northern Channel Islands. The acquisition of Santa Rosa Island added a crucial component to this conservation effort, safeguarding diverse ecosystems, endemic species, and significant archaeological sites. This move facilitates broader public access for recreation, education, and scientific research, aligning the island's future with national environmental protection goals.

Key details of the 1986 Santa Rosa Island acquisition:
*   **Location:** Off the coast of Santa Barbara, California.
*   **Acreage:** 53,000 acres.
*   **Sale Price:** Nearly $30 million.
*   **Seller:** A private ranching family (former landowners).
*   **Buyer:** United States Government.
*   **Designation:** Became part of the Channel Islands National Park.
*   **Retention Clause:** Former landowners retained approximately 7 acres and a 25-year leaseback right.

Following the expiration of the 25-year leaseback period in 2011, full management and control of Santa Rosa Island transitioned to the National Park Service. Today, the island is fully accessible to the public, offering opportunities for hiking, camping, and wildlife viewing, while continuing to serve as a vital site for ecological restoration and scientific study. The 1986 sale remains a landmark event in U.S. land conservation, solidifying the long-term protection of a significant California island ecosystem.
