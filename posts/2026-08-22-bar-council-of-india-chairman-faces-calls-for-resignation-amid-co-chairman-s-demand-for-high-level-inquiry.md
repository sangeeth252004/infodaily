The Chairman of the Bar Council of India (BCI), Manan Kumar Mishra, is facing significant pressure to resign, alongside a formal demand for a high-level inquiry into allegations of misconduct and impropriety. The call for an investigation has been spearheaded by BCI Co-Chairman S. Prabakaran, who has directly appealed to the Chief Justice of India to intervene. This development, emerging recently, places India's apex regulatory body for legal education and the legal profession under intense public and professional scrutiny.

The controversy intensified following a letter dispatched by Mr. Prabakaran to Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud, who holds the statutory position of Visitor to the BCI. In his communication, dated May 29, 2024, Mr. Prabakaran urged the Chief Justice to constitute an independent committee to thoroughly investigate specific accusations against Chairman Mishra. Mr. Prabakaran emphasized the gravity of the situation, stating that he would disassociate himself from the affairs of the BCI if Chairman Mishra did not resign to facilitate an impartial probe into the allegations.

The primary allegations detailed in Mr. Prabakaran's correspondence, and echoed by other legal professionals, revolve around two critical areas of governance within the BCI:

*   **Illegal Sale of Electoral Rolls:** Allegations claim the unauthorized and illegal sale of electoral rolls to candidates during the recently concluded state bar council elections. Such a practice, if substantiated, could significantly undermine the fairness and transparency of the electoral process for electing representatives to state bar councils, which are crucial for the functioning of the legal fraternity.
*   **Improper Shop Allotments:** Further accusations involve the illegal allotment of commercial shops and spaces situated within the BCI premises. These allotments are alleged to have been made to family members and close relatives of Chairman Mishra, raising serious questions about potential conflicts of interest, favoritism, and adherence to established allocation procedures.

The Bar Council of India, established under the Advocates Act, 1961, is mandated with the crucial responsibilities of regulating legal education, setting standards for professional conduct and etiquette for lawyers, and exercising disciplinary jurisdiction over legal practitioners across India. Any challenge to the integrity or leadership of this institution is considered to have profound implications for the credibility and public trust in the Indian legal system.

Adding to the pressure, members of the executive committee of the Bar Council of Uttar Pradesh have also publicly called for Mr. Mishra's immediate resignation. Their demand cites broader concerns of "mismanagement and corruption" within the BCI's administration. These collective demands from senior members and state-level bodies underscore a growing concern regarding governance and transparency at the national regulatory level for legal professionals.

The situation remains dynamic as the legal community awaits a formal response from the Chief Justice of India to Mr. Prabakaran's urgent request. The potential constitution of an inquiry committee and its subsequent findings are poised to significantly influence the leadership, internal accountability mechanisms, and future operational integrity of the Bar Council of India.
