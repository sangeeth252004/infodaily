Iranian authorities have escalated efforts to enforce mandatory hijab laws across the country, marked by the reintroduction of morality police patrols and significant judicial and business penalties. This renewed crackdown, which commenced in mid-July 2024, follows a period of reduced enforcement activity in the wake of widespread anti-establishment protests that began in September 2022. The latest measures aim to counter persistent defiance by women who continue to appear in public without head coverings.

The intensified enforcement campaign, officially termed "Project Nour" (Project Light), signals a firm resolve by the government to uphold the country's Islamic dress code. The implementation includes a multi-pronged approach involving increased street patrols, judicial prosecutions, and the use of advanced surveillance technologies. This initiative comes nearly two years after the death of Mahsa Amini, a 22-year-old woman who died in the custody of the morality police after being arrested for alleged hijab violations, triggering nationwide "Woman, Life, Freedom" protests.

Key details of the renewed enforcement include:

*   **Reinstatement of Morality Police Patrols:** Plainclothes and uniformed officers associated with the Guidance Patrols (Gasht-e Ershad) have returned to city streets, directly confronting women deemed to be in violation of the hijab regulations.
*   **Business Closures:** Numerous businesses, including cafes, restaurants, and shops, have been ordered to close permanently or temporarily. These establishments are penalized for failing to enforce hijab rules among their female customers or staff.
*   **Judicial Action:** Iranian courts have initiated proceedings against hundreds of women identified as non-compliant. Authorities have reported sending tens of thousands of warning messages to individuals detected violating the dress code.
*   **Technological Surveillance:** The government is leveraging technology, including closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras and facial recognition software, to identify and track women who do not adhere to the mandatory veiling rules in public spaces.
*   **New Legislative Measures:** Discussions are ongoing regarding the "Chastity and Hijab Bill," a proposed law that seeks to introduce harsher penalties for non-compliance, including stricter fines and extended prison sentences for individuals deemed to be promoting unveiled attire.

The renewed enforcement has elicited varied reactions across Iranian society. While authorities frame the actions as necessary to preserve Islamic values and public order, human rights organizations and segments of the populace view them as an infringement on personal freedoms and a regression of women's rights. The crackdown has notably impacted the hospitality sector, with business owners facing economic losses due to forced closures.

The Iranian judiciary and Ministry of Interior have issued directives emphasizing the mandatory nature of the hijab. Officials have stated that defiance will not be tolerated and that all necessary legal means will be employed to ensure compliance. The situation indicates an ongoing period of social tension as authorities continue their efforts to impose dress codes while many women maintain their defiance of the mandatory veiling rules. Future developments may include further legislative action and continued public resistance to the enforcement measures.
