Former U.S. President Donald Trump recently shared an image on social media that labeled the Strait of Hormuz as "new US territory." This development coincided with a declaration from Iranian authorities stating that the strategically vital waterway is "closed." These contrasting pronouncements regarding one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints have introduced a new layer of tension to an already complex geopolitical landscape.

The Strait of Hormuz serves as the sole sea passage from the Persian Gulf to the open ocean, making it an indispensable route for global energy markets. Approximately one-fifth of the world's total petroleum consumption, and a significant portion of liquefied natural gas (LNG), transits through this narrow channel daily. Its strategic location between Iran and the Musandam Peninsula, shared by Oman and the United Arab Emirates, underscores its economic and geopolitical importance. Disruptions to shipping in the strait can have immediate and widespread effects on international oil prices and global supply chains.

The image shared by Mr. Trump depicted a map with the Strait of Hormuz marked as U.S. territory, accompanied by text outlining this claim. The specifics of the image's origin or the intent behind its dissemination were not detailed in the shared post. This action by a former U.S. President, while not representing current U.S. foreign policy, has garnered attention due to the sensitivity surrounding the strait's status and the history of U.S.-Iran relations.

In response to these developments and potentially unrelated regional events, Iranian officials issued statements affirming their control over the Strait of Hormuz and indicating its closure. While specific details regarding the duration or nature of this "closure" were not immediately provided, such statements from Iran have historically been viewed as warnings against perceived threats to its national security or sovereignty in the region. Iran has previously articulated that the strait's status as an international waterway does not preclude its right to control access under specific circumstances, a stance often contested by international maritime law advocates.

*   **Strategic Significance:** The Strait of Hormuz is approximately 39 miles (63 km) wide at its narrowest point, with the shipping lane itself only about 2 miles (3 km) wide in each direction.
*   **Economic Impact:** Any sustained disruption to traffic through the strait could lead to severe global energy shortages and significant price volatility.
*   **International Law:** Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), the strait is considered an international waterway where all vessels, including warships, have the right of transit passage.

The sharing of the image by Mr. Trump and the subsequent declaration by Iran underscore the enduring geopolitical sensitivities surrounding the Strait of Hormuz. International governments and maritime organizations typically advocate for the free and unhindered passage of commercial and military vessels through the strait, in line with international maritime conventions. The situation remains subject to ongoing observation by international bodies, with potential for further diplomatic engagement or statements from official government channels to clarify stances on the waterway's international status and navigation rights.
