The Indian Sugar Mills Association (ISMA) has attributed the recent surge in domestic sugar prices to speculative stocking by traders and not to any fundamental shortage in supply. The industry body characterized the increase as "unnatural," asserting that current price levels do not reflect the actual demand-supply dynamics within the country. This statement comes amidst concerns over rising food inflation and its potential impact on consumers nationwide.

ISMA's position highlights a divergence between market prices and underlying production estimates. According to the association, sugar production for the current season, which commenced in October, is projected to be sufficient to meet domestic consumption requirements. Despite these adequate supply forecasts, wholesale and retail sugar prices have reportedly seen an uptick in recent weeks, prompting ISMA to call for closer monitoring of market activities.

The association elaborated that the current upward trajectory in prices is largely a result of certain market participants holding back stocks in anticipation of further price hikes. This behavior, ISMA suggests, artificially inflates prices by reducing immediate market availability, even when overall stocks are healthy. Such speculative stocking can create an illusion of scarcity, thereby driving up consumer costs.

*   **Key points raised by ISMA:**
    *   Current production estimates indicate sufficient sugar availability to meet domestic demand.
    *   Price increases are not aligned with underlying demand-supply fundamentals.
    *   Speculative holding of stocks by traders is identified as a primary driver of the price surge.
    *   The market trend is officially described as "unnatural" by the industry body.

The rise in sugar prices is a significant concern for households, as sugar is a staple commodity in daily consumption. Any sustained increase in its cost can contribute to broader inflationary pressures on food items, affecting household budgets. Government agencies are understood to be monitoring the situation closely, given the potential for consumer distress and the wider economic implications of commodity price volatility.

ISMA has indicated that it will continue to engage with government authorities to provide updated information on production, stock levels, and market trends. The focus remains on ensuring market stability and preventing artificial price manipulation. Future actions by regulatory bodies could include measures to deter speculative activities or steps to ensure a consistent flow of sugar into the market, should prices continue on their upward trajectory without a clear fundamental basis. The industry's outlook for the remainder of the season will depend heavily on these potential market interventions and the actualization of production forecasts.
