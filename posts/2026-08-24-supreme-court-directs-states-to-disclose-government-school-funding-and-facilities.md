New Delhi, India – The Supreme Court of India, led by Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud, recently directed all state governments and Union Territories to submit detailed affidavits outlining the funding allocated to and facilities available in government-run schools. The directive, issued during a hearing, underscores the court's emphasis on transparency and accountability in public education, asserting that citizens possess a fundamental "right to know" about these crucial aspects.

The Supreme Court's bench highlighted the constitutional mandate for education and the necessity for state authorities to ensure robust infrastructure and adequate resources for students. The move is a significant step towards addressing disparities and ensuring quality education standards across the nation's vast network of government schools. The court's demand seeks to empower citizens with information regarding the utilization of public funds and the actual state of educational provisions.

Key details requested by the Supreme Court include:

*   **Budget Allocation:** Comprehensive figures detailing the annual budget allocated to government schools at state and district levels.
*   **Expenditure Reports:** Specifics on how these funds have been utilized, including expenditures on infrastructure development, teacher salaries, educational materials, and maintenance.
*   **Infrastructure Availability:** Data on the current state and availability of essential facilities, such as:
    *   Adequate classrooms and seating arrangements.
    *   Functional sanitation facilities for both boys and girls.
    *   Access to safe drinking water.
    *   Availability of playgrounds and sports equipment.
    *   Libraries and laboratories.
*   **Teacher-Student Ratios:** Information on the number of qualified teachers per student, addressing potential staffing shortages.
*   **Enrollment and Outcomes:** Data on student enrollment, retention rates, and general academic performance metrics.

The directive stems from concerns raised regarding the varying quality of education and infrastructure in government schools, which serve a significant portion of India's student population. The court has granted states and Union Territories a stipulated timeframe, typically several weeks, to compile and submit the requested information. This comprehensive data is expected to provide a clearer picture of the challenges and progress within the public education sector.

The Supreme Court's intervention reiterates the judiciary's role in upholding fundamental rights, including the right to education under Article 21A of the Indian Constitution. By demanding this level of detail, the court aims to facilitate informed public discourse, enable better policy-making, and potentially prompt states to address deficiencies more effectively. The outcome of these submissions is anticipated to guide further judicial oversight and could lead to specific recommendations for improving the educational landscape in government schools nationwide. The next phase will involve the court's review of the submitted affidavits and subsequent directions.
