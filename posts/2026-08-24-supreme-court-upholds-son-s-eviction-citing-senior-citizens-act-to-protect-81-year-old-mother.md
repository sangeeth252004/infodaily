NEW DELHI – The Supreme Court of India has recently intervened in a property dispute, restoring an eviction order against a son to protect his 81-year-old mother. The ruling, which emphasizes the provisions of the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007, allows the elderly mother to regain full possession of her residential property after she alleged being forced to leave.

The apex court's decision reverses an earlier High Court order that had set aside the initial eviction. This judgment reinforces the legal framework designed to safeguard the welfare and property rights of senior citizens, particularly in situations where they face harassment or are dispossessed from their homes by family members. The case highlights the judiciary's commitment to upholding the intent of the 2007 Act.

The dispute originated from a complaint filed by the 81-year-old mother, who alleged that her son had forced her out of her own residence. She sought protection under the Senior Citizens Act, which grants Maintenance Tribunals the power to order the eviction of children or other relatives from a senior citizen's property if they are subject to harassment or neglect. The Tribunal, recognizing the mother's plight, initially ordered the son's eviction.

However, the son contested this decision, arguing that he had co-ownership rights to the property and that it was a self-acquired asset. The High Court subsequently ruled in his favor, setting aside the Tribunal's eviction order. This prompted the mother to appeal to the Supreme Court, seeking reinstatement of the original eviction.

*   **Key Legal Provisions:** The Supreme Court focused on the welfare objective enshrined in the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.
*   **Tribunal Powers:** The Act empowers Maintenance Tribunals to ensure the protection of life and property of senior citizens.
*   **Ownership Immateriality:** The Supreme Court clarified that the right of a senior citizen to reside peacefully in their home, free from harassment, takes precedence. Claims of co-ownership or the self-acquired nature of the property do not automatically negate the Tribunal's power to evict.
*   **Welfare Over Title:** The judgment underscored that the Act aims to prevent senior citizens from being left abandoned or neglected, providing a mechanism for swift redressal even in complex property disputes.

In its ruling, the Supreme Court determined that the High Court had erred in overturning the Tribunal's decision. The Court observed that the primary purpose of the Senior Citizens Act is to ensure the dignity and security of elderly individuals. It reiterated that Tribunals are well within their jurisdiction to issue eviction orders to safeguard a senior citizen's property rights against children or heirs who cause distress or attempt to usurp property.

Following the Supreme Court's pronouncement, the eviction order against the son has been reinstated. This development ensures that the 81-year-old mother will regain lawful possession of her home, allowing her to live in peace and security. The judgment is expected to serve as a significant precedent, reinforcing the legal protections available to senior citizens across India and encouraging Maintenance Tribunals to exercise their powers effectively in similar cases.
