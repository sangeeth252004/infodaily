PATNA, BIHAR – Political strategist Prashant Kishor has publicly challenged Bihar Chief Minister Nitish Kumar, Deputy Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, and Rashtriya Janata Dal (RJD) leader Tejashwi Yadav to successfully clear a Class 10 examination. The challenge, issued during a public address in Bihar, has ignited discussions regarding educational qualifications and their perceived relevance to political leadership and governance in the state.

Kishor's unconventional dare forms part of his ongoing "Jan Suraaj" campaign, a grassroots initiative aimed at fostering better governance and development in Bihar. During his address, Kishor explicitly invited the three prominent political figures to collectively sit for and pass a Class 10 matriculation exam, suggesting that their ability to do so would reflect on their intellectual capacity to lead the state. He elaborated that he would arrange for the examination and subsequent evaluation, emphasizing the objective nature of the test.

The political strategist has frequently criticized the state's leadership, alleging that they lack the vision and intellectual acumen required to address Bihar's persistent developmental challenges. This latest challenge is framed as a direct questioning of their fundamental understanding and problem-solving abilities, linking educational attainment to effective governance. Kishor highlighted that the leaders, despite their extensive political experience, have presided over a state that continues to struggle with various socio-economic indicators.

Key details of Kishor's challenge include:
*   **Targeted Leaders:** Chief Minister Nitish Kumar, Deputy Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, and RJD leader Tejashwi Yadav.
*   **The Challenge:** Successfully clear a standard Class 10 (matriculation) examination.
*   **Kishor's Rationale:** To demonstrate intellectual capability and a foundational understanding deemed necessary for effective state governance.
*   **Context:** Issued amidst Kishor's "Jan Suraaj" foot march, which aims to engage directly with the public on issues of development and governance.

Kishor has been undertaking a extensive "padayatra" (foot march) across Bihar as part of his "Jan Suraaj" movement, engaging with citizens and critiquing the current political establishment. His campaign focuses on identifying local issues, proposing solutions, and advocating for a new political alternative in the state. The Class 10 challenge is consistent with his broader strategy of directly questioning the competencies and track records of established political figures.

As of reporting, there has been no official response from Chief Minister Nitish Kumar, Deputy Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, or RJD leader Tejashwi Yadav regarding Kishor's specific challenge. The public dare, however, is expected to fuel further debate within Bihar's political circles, especially as the state prepares for future assembly elections. This public discourse contributes to ongoing discussions about accountability, leadership qualities, and the standards expected from public representatives in the region.
