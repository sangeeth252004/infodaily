Beijing, China – In June 2018, China issued a firm warning to the United States regarding new sanctions targeting Iran's oil sector, declaring its intent to defend its legitimate interests. The response from China's Foreign Ministry came as the US administration under then-President Donald Trump moved to reimpose punitive measures following its withdrawal from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), also known as the Iran nuclear deal. These developments marked a significant point of contention in international trade and diplomatic relations.

The US administration had announced its intention to exert "unprecedented financial pressure" on Tehran, aiming to reduce Iran's oil exports to "zero." This move was described by some US officials as an "economic D-day" for Iran. The renewed sanctions were slated to take full effect by November 4, 2018, compelling countries globally to cease oil purchases from Iran or face secondary sanctions from the United States.

China, a major importer of Iranian crude oil and a key trading partner, expressed strong opposition to these unilateral actions. China's Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Lu Kang addressed the issue, emphasizing Beijing's position on international law and trade.

Key details of China's stance and the broader context include:

*   **Opposition to Unilateral Sanctions:** China consistently reiterated its stance against any unilateral sanctions not authorized by the United Nations Security Council, arguing that such measures violate international law and undermine the global trading system.
*   **Defense of Legitimate Interests:** Spokesperson Lu Kang stated that China would take measures to protect the legitimate rights and interests of its companies. This included upholding normal economic and trade cooperation with Iran, which Beijing views as permissible under international frameworks.
*   **Bilateral Trade Importance:** China is Iran's largest oil customer and a significant investor in the country's energy infrastructure. The bilateral trade relationship, particularly in crude oil, is a crucial component of China's energy security and economic engagement in the Middle East.
*   **Implications for Global Oil Markets:** The prospect of major disruptions to Iranian oil exports raised concerns about global crude supply and potential price volatility, affecting economies worldwide.

The confrontation highlighted the growing divergence between the US and China on international economic policy and multilateral agreements. While the US sought to isolate Iran, China maintained that dialogue and adherence to the JCPOA were the appropriate paths forward.

Looking ahead from that period, the situation presented challenges for countries reliant on Iranian oil and for global stability. The US sanctions placed other nations, including allies, in a difficult position of choosing between compliance with US demands or maintaining established trade relations with Iran. For China, the commitment to defending its interests signaled a readiness to resist what it viewed as extraterritorial application of US law and to safeguard its strategic energy supplies and economic partnerships. The long-term implications for US-China relations and the future of the Iran nuclear deal remained a focal point for international observers.
