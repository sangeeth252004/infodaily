HDFC Bank has announced its intention to appeal a decision by the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) that approved a resolution plan involving a significant 99.97% haircut on its claims against Subhash Chandra, founder of Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. The private sector lender seeks to challenge the substantial reduction in recoverable dues linked to personal guarantees provided by Chandra, by filing an appeal before the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT).

This development stems from the resolution process concerning an entity within the Essel Group, where Subhash Chandra had reportedly provided personal guarantees for corporate debts. The NCLT's approval of the resolution plan effectively means HDFC Bank would recover only 0.03% of its original claimed amount against Chandra. Such a severe reduction highlights the complexities and challenges faced by financial creditors in recovering dues, particularly when dealing with personal guarantees under India’s Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC), 2016.

The specific claims relate to defaults on loans where Chandra acted as a personal guarantor. While the exact initial claim amount has not been publicly detailed by HDFC Bank in its announcement, the scale of the haircut indicates a multi-crore exposure for the lender. The NCLT, in its judgment, had sanctioned the resolution plan, which included the drastic reduction in the amount recoverable from Chandra as a personal guarantor. HDFC Bank's decision to appeal signals its dissatisfaction with the approved recovery terms and its commitment to maximizing its financial recoveries from stressed assets.

Key details surrounding the appeal include:
*   **Appellant:** HDFC Bank
*   **Subject of Appeal:** 99.97% haircut on claims
*   **Basis of Claim:** Personal guarantees provided by Subhash Chandra
*   **Original Adjudicating Authority:** National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT)
*   **Appellate Authority:** National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT)
*   **Context:** Resolution process under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) involving an Essel Group company.

The appeal underscores HDFC Bank's strategic pursuit of better recovery outcomes and potentially aims to set a precedent for the enforcement of personal guarantees under the IBC framework. The outcome of this case could significantly influence how financial creditors approach the recovery of dues from personal guarantors of corporate debtors, particularly regarding the valuation and acceptance of resolution plans. It also brings into focus the ongoing evolution of personal insolvency provisions within the IBC and their practical application.

The legal proceedings are now set to advance to the NCLAT, where HDFC Bank will present its arguments against the approved resolution plan's treatment of its claims. The appellate tribunal's decision will be closely watched by the financial sector and legal experts, as it carries significant implications for the interpretation and application of personal insolvency laws and the rights of financial creditors in India's dynamic insolvency regime.
