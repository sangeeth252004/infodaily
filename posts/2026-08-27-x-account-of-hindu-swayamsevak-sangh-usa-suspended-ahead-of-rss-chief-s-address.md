The official X (formerly Twitter) account of the Hindu Swayamsevak Sangh (HSS) USA, an organization affiliated with the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) of India, was suspended earlier this week. The suspension occurred just days before a significant event in the United States, "Sangam 2024," where RSS chief Mohan Bhagwat is scheduled to address the Indian diaspora.

The HSS USA confirmed the account's suspension, expressing surprise and stating it was actively working to understand the reasons behind X's action. No immediate public explanation for the suspension was provided by X, nor had the HSS USA received a direct notification detailing a violation of the platform's terms of service at the time of reporting.

The "Sangam 2024" event, organized by HSS USA, is a large-scale gathering planned for September 22-24 in the Chicago area. The event aims to connect with and engage members of the Indian diaspora, with Mr. Bhagwat's address being a key highlight. The suspension of the organization's primary social media channel for outreach has raised concerns among organizers regarding communication and promotion for the upcoming program.

The Hindu Swayamsevak Sangh USA describes itself as a voluntary, non-profit, social, and cultural organization. It operates with the stated mission of preserving and promoting Hindu culture and values within the United States. The organization identifies as the largest Hindu organization in the US, engaging in various community and educational activities.

Social media platforms like X maintain specific policies regarding user conduct and content. These policies typically prohibit activities such as hate speech, harassment, impersonation, spam, and the promotion of illegal activities. Accounts found to be in violation of these rules can face actions ranging from temporary suspensions to permanent bans. In past instances, X has temporarily suspended high-profile accounts, including a brief suspension of the Indian government's X account in June.

The suspension of a prominent organizational account ahead of a major event underscores the impact of social media platform moderation decisions on communication and public engagement. For organizations heavily reliant on digital channels for event promotion and community interaction, such suspensions can disrupt outreach efforts and information dissemination.

The organization has not yet announced alternative primary communication channels for the "Sangam 2024" event in light of this development. The situation highlights ongoing discussions about platform governance, content moderation, and their implications for organizations operating within public digital spaces.
