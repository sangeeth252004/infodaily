Harmeet Dhillon, a notable Republican attorney and former Republican National Committeewoman, has weighed in on the burgeoning controversy involving Indian tech founder Ankit Gupta and prominent US-based startup accelerator Y Combinator (YC). Dhillon stated that "American employers cannot discriminate against US citizens," underscoring legal principles amid allegations of discriminatory practices in venture capital investment.

The controversy originated from claims by Ankit Gupta, founder of the AI startup "Aspect," who alleged that Y Combinator rescinded a previously extended offer for his company to join its accelerator program. According to Gupta, YC cited concerns regarding the "non-US persons" status of the founders and mentioned the prevailing "geopolitical climate," specifically referencing AI companies and the US-India tax treaty, as reasons for the decision. This has ignited debate within the global startup ecosystem regarding investment policies and potential biases.

Gupta's public allegations detailed specific communications from Y Combinator, which he claimed indicated a shift in the accelerator's investment strategy concerning international founders in sensitive technology sectors. His account suggested that YC was "not investing in any companies founded by individuals who are not US persons due to the current geopolitical climate, specifically regarding AI companies and the US-India tax treaty."

In response to these claims, Y Combinator has issued a denial. Kat Manalac, YC’s Head of Communications, publicly refuted Gupta's assertions, stating, "We have not rescinded an offer from any company due to the founder not being a US person." Manalac further emphasized YC’s longstanding history of supporting international entrepreneurs, adding, "Y Combinator has invested in companies with international founders for 18 years and will continue to do so." This denial suggests a fundamental disagreement over the reasons for the alleged withdrawal of the offer.

Dhillon's comments highlight the broader legal implications of such allegations within the American employment and business landscape. While the specific details of Ankit Gupta's citizenship status have not been publicly confirmed by independent sources, Dhillon's intervention draws attention to the general legal frameworks designed to prevent discrimination based on national origin or other protected characteristics in the United States. Her statement underscores that any entity operating within the US, including venture capital firms and accelerators, must adhere to anti-discrimination laws.
