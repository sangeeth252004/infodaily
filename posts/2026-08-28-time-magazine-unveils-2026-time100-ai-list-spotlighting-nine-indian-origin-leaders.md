TIME magazine has announced its "2026 TIME100 AI" list, identifying 100 individuals globally who are significantly influencing the field of artificial intelligence. Among those recognized are nine individuals of Indian origin, acknowledged for their diverse contributions across technology, research, and application. Prominent names from this group include Arvind Krishna, Chairman and CEO of IBM; Anima Anandkumar, Bren Professor at Caltech and Director of ML Research at NVIDIA; and Vivek Raghavan, Chief Product and Technology Officer at the Nandan Nilekani-founded EkStep Foundation.

The "TIME100 AI" list serves as a comprehensive overview of the individuals shaping the development, ethical considerations, and real-world implementation of artificial intelligence. Its selection process aims to highlight leaders whose work is not only advancing AI technology but also impacting society, industry, and policy worldwide. The inclusion of a notable contingent of Indian-origin professionals underscores the growing global footprint and influence of Indian talent in this rapidly evolving technological domain.

Arvind Krishna is recognized for his leadership in steering IBM's strategy towards hybrid cloud and artificial intelligence, focusing on the integration of AI solutions for enterprise clients across various sectors. Under his direction, IBM has continued to invest heavily in AI research and development, emphasizing responsible AI and ethical guidelines. His work is central to defining how large corporations adopt and leverage AI technologies for efficiency and innovation.

Anima Anandkumar's inclusion highlights her foundational research in machine learning and her role in pushing the boundaries of AI hardware and software integration. At Caltech, her work spans theoretical machine learning, tensor methods, and large-scale optimization. Her leadership at NVIDIA's machine learning research further demonstrates her impact on the development of AI algorithms that power high-performance computing and various AI applications, from scientific discovery to industrial automation.

Vivek Raghavan is noted for his significant contributions to public digital infrastructure and the application of AI for social good. His involvement with initiatives such as the Aadhaar project in India and his current role at the EkStep Foundation, which focuses on learning and skilling platforms, showcase his expertise in leveraging technology for large-scale societal impact. His work often involves developing AI-powered solutions that enhance accessibility, education, and public services, particularly within developing economies.

The remaining six Indian-origin individuals on the list represent a spectrum of expertise, including:
*   Pioneers in AI ethics and governance, developing frameworks for responsible AI deployment.
*   Researchers driving breakthroughs in areas such as natural language processing, computer vision, and robotics.
*   Entrepreneurs leading AI-driven startups that are innovating across industries.
*   Policy advisors influencing government strategies on AI development and regulation.

This recognition by TIME magazine reflects the increasing prominence of professionals of Indian origin in steering the global conversation and direction of artificial intelligence. As AI continues to evolve and integrate into more aspects of daily life and industry, the contributions of these leaders are expected to play a crucial role in shaping its future trajectory and societal impact. The list provides insight into the individuals whose vision and work are defining the next era of AI innovation and responsible development.
