Hyderabad, India – All India Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen (AIMIM) President Asaduddin Owaisi has recently delivered a critical assessment of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's reception of the "Order of Friendship" from Uzbekistan. Mr. Owaisi's remarks, made during a public address in Hyderabad, specifically highlighted the historical connection of Uzbekistan to Mughal emperor Babur, prompting a political discussion surrounding the significance of such diplomatic honours within India's domestic context.

Prime Minister Modi was conferred Uzbekistan's highest civilian award, the "Order of Friendship" (Do'stlik Orderi), by Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev during a bilateral visit this week. The prestigious honour was presented in recognition of Prime Minister Modi's substantial contributions to strengthening bilateral relations and fostering cooperation between India and Uzbekistan across diverse sectors, including economic, cultural, and strategic partnerships. The award signifies the deepening strategic ties and mutual respect between the two nations, according to official statements from both sides.

Addressing a gathering, Mr. Owaisi explicitly questioned the narrative surrounding the honour by stating, "the country Babur came from," in reference to Uzbekistan. His statement drew an implicit parallel between the contemporary diplomatic achievement and a historical figure whose legacy remains a subject of complex and often contentious debate in India. Babur, the founder of the Mughal Empire in India in the 16th century, originated from the Fergana Valley, a region located in modern-day Uzbekistan.

Political analysts interpret the AIMIM chief's comments as an effort to introduce a historical and cultural dimension into the discourse surrounding Prime Minister Modi's foreign policy endeavours. Babur's historical role and the subsequent Mughal rule are frequently invoked in Indian political discussions, particularly concerning issues of national identity, historical interpretations, and various communal narratives. Mr. Owaisi's reference taps into the ongoing political sensitivity attached to figures from India's medieval past, particularly within segments of the political spectrum.

Key details surrounding the diplomatic recognition and subsequent political reaction include:

*   **Award Name:** The "Order of Friendship" (Do'stlik Orderi), Uzbekistan's highest civilian decoration.
*   **Recipient:** Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
*   **Presenter:** Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev.
*   **Context of Award:** Recognition of significant contributions to strengthening bilateral ties and promoting cooperation between India and Uzbekistan.
*   **Owaisi's Statement:** Referencing Uzbekistan as "the country Babur came from," drawing a historical link to the Mughal emperor.
*   **Babur's Historical Role:** Founder of the Mughal Empire in India, originating from the Fergana Valley (modern Uzbekistan), a figure central to ongoing historical and political discussions in India regarding national identity and historical narratives.

The exchange highlights how international diplomatic events, while aimed at fostering inter-state relations, can quickly become integrated into domestic political narratives. While the Indian Ministry of External Affairs has consistently emphasized the strategic importance of strengthening ties with Central Asian nations like Uzbekistan, the opposition's commentary reflects an ongoing political strategy to scrutinize government actions through a critical lens. This often involves invoking historical figures to resonate with specific electoral bases and articulate differing viewpoints on national identity and foreign relations.
