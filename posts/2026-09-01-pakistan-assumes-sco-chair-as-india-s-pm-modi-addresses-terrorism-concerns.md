Pakistan has assumed the rotating chairmanship of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO), a Eurasian political, economic, and security alliance. The handover occurred during a recent virtual summit, where member states discussed regional cooperation and global challenges. During the proceedings, Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi delivered a statement underscoring the critical need to address terrorism, directly criticizing the practice of using it as a "strategic asset."

The SCO, established in 2001, comprises eight permanent member states: China, India, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, Russia, Tajikistan, and Uzbekistan. It serves as a platform for multilateral cooperation on security, economic development, and cultural exchange across the Eurasian region. Pakistan's assumption of the chair means it will host various SCO meetings and shape the organization's agenda for the upcoming year, typically a 12-month tenure. This position grants Pakistan the responsibility of coordinating initiatives and fostering dialogue among member nations.

Prime Minister Modi's remarks highlighted India's consistent stance on combating terrorism, a significant concern for regional stability. He stated the imperative for all nations to work collectively against cross-border terrorism and its financing. The phrase "strategic asset" is understood in diplomatic circles to refer to the alleged use of terrorist groups by states to achieve political or geopolitical objectives. India has historically voiced concerns regarding the alleged support for such activities in its immediate neighborhood, without specifically naming any country during the SCO address.

Key aspects of Prime Minister Modi's address included:

*   **Unified Approach:** A call for all SCO member states to adopt a unified and unequivocal approach to counter terrorism in all its forms and manifestations.
*   **No Double Standards:** An insistence that there should be no double standards in dealing with terrorist groups or their sponsors.
*   **Transnational Threat:** Emphasis on terrorism as a transnational threat that requires coordinated international action, transcending national borders.
*   **Financing Terrorism:** A focus on disrupting financial networks and safe havens used by terrorist organizations.

The virtual summit provided a forum for leaders to reaffirm their commitments to the SCO's foundational principles, which include mutual trust, mutual benefit, equality, consultations, respect for cultural diversity, and the pursuit of common development. Discussions also reportedly covered strengthening economic ties, promoting connectivity, and addressing emerging security challenges like cybercrime and drug trafficking within the region.

As Pakistan takes on the chairmanship, the SCO agenda is expected to include continued efforts towards regional stability and economic integration. The organization's focus on counter-terrorism initiatives is likely to remain a prominent theme, particularly in light of ongoing geopolitical developments and the statements made by member leaders during the recent summit. The coming year will see Pakistan hosting and presiding over various SCO ministerial and expert-level meetings, culminating in the next annual Council of Heads of State summit.
