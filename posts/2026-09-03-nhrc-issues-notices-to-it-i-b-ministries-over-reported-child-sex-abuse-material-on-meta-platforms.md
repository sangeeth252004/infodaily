The National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) has recently issued notices to the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (I&B) concerning the reported presence of Child Sex Abuse Material (CSAM) on platforms operated by Meta, including Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. The Commission acted *suo motu* (on its own motion) after taking cognizance of various media reports highlighting the deeply concerning issue of CSAM circulating online.

The notices, directed to the Secretaries of both ministries, mandate a detailed report within four weeks. This report is expected to outline the specific steps already taken or proposed to be taken by the government to address the proliferation of such illegal content. Additionally, the NHRC has requested information regarding actions initiated against individuals responsible for uploading and sharing material depicting child sexual abuse.

The NHRC's intervention underscores the severe threat posed to children by online sexual exploitation and abuse. The Commission has emphasized India's commitment to the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC), which the nation ratified, obliging it to protect children from all forms of exploitation and abuse. This aligns with national laws aimed at safeguarding children in the digital space.

Key details of the NHRC's concern and the legal framework include:

*   **Legal Mandate:** The Commission highlighted Section 67B of the Information Technology (IT) Act, 2000, which explicitly criminalizes the publication or transmission of material that depicts children in sexually explicit acts.
*   **Platform Responsibility:** The IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, impose a legal obligation on social media intermediaries to exercise "due diligence." These rules require platforms to remove unlawful content, including CSAM, within 36 hours of receiving a complaint or a court order.
*   **Deeply Disturbing Trend:** The NHRC noted the "deeply disturbing trend" of child sex abuse material being available and shared across digital platforms, stressing the urgent need for stringent measures to combat this menace.
*   **Government Oversight:** The notices to MeitY and I&B Ministries reflect the government's overarching responsibility in regulating digital platforms and ensuring compliance with laws designed to protect vulnerable populations, particularly children.

The issuance of these notices signals a heightened scrutiny of digital platforms' content moderation practices and their adherence to Indian law regarding child protection. It also reinforces the government's role in enforcing these regulations and holding intermediaries accountable for content that violates human rights and national laws. The requested report is anticipated to provide transparency on governmental efforts and potentially lead to further policy interventions or enforcement actions to enhance online safety for children in India. The outcome of this directive will be closely watched as stakeholders await concrete measures to curb the online spread of CSAM.
