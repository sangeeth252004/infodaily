NAHANT, MA – The town of Nahant, Massachusetts, has successfully concluded an eight-year legal dispute, securing the right to acquire a 12-acre coastal peninsula by eminent domain. This action will preserve the land for public use, halting Northeastern University's plans for a significant expansion on the site.

The legal victory ensures that a parcel of land known as East Point, adjacent to Northeastern University's Marine Science Center, will remain open to the public, a tradition spanning generations. The town's initiative specifically targeted preventing a proposed 55,000-square-foot expansion by the university, which had raised concerns among residents about restricted access and potential environmental impact.

The conflict originated in 2018 when Northeastern University announced its intention to construct a new research facility and other infrastructure on the peninsula. Historically, this land, though privately owned by the university, had been used by Nahant residents and visitors for recreation, including walking, fishing, and enjoying the natural coastal environment.

The town of Nahant responded by initiating eminent domain proceedings, a legal process allowing a government entity to take private property for public use, provided just compensation is paid to the owner. This move signaled the town's commitment to prioritizing public access and the preservation of open space over private institutional development.

Key details of the protracted legal process include:

*   **Initial Plans (2018):** Northeastern University proposed a 55,000-square-foot expansion at its Marine Science Center on East Point.
*   **Town's Response:** Nahant officials began eminent domain proceedings to acquire the 12-acre parcel, citing the need to maintain public access and conservation.
*   **Legal Battle:** The dispute progressed through various legal channels over an eight-year period, involving extensive litigation and community advocacy.
*   **Jury Verdict:** A jury ultimately ruled in favor of the Town of Nahant, affirming its right to take the land by eminent domain. The jury also determined the fair market value and compensation owed to Northeastern University for the acquisition.

The ruling represents a significant outcome for coastal communities grappling with balancing institutional growth, private land ownership, and public access rights. For Nahant, a small town with limited open space, the preservation of East Point is seen as crucial for community character and ecological integrity.

Northeastern University, which operates its Marine Science Center at East Point, had argued for the necessity of the expansion to advance its research capabilities. The university has not yet publicly commented on the verdict's potential impact on its long-term expansion plans or research initiatives in Nahant.

Following the jury's decision, the Town of Nahant is now poised to complete the acquisition process, which will officially transfer ownership of the 12-acre peninsula to the town. This will formally secure the public's right to access and enjoy the coastal area, solidifying its status as protected open space for future generations.
