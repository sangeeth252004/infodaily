The West Bengal Department of Tourism has recently launched a new advertising campaign aimed at promoting the state as a premier travel destination, but its central tagline, "Every corner of world but Bengal," has swiftly drawn widespread criticism and debate across various social media platforms. The campaign, which debuted in recent days, quickly became a focal point of online discussions, with many users interpreting the slogan differently than its apparent intended message.

The advertisement reportedly seeks to position West Bengal as a comprehensive tourist destination, suggesting that visitors can find an array of experiences within the state that might typically be sought in diverse global locations. The tagline's intended meaning appears to convey that Bengal offers such a rich variety of attractions – from mountains to beaches, historical sites to cultural experiences – that one might not need to look elsewhere. However, this nuanced message was largely lost on a significant portion of the online audience.

The core of the online backlash stemmed from a perceived ambiguity and potential negative connotation within the phrase "Every corner of world but Bengal." Critics primarily interpreted the slogan to mean that Bengal itself was excluded from the "corners of the world" worth visiting, or that it lacked the appeal found elsewhere. This interpretation led to a wave of online engagement, characterized by:

*   **Logical critiques:** Many social media users questioned the grammatical construction and logical coherence of the tagline for a tourism promotion.
*   **Humorous interpretations:** Numerous memes and satirical posts emerged, playfully suggesting that the ad inadvertently discouraged visits to the state.
*   **Comparisons:** Users drew comparisons to other successful tourism campaigns, highlighting what they perceived as a misstep in messaging.
*   **Queries about strategy:** Some commentators questioned the advertising agency's strategy and the approval process for such a prominent campaign.

The discussions predominantly unfolded on platforms like X (formerly Twitter), Facebook, and Instagram, with related hashtags trending as users shared their opinions and reactions. The speed and scale of the online response underscore the immediate impact digital platforms have on public perception of government initiatives and marketing efforts.

As of the time of reporting, there has been no official statement from the West Bengal Department of Tourism directly addressing the online criticism or clarifying the intended meaning of the tagline. The incident highlights the complex challenges faced by public bodies in crafting effective digital communication strategies and managing public relations in an era of instant feedback and widespread online discourse. The longevity and effectiveness of the campaign, along with any potential revisions to its messaging, remain subject to future developments.
