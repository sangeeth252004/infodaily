A Boeing 767-300 freighter aircraft, operated by Atlas Air and affiliated with Amazon's global logistics network, experienced a runway excursion and subsequent crash landing at Miami International Airport (MIA) on Thursday. The incident, which occurred at approximately 4:40 PM ET, resulted in a significant plume of black smoke visible from the airport, triggering an immediate emergency response from fire and rescue services.

The cargo jet, carrying three crew members, reportedly veered off Runway 9 during the incident. Miami-Dade Fire Rescue teams rapidly responded to the scene, successfully extinguishing a fire that had erupted. All three crew members on board were reported to have escaped without serious injuries, a detail confirmed by initial reports from authorities. The aircraft sustained substantial damage.

The incident is currently under investigation by federal agencies.
*   The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) was notified immediately and commenced its preliminary inquiry.
*   The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) has dispatched a team of investigators to the site to determine the probable cause of the crash.

Initial reports from various sources, cited by officials, suggest potential mechanical issues or a landing gear collapse as possible contributing factors to the incident. However, these remain unconfirmed as the official investigation proceeds.

This event marks a significant disruption to cargo operations at MIA and draws attention to the safety protocols within air freight services. Atlas Air is a major cargo airline that operates flights globally, including services for Amazon's dedicated air cargo fleet, Amazon Air. The Boeing 767 series is a widely used aircraft for cargo transport due to its capacity and range.

In statements following the incident, both Atlas Air and Amazon acknowledged the event. Atlas Air confirmed the involvement of one of its aircraft and stated its full cooperation with investigating authorities. Amazon reiterated its commitment to safety and expressed relief that the crew members were safe.

Airport operations at MIA experienced some temporary impacts following the incident, with certain runways potentially closed or restricted to facilitate emergency response and initial investigation efforts. Travelers and cargo shippers were advised to check flight statuses for potential delays.

The NTSB's investigation is expected to be thorough, examining flight data recorders, cockpit voice recorders, maintenance records, and witness accounts. Findings from this investigation will be crucial in understanding the precise sequence of events leading to the crash and may inform future aviation safety recommendations. The timeline for a full report from the NTSB typically spans several months to over a year.
