Congress President Mallikarjun Kharge recently issued a response to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's characterization of the opposition Congress party as a "Naraz Fufa" (sulking maternal uncle), asserting that the Prime Minister frequently alters his descriptions of the party. The exchange marks the latest instance of sharp rhetoric between the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and the principal opposition party as political activities intensify across the nation.

Speaking publicly, Mr. Kharge highlighted what he described as a pattern of changing adjectives used by Prime Minister Modi to describe the Congress. He questioned the consistency of these labels, suggesting a political strategy behind the evolving terminology. This counter-attack comes after Prime Minister Modi, during a recent public address, likened the Congress to a "Naraz Fufa" – a common Indian cultural reference for a relative who is perennially displeased and expects constant appeasement. The jibe implied that the Congress party consistently obstructs progress and holds grudges.

Mr. Kharge's remarks underscored a broader point about the nature of political commentary during electoral cycles. He reportedly stated that the ruling party's narrative against the Congress shifts depending on the political context or specific perceived challenges. This criticism aims to portray the Prime Minister's rhetoric as inconsistent and politically motivated rather than based on a stable assessment of the opposition.

Key points of Mr. Kharge's retort included:

*   **Inconsistent Labeling:** Kharge specifically pointed out that the Congress party has been labeled with various terms over time, from "urban Naxals" and "tukde-tukde gang" to being accused of corruption or anti-national sentiments. He indicated that the "Naraz Fufa" jibe is merely the latest in this series of changing descriptions.
*   **Political Context:** The Congress President suggested that these shifting adjectives are tactical, designed to discredit the opposition in different scenarios and influence public perception.
*   **Focus on Issues:** Implicit in Kharge's response was a call to shift the political debate from personal attacks and evolving labels to substantive issues facing the country.

The "Naraz Fufa" metaphor itself carries cultural weight in India, often evoking images of a difficult family member who is never satisfied. By deploying this term, Prime Minister Modi aimed to frame the Congress as a perpetually negative and uncooperative entity in the political landscape. Kharge's rebuttal seeks to neutralize this by redirecting attention to the perceived inconsistency of the accuser.

This latest exchange is indicative of the heightened political temperature ahead of upcoming state assembly elections and the broader build-up to the 2024 general elections. Both parties are actively engaged in shaping narratives, with verbal sparring becoming a prominent feature of public addresses and media interactions. The use of culturally resonant metaphors and direct counter-accusations forms a core part of their communication strategies, aiming to energize their respective voter bases and sway undecided citizens. Political analysts observe that such exchanges are likely to continue, setting the tone for a dynamic and often combative electoral season.
