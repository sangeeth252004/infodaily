India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) has officially dismissed the creation of a common currency for the BRICS bloc, reaffirming its preference for promoting trade settlements in national currencies. This position was articulated by Secretary (Economic Relations) Dammu Ravi during a recent press briefing held in New Delhi, ahead of the upcoming BRICS summit scheduled to take place in Kazan, Russia.

The BRICS group, which includes Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, and its new members, has been exploring various avenues to reduce reliance on the US dollar in international trade. The concept of a common BRICS currency gained traction following discussions at the August 2023 summit in Johannesburg, South Africa, driven by a broader de-dollarization trend among several emerging economies. Proponents suggested a common currency could streamline transactions and mitigate foreign exchange risks for member states.

However, Mr. Ravi emphasized that India's focus remains on strengthening bilateral trade mechanisms, enabling countries to use their respective national currencies for transactions. He highlighted the significant practical complexities involved in establishing a common currency, which would necessitate the creation of a supranational central bank and a unified monetary policy. Such frameworks currently lack a clear consensus or detailed institutional structure among the diverse member states.

The Indian government views local currency settlements as a more achievable and pragmatic approach to facilitate trade within the expanded bloc. This strategy is intended to reduce dependency on third-country currencies like the US dollar, mitigate exchange rate volatilities, and lower transaction costs for businesses operating across BRICS nations. India has actively pursued similar bilateral arrangements, aiming to enhance the international usage of the Indian rupee and streamline trade with various partners.

*   **Official Stance:** India's MEA clarified that there is no active proposal under consideration for a BRICS common currency.
*   **Preferred Mechanism:** The focus is on increasing trade settlements using national currencies between member countries.
*   **Rationale:** To reduce reliance on the US dollar and other major international currencies, lower transaction costs, and manage exchange rate risks.
*   **Challenges of Common Currency:** Establishing a common currency would require a dedicated central bank and harmonized monetary policies, which pose significant hurdles for the geographically and economically diverse BRICS members.

As the BRICS summit in Kazan, Russia, approaches in October 2024, discussions are expected to continue on deepening economic cooperation and fostering resilient financial architectures within the expanded bloc. The promotion of local currency trade and financial integration among member states is anticipated to remain a key agenda item, reflecting a pragmatic pathway toward enhancing economic autonomy and stability.
