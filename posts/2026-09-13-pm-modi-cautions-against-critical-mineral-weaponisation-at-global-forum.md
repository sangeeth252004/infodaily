Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi recently cautioned against the "weaponisation of critical minerals" at an international forum, underscoring the potential for such practices to destabilize global economies and hinder technological progress. The statement, delivered in November 2023 in the presence of various world leaders, including Chinese President Xi Jinping, highlighted growing geopolitical concerns over the control and supply of these essential resources.

The Prime Minister's remarks addressed a critical issue currently facing international relations: the strategic importance of minerals vital for modern industries and clean energy technologies. Critical minerals, which include rare earth elements, lithium, cobalt, and graphite, are indispensable components in a wide array of products, from electric vehicles and renewable energy systems to advanced electronics and defense applications. Disruptions or politically motivated restrictions on their supply could have far-reaching economic and security implications worldwide.

The concept of "weaponisation" in this context refers to the use of critical mineral supply dominance as a geopolitical tool. This can manifest through various means:
*   **Export controls:** Limiting the export of processed or raw critical minerals to exert leverage over importing nations.
*   **Supply chain disruptions:** Creating artificial scarcities or bottlenecks to influence political or economic outcomes.
*   **Strategic hoarding:** Accumulating vast reserves to control market prices and availability.
*   **Investment restrictions:** Blocking foreign access to mineral deposits or processing facilities.

Modi’s warning reflects a broader global sentiment regarding the concentration of critical mineral mining and processing capabilities in a limited number of countries. China, for instance, holds a dominant position in the refining and processing of many rare earth elements and other critical minerals, prompting calls from several nations, including India, the United States, and European Union members, for diversification of supply chains and investment in alternative sources. India, which possesses some reserves of critical minerals like lithium, has been actively exploring options to enhance its domestic capabilities and secure international partnerships to mitigate supply risks.

The increasing demand for critical minerals, driven by the global transition to green energy and advanced digital technologies, has intensified competition and scrutiny over their supply. Ensuring equitable access and stable supply chains is seen as crucial for achieving global sustainability goals and fostering inclusive economic growth. The Prime Minister's statement therefore advocates for collaborative international frameworks and transparent trade practices to prevent the exploitation of mineral dependencies for strategic advantage. Moving forward, discussions are expected to continue among nations regarding the establishment of resilient and diversified supply networks for these vital resources, alongside efforts to explore new deposits and develop recycling technologies.
