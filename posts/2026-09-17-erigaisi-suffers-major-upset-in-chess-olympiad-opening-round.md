Indian Grandmaster Arjun Erigaisi, ranked among the world's top chess players, suffered an unexpected defeat in the opening round of the 45th FIDE Chess Olympiad in Budapest, Hungary. The highly-rated Indian player, with a FIDE rating of 2772, lost to Uzbekistan's International Master Jakhongir Vakhidov, who holds a FIDE rating of 2500. This result marks one of the most significant upsets of the tournament's initial stages, occurring on the event's opening day.

The loss for Erigaisi is notable given his stature in the global chess community. As India's third-highest rated player and currently ranked 14th in the world, he was considered a strong contender for individual performance and a crucial component of the Indian team's aspirations. The 272-point rating difference between Erigaisi and Vakhidov underscores the unlikelihood of the outcome, making it a prominent talking point as the Olympiad commenced. Such early round upsets can set a challenging tone for top seeds, though the long format of the Olympiad allows for recovery.

During the match, Erigaisi, playing with the white pieces—an advantage often associated with a higher win probability—was unable to convert his position into a victory. Vakhidov, despite the rating disparity, executed a strategic game that navigated the complexities and ultimately secured a decisive win. This performance by the Uzbek International Master has drawn attention to his tactical prowess and ability to perform under pressure against a formidable opponent.
