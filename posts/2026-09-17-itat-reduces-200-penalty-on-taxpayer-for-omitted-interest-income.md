New Delhi – An individual taxpayer in India, who initially declared an income of Rs 43,796 but failed to report Rs 14.02 lakh in interest earnings for the assessment year 2011-12, faced a 200% penalty from tax authorities. The Assessing Officer (AO) had imposed a penalty of Rs 4.85 lakh under Section 271(1)(c) of the Income-tax Act, 1961, for furnishing inaccurate particulars of income. However, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) has significantly reduced this penalty to Rs 1.21 lakh, citing an absence of deliberate intent to conceal income.

The case centered on the taxpayer's omission of interest income primarily from a Fixed Deposit (FD). Following the assessment, the taxpayer acknowledged the discrepancy and, prior to the final assessment order, voluntarily paid the additional tax due along with the applicable interest. Despite this voluntary compliance, the Assessing Officer proceeded with the maximum penalty, equating to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded.

The taxpayer subsequently appealed the decision, arguing that the omission was an inadvertent error and not a deliberate attempt to conceal income. She emphasized her status as a senior citizen, being 65 years old at the time of filing the original Income Tax Return (ITR), suggesting that the mistake was an honest oversight rather than an act of malfeasance.

The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal, Delhi bench, comprising Judicial Member Yogesh Kumar Singh and Accountant Member Anil Kumar Sethy, reviewed the specifics of the case. The Tribunal noted several key factors in its decision to grant relief:

*   **Absence of Mens Rea:** The ITAT found no evidence of "mens rea" or a guilty mind on the part of the taxpayer, indicating that the omission was not a deliberate act of concealment.
*   **Voluntary Compliance:** The taxpayer had voluntarily paid the tax on the omitted income along with interest before the assessment was finalized, demonstrating an intent to comply once the error was identified.
*   **Supreme Court Precedent:** The Tribunal referenced a Supreme Court ruling which differentiates between cases of deliberate concealment and those where particulars are merely inaccurate without intent to defraud. This ruling guides that a penalty under Section 271(1)(c) for furnishing inaccurate particulars should not be imposed if there is no element of fraud or wilful neglect.

Based on these considerations, the ITAT concluded that the penalty should be proportionate to the actual tax that would have been evaded if not for the voluntary payment. The Tribunal effectively reduced the penalty from 200% to 25% of the tax due on the under-reported income, bringing the final penalty amount to Rs 1.21 lakh.

This ruling underscores the nuanced application of tax penalties in India. While stringent penalties are in place to deter tax evasion and ensure accurate reporting, the appellate system provides recourse for taxpayers to demonstrate the absence of malafide intent. The ITAT’s decision highlights the importance of distinguishing between honest mistakes and deliberate attempts to conceal income, offering a degree of relief for taxpayers who can prove their bona fide intent while also reinforcing the necessity of due diligence in filing accurate tax returns.
