Pakistan has been ranked 145th out of 146 countries in the World Economic Forum's (WEF) recently released Gender Gap Index, placing it third from the bottom globally. The report highlights significant disparities, with women's low participation in the workforce identified as a primary factor contributing to the nation's persistent gender gap.

The latest WEF report indicates that Pakistan has closed approximately 57.5% of its overall gender gap, a figure largely unchanged from the previous year. This consistent low ranking underscores ongoing challenges in achieving gender parity across key indicators. The index assesses countries based on four sub-indexes: Economic Participation and Opportunity, Educational Attainment, Health and Survival, and Political Empowerment. Pakistan's performance across these areas reflects systemic issues that hinder women's progress and integration into national development.

The most substantial challenge for Pakistan lies in the realm of economic participation and opportunity, where women face significant barriers to entering and remaining in the workforce. This sub-index measures aspects such as labor force participation, wage equality, and the presence of women in leadership roles. The report suggests that societal norms, lack of access to resources, and limited educational opportunities, particularly for rural women, contribute to these low figures.

Furthermore, Pakistan's ranking is also impacted by its performance in other critical areas. The report details the following:
*   **Economic Participation and Opportunity:** This sub-index remains a major area of concern, with women facing considerable hurdles in employment and economic independence.
*   **Political Empowerment:** Women's representation in political decision-making bodies at local, provincial, and national levels continues to be limited, affecting their ability to influence policy and governance.
*   **Health and Survival:** Despite some improvements in certain health metrics, disparities persist in areas related to maternal health and access to healthcare services for women and girls.
*   **Educational Attainment:** While progress has been made in primary education, significant gaps remain, especially in higher education and literacy rates among women, particularly those in rural areas. The lack of equitable access to education directly impacts women's future economic prospects and overall empowerment.

The WEF's annual Gender Gap Index serves as a global benchmark for understanding gender disparities and monitoring progress towards equality. Pakistan's consistent position near the bottom of this index suggests a pressing need for comprehensive policy interventions aimed at improving women's access to education, enhancing their economic opportunities, and ensuring their equitable representation in political and public life. Addressing these entrenched disparities is crucial for the country's social and economic development.
