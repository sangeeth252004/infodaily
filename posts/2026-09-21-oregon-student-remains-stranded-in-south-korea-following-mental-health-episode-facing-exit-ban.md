An Oregon student, August Schroeder, remains unable to leave South Korea months after a July 2023 incident in Seoul involving property damage attributed to a mental health episode. Despite his release from a South Korean detention facility, authorities have imposed a travel ban, preventing his return to the United States pending the resolution of outstanding legal and civil matters.

The incident occurred in July 2023 when Schroeder, then 20 years old, reportedly caused approximately $170 (230,000 Korean Won) in damages. His family has publicly stated that the incident was a manifestation of a severe mental health crisis. Following the event, Schroeder was arrested by South Korean authorities and subsequently held in prison.

After a period of detention, Schroeder was released from prison. However, South Korean legal procedures dictate that a travel ban can be imposed on foreign nationals involved in legal disputes, particularly when civil compensation claims are pending. This ban aims to ensure individuals remain in the country to address these obligations. Schroeder's continued presence in South Korea is reportedly due to ongoing efforts to negotiate a civil settlement with the parties whose property was damaged.

The ongoing travel restrictions present significant challenges for Schroeder and his family, who are based in Oregon. His family has been managing considerable financial burdens, including legal fees, daily living expenses for Schroeder in South Korea, and the costs associated with supporting him remotely. The situation underscores the complexities that can arise when U.S. citizens encounter legal systems in foreign countries, particularly when compounded by health-related circumstances.

The U.S. State Department is aware of Schroeder's case and is providing consular assistance, a standard practice for U.S. citizens facing legal difficulties abroad. This assistance typically includes helping U.S. citizens understand local laws, find legal representation, and communicate with family members, but it does not intervene directly in foreign legal proceedings.

Schroeder's ability to return home is contingent upon the successful resolution of the civil compensation negotiations and the subsequent lifting of the travel ban by South Korean authorities. The timeline for these processes remains indeterminate, leaving Schroeder's future travel plans uncertain as efforts continue to navigate the legal and bureaucratic pathways to secure his return to the United States.
