NEW DELHI – The Supreme Court of India has mandated that all individuals arrested, including those facing charges under the stringent Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA), must be provided with the grounds for their arrest in writing. This significant ruling, delivered on October 3, 2023, by a bench comprising Justices AS Bopanna and PV Sanjay Kumar, reinforces the constitutional right to be informed of the reasons for detention.

The verdict stems from a petition challenging arrests made by the Directorate of Enforcement (ED) under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA). The Court clarified that the requirement to provide written grounds for arrest is not limited to PMLA cases but is a fundamental procedural safeguard applicable across all criminal laws, including the UAPA. This move aims to enhance transparency and ensure due process, particularly in cases involving serious national security allegations where personal liberty is often heavily restricted.

Key aspects of the Supreme Court's ruling include:

*   **Extension of Principle:** The judgment explicitly extends the principle established in previous rulings, such as *Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India*, which mandated written grounds for arrest in PMLA cases, to all arrests made under any law.
*   **Constitutional Mandate:** The Court underscored that this requirement is rooted in Article 22(1) of the Indian Constitution, which guarantees that no person who is arrested shall be detained in custody without being informed, as soon as may be, of the grounds for such arrest.
*   **Beyond Oral Communication:** The ruling clarifies that merely informing an arrested person orally about the grounds of arrest is insufficient. A written document must be furnished to the accused, enabling them to comprehend the specific allegations against them and prepare their defence.
*   **UAPA Implications:** For UAPA cases, where arrests often precede extensive investigations and bail is notoriously difficult to obtain, the mandate for written grounds provides a crucial layer of accountability for arresting authorities. It ensures that even in matters of national security, fundamental rights are upheld.

This judgment is poised to have a substantial impact on the procedures followed by various law enforcement agencies, including state police forces, the National Investigation Agency (NIA), and the ED. It necessitates a shift towards standardized documentation and immediate communication of arrest reasons in a tangible format. Legal experts suggest that this ruling will strengthen the rights of the accused and reduce arbitrary detentions by compelling agencies to articulate clear and justifiable grounds at the time of arrest.

The decision underscores the judiciary's role in safeguarding individual liberties against potential overreach by the state, even when dealing with laws designed to combat serious crimes like terrorism. It is expected to prompt law enforcement agencies to review and update their arrest protocols to align with the Supreme Court's explicit directive, ensuring that the constitutional right to information during arrest is uniformly and effectively implemented across the country.
