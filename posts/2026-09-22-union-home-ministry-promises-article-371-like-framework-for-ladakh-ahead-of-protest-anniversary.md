The Union Home Ministry has committed to developing a unique constitutional arrangement for Ladakh, potentially similar to provisions under Article 371 or the Sixth Schedule, aimed at addressing the region's demands for special status and protection. This commitment was conveyed during a recent meeting with a joint delegation from the Leh Apex Body (LAB) and the Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA), occurring ahead of the first anniversary of a protest where two individuals reportedly died.

The announcement marks a significant development in the ongoing dialogue between the Central government and various stakeholders in Ladakh. Following its reorganization as a Union Territory (UT) in August 2019, separating it from the erstwhile state of Jammu and Kashmir, local leaders and residents have consistently advocated for constitutional safeguards. These demands primarily center on the protection of Ladakh's unique demographic, culture, land, and environment, alongside calls for statehood.

Key aspects of the ongoing discussions and demands include:
*   **Statehood:** A primary demand from both the LAB and KDA is for Ladakh to be granted full statehood.
*   **Sixth Schedule Inclusion:** The delegations have sought the inclusion of Ladakh under the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution, which provides for the administration of tribal areas in four Northeastern states, granting them autonomy for self-governance and land protection.
*   **Article 371-like Provisions:** Alternatively, the proposed framework could involve special provisions akin to Article 371, which offers special status to various states to protect their indigenous populations and ensure equitable development.
*   **Separate Lok Sabha Seats:** Demands also include the creation of distinct Lok Sabha constituencies for Leh and Kargil districts.
*   **Ladakh Public Service Commission (LPSC):** Establishing an independent LPSC is another key request to ensure local representation in government employment.

The discussions with the Home Ministry follow the formation of a High-Powered Committee in December 2022, tasked with addressing these long-standing concerns. The committee's mandate includes exploring measures to protect Ladakh's culture, language, land, and employment opportunities, in addition to assessing strategies for inclusive development and empowering the Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils (LAHDCs) of Leh and Kargil.

The promise of a constitutional framework comes as the region approaches the first anniversary of an incident in February 2023. During a protest related to these demands, two individuals reportedly died, intensifying calls for governmental action. The ongoing engagement between the Union Home Ministry and Ladakh's representative bodies reflects an effort to find a mutually acceptable resolution to the region's constitutional future.

While specific details of the proposed Article 371-like framework are yet to be finalized and publicly disclosed, the commitment signals a willingness by the government to address the unique concerns of Ladakh. Further meetings and detailed discussions are anticipated as both parties work towards developing a comprehensive and tailored constitutional arrangement. The implementation timeline and exact provisions will likely emerge from these continued deliberations.
