India's External Affairs Minister, S. Jaishankar, recently provided a direct and unequivocal response to a Pakistani journalist during a press interaction, addressing the persistent issue of cross-border terrorism. The exchange occurred in a public forum where the minister was questioned about regional challenges, prompting a statement that underscored India's firm diplomatic position.

During the interaction, after a Pakistani journalist reportedly raised concerns about stability and a perceived lack of dialogue in the region, Jaishankar responded by stating, "A terrorist country is asking me." He then elaborated on India's perspective, highlighting the country's consistent stance against terrorism emanating from Pakistan and emphasizing the impact of such activities on bilateral relations and regional stability. The minister's remarks reiterated India's long-standing policy of non-engagement with countries perceived to be sponsoring or harboring terrorist groups until concrete actions are taken to dismantle their infrastructure.

This statement aligns with India's established diplomatic efforts to bring the issue of cross-border terrorism to the forefront of international discourse. India has consistently accused Pakistan of supporting and nurturing various militant organizations responsible for numerous attacks on Indian soil.

Key aspects of India's position include:
*   **Historical Context:** India has cited several significant terrorist attacks, including the 2008 Mumbai attacks, the 2016 Pathankot airbase attack, and the 2019 Pulwama attack, as evidence of Pakistan's alleged involvement in state-sponsored terrorism.
*   **International Forums:** India has regularly used platforms such as the United Nations General Assembly, the UN Security Council, and the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) to advocate for global action against terrorism and to highlight concerns regarding Pakistan's alleged failure to curb terror financing and operations within its borders.
*   **Bilateral Stance:** India maintains that dialogue and normalized relations with Pakistan cannot progress meaningfully as long as cross-border terrorism persists. This stance has led to a significant downturn in diplomatic engagement between the two nuclear-armed neighbors over recent years.

The minister's direct public address to the Pakistani journalist underscores India's unwavering commitment to its counter-terrorism agenda and its resolve to hold nations accountable for actions perceived to be destabilizing the region through proxy warfare. The incident reflects the ongoing diplomatic tensions and the significant chasm in perspectives between India and Pakistan regarding regional security and the issue of terrorism.

Looking ahead, India's diplomatic strategy is expected to continue focusing on international cooperation to combat terrorism and to press for tangible actions against terror networks. The exchange serves as a reiteration that the issue of terrorism remains a central impediment to any potential normalization of ties between India and Pakistan, and a priority for India's foreign policy objectives on global platforms.
