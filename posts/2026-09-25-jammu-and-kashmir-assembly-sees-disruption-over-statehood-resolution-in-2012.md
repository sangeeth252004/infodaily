On May 28, 2012, the Jammu and Kashmir Legislative Assembly witnessed significant disruption when members of the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) tore up a resolution introduced by then-Chief Minister Omar Abdullah. The resolution aimed to restore the original constitutional position of Jammu and Kashmir and sought a review of central laws extended to the state after 1953.

The resolution, moved by the National Conference leader, proposed the restoration of the "Sadr-e-Riyasat" (Head of State) and "Wazir-e-Azam" (Prime Minister) designations for the state, which were replaced with Governor and Chief Minister, respectively, in 1965. It further called for a comprehensive review of all central laws and constitutional provisions applied to Jammu and Kashmir following the 1953 events, arguing that these had eroded the state's special autonomous status under Article 370 of the Indian Constitution.

BJP members vehemently opposed the resolution, asserting that it challenged India's territorial integrity and sought to reverse the constitutional integration of Jammu and Kashmir. The opposition MLAs chanted slogans and tore copies of the resolution, leading to a heated exchange and procedural chaos within the assembly chamber. Their protest underscored a long-standing ideological divergence regarding the constitutional relationship between Jammu and Kashmir and the Indian Union.

Chief Minister Omar Abdullah, addressing the assembly, defended the resolution by emphasizing the historical context of Article 370 and the instrument of accession. He stated that the resolution sought to address the "erosion of autonomy" that had occurred over decades, maintaining that it was within the constitutional framework to discuss and seek to restore the state's original terms of accession.

The incident highlighted the deep political divisions concerning Jammu and Kashmir's unique constitutional status.

Key points of contention included:
*   **Restoration of Sadr-e-Riyasat and Wazir-e-Azam:** The resolution aimed to revert to pre-1965 constitutional roles, symbolizing greater autonomy.
*   **Review of Post-1953 Central Laws:** This aspect sought to examine the applicability of various central legislations in J&K, challenging their legitimacy from the perspective of state autonomy.
*   **Article 370:** The debate intrinsically revolved around the interpretation and implementation of Article 370, which granted special status to Jammu and Kashmir. BJP leaders voiced their consistent demand for the abrogation of Article 370, while the National Conference advocated for its preservation and restoration of its original scope.

The legislative session ultimately saw the resolution face significant opposition, reflecting the broader political landscape in India where the status of Jammu and Kashmir remained a subject of intense national debate. The disruption prevented smooth proceedings and underscored the persistent challenges in achieving consensus on the region's governance and constitutional future. The event became a notable moment in the ongoing discourse surrounding statehood, autonomy, and national integration within India.
