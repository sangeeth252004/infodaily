Congress leader Rahul Gandhi has publicly accused Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Amit Shah of orchestrating "match-fixing" in the upcoming general elections by allegedly influencing the appointment of the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC). Speaking at an event, Mr. Gandhi claimed the two senior leaders have effectively placed the CEC in charge of manipulating the electoral outcome, raising significant concerns about the impartiality of India's democratic process.

Mr. Gandhi's allegations draw attention to the legislative changes introduced last year concerning the appointment of election commissioners. The accusations underscore a contentious political debate regarding the independence of the Election Commission of India (ECI), a constitutional body responsible for overseeing free and fair elections across the nation. Such statements, made by a prominent opposition figure, introduce a layer of scrutiny over the ECI's autonomy as the country approaches national polls.

The basis for Mr. Gandhi's remarks appears to stem from the enactment of The Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Bill, 2023. This legislation, passed by Parliament in December 2023, significantly altered the selection process for the CEC and other Election Commissioners.

Key details of the new appointment process include:
*   **Previous System:** Prior to the 2023 Bill, a Supreme Court judgment in March 2023 had stipulated that the selection committee for Election Commissioners would comprise the Prime Minister, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, and the Chief Justice of India.
*   **New System (2023 Bill):** The new law replaced the Chief Justice of India with a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister. Consequently, the new selection committee consists of:
    *   The Prime Minister (Chairperson)
    *   A Union Cabinet Minister (nominated by the Prime Minister)
    *   The Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha (or the leader of the largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha)

Mr. Gandhi and other opposition leaders have consistently argued that this revised structure effectively grants the executive a majority in the selection committee, thereby diminishing the independence of the appointment process. They contend that by removing the Chief Justice of India and replacing them with a government nominee, the ruling party gains undue influence over who is appointed to these critical electoral posts. This perceived shift, according to Mr. Gandhi, creates an environment where the CEC could be perceived as beholden to the government, thus facilitating what he termed "match-fixing" of elections.

The ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) and the Election Commission of India have not yet issued a detailed response to these specific allegations from Mr. Gandhi at the time of reporting. However, the government has previously defended the 2023 Bill, stating it brings clarity and a structured process to the appointments. The ECI has consistently maintained its commitment to upholding the integrity and impartiality of the electoral process. The accusations are anticipated to intensify the political discourse surrounding electoral reforms and the ECI's independence as India gears up for the general elections.
