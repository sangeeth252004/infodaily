Indian aviation authorities have initiated a comprehensive review of existing security Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and measures across the nation's airports. This re-evaluation follows an incident on July 23, 2024, involving a flydubai flight operating from Dubai to Indore, which was diverted to Jaipur due to a security alert. The objective of the ongoing review is to enhance and fortify aviation security protocols, addressing potential vulnerabilities highlighted by the event.

The incident involved flydubai flight FZ 435, which departed from Dubai International Airport (DXB) en route to Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport (IDR) in Indore. During the flight, reports emerged of a suspicious object on board, prompting the pilot to divert the aircraft to Jaipur International Airport (JAI) for an emergency landing. Upon landing, all 185 passengers and crew were safely deplaned, and a thorough security sweep of the aircraft was conducted by local security agencies, including specialized bomb disposal squads. Initial reports indicated that no explosive material or threat device was ultimately found.

However, the event immediately prompted a high-level response from India's Ministry of Civil Aviation. Key agencies, including the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS), responsible for laying down standards and measures for security of civil flights at international and domestic airports in India, and the Central Industrial Security Force (CISF), which provides security cover at most Indian airports, are actively involved in the review process. Officials have stated that the incident, regardless of the ultimate finding, served as a critical opportunity to reassess the robustness of current security frameworks.

The review is expected to encompass a wide array of aviation security aspects. This includes, but is not limited to, the efficacy of pre-flight security checks, baggage screening protocols for both international and domestic flights, passenger frisking procedures, and the overall coordination mechanisms between various security agencies operating at airports. India's rapidly expanding aviation sector, projected to become the third-largest globally by 2024, necessitates the maintenance of stringent and adaptive security standards.

Specific areas of scrutiny during this review reportedly include:

*   **International Flight Protocols:** An in-depth examination of security procedures for flights originating from international destinations, particularly concerning baggage and cargo screening.
*   **Passenger and Cabin Crew Screening:** Evaluation of current frisking techniques, use of advanced screening technologies, and potential enhancements for identifying prohibited items.
*   **Aircraft Security Checks:** Review of pre-departure aircraft search procedures to ensure no unauthorized items are onboard prior to flight.
*   **Inter-Agency Coordination:** Assessment of communication channels and emergency response protocols among airlines, airport operators, BCAS, CISF, and local law enforcement during security alerts.
*   **Personnel Training:** Evaluation of ongoing training programs for security staff and their preparedness for diverse threat scenarios.

Upon the completion of this comprehensive review, revised security SOPs and guidelines are anticipated to be formulated and disseminated to all airports, airlines, and relevant security stakeholders across India. These enhancements aim to bolster the country's aviation security posture, ensuring the continued safety and confidence of passengers and operational integrity within Indian airspace. Further announcements regarding specific policy changes or implementation timelines are expected as the inter-agency review progresses in the coming weeks.
