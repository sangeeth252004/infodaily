The World Health Organization (WHO) has released its "World Health Statistics Report 2024," which includes data for 2022, indicating that the average life expectancy in India has surpassed that of Pakistan while still lagging behind Bangladesh. The report provides a comparative overview of health outcomes and developmental progress across the South Asian subcontinent.

According to the WHO's findings, based on 2022 data, the average life expectancy at birth for Indians was recorded at 69.3 years. This places India ahead of Pakistan, which registered an average life expectancy of 67.2 years. Conversely, Bangladesh demonstrated a higher average, with its population living an estimated 71.9 years.

The "World Health Statistics Report 2024" provides the following specific life expectancy figures for the region:

*   **India:** 69.3 years
*   **Bangladesh:** 71.9 years
*   **Pakistan:** 67.2 years

For broader context, the report also detailed:

*   **Global Average Life Expectancy (2022):** 73.4 years
*   **WHO South-East Asia Region (SEARO) Average Life Expectancy (2022):** 70.4 years

These figures illustrate India's position above Pakistan but below Bangladesh, and both India and Pakistan are below the regional and global averages, with Bangladesh slightly surpassing the SEARO average.

The report highlights that significant gains in life expectancy across these South Asian nations are often linked to a reduction in infant mortality rates and improved access to essential health services. India, for instance, has observed a substantial increase in its life expectancy from approximately 58 years in 1990.

Despite these advancements, the WHO's findings underscore ongoing public health challenges. These include the rising prevalence of non-communicable diseases (NCDs), road accidents, and environmental factors, which continue to significantly impact overall health outcomes in the region.

The disparities and improvements noted in the WHO report emphasize the ongoing need for targeted public health interventions and policy development across South Asia. Governments and health organizations in the region are expected to continue focusing on strengthening healthcare infrastructure, addressing the rising burden of NCDs, and mitigating environmental health risks to further enhance life expectancy and overall population health in the coming years.
