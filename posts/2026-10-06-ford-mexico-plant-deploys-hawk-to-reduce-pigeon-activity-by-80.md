Ford Motor Company's Hermosillo Assembly Plant in Sonora, Mexico, has successfully mitigated a persistent pigeon issue by employing a trained hawk as part of its facility management strategy. This initiative, aimed at addressing operational and hygiene challenges posed by bird populations, has resulted in an 80% reduction in pigeon activity at the site. The program underscores an increasing trend in industrial settings towards non-lethal, environmentally conscious pest control methods.

The presence of large pigeon populations at the automotive manufacturing facility had created several operational challenges. Bird droppings frequently accumulated on finished vehicles, machinery, and in areas frequented by the plant's approximately 4,000 employees. This necessitated additional cleaning efforts and posed potential risks to product quality and workplace sanitation. Seeking an effective and sustainable solution, Ford opted for an avian deterrent program.

Key details of the initiative include:

*   **Avian Predator:** The program centers on a trained Harris's hawk named El Charro. Harris's hawks are known for their intelligence and social hunting behaviors, making them effective for deterrence.
*   **Methodology:** El Charro, accompanied by a professional handler, patrols designated areas of the plant. The hawk's mere presence acts as a natural deterrent, frightening pigeons away without harming them. Pigeons perceive the hawk as a predator and instinctively avoid the area.
*   **Factory Location:** The Hermosillo Assembly Plant is located in Sonora, Mexico, a significant manufacturing hub for Ford, producing models such as the Ford Maverick and Ford Bronco Sport.
*   **Impact on Workers:** The pigeon issue directly affected the working environment for approximately 4,000 employees, impacting areas from production lines to break rooms. Improved conditions contribute to a safer and cleaner workplace.
*   **Effectiveness:** Ford reported an 80% reduction in pigeon activity since the implementation of El Charro's patrols, demonstrating the method's efficacy compared to traditional pest control options like netting or chemical deterrents.

The adoption of a raptor for pest control aligns with a growing movement towards eco-friendly and humane solutions in facility management. Unlike methods that involve traps or chemicals, using a hawk provides a natural deterrent that does not harm the birds or introduce environmental contaminants. This approach minimizes disruption to the ecosystem while effectively addressing the pest problem.

Moving forward, the success at the Hermosillo plant may encourage similar implementations across other industrial facilities facing wildlife management challenges. The positive outcome demonstrates the practical application of biological pest control in complex manufacturing environments, potentially setting a precedent for sustainable facility maintenance practices within the automotive industry and beyond.
