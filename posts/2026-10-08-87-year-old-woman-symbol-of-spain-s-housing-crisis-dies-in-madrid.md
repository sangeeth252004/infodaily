Maria del Carmen Garcia, an 87-year-old woman widely known as Maricarmen, died on Sunday in Madrid, Spain. Her case had recently drawn national attention, making her a symbol of the country's ongoing housing crisis amidst a pending eviction from her long-term residence.

Maricarmen's situation garnered significant public and media focus after an eviction order was issued for her Madrid apartment. Her family had resided in the flat for approximately 80 years, under a rent-controlled contract that expired in 2021. Despite the expiration, Maricarmen continued to pay rent at her previous rate, a payment which the property owner reportedly did not accept. The apartment building is owned by Vauras Investment, a large investment fund.

The initial eviction date, set for May 14, 2024, was suspended following extensive public outcry and protests by housing rights activists. Her deteriorating health and advanced age further underscored the human impact of Spain's complex housing market, particularly for vulnerable populations. The case highlighted broader concerns regarding the challenges faced by elderly tenants and those with expiring rent-controlled agreements, often against corporate landlords.
