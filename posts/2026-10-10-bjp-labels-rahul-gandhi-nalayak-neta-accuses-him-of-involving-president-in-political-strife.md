The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) has launched a strong offensive against Congress leader Rahul Gandhi, labeling him a "'Nalayak neta'" (incompetent leader) and accusing him of deliberately involving President Droupadi Murmu in partisan political "street fights." The ruling party alleged that Gandhi’s actions and rhetoric were part of a broader attempt to create "anarchy" within India’s democratic framework.

The accusations escalated political tensions, with senior BJP figures expressing strong disapproval of Gandhi's alleged conduct. BJP national spokesperson Shehzad Poonawalla, addressing the media, stated that Gandhi was consistently disrespecting constitutional positions and institutions, particularly targeting the office of the President. The BJP's condemnation centers on what it perceives as an unwarranted politicization of the President’s role, a position traditionally held above direct party-political fray.

Key points of the BJP's criticism include:
*   **Disrespect for Constitutional Office:** The BJP emphasized that President Droupadi Murmu, as the head of state and the first tribal woman to hold the position, deserves utmost respect across the political spectrum. They contend that any attempt to draw her into political disputes undermines the dignity of her office.
*   **Allegations of Anarchy:** The term "anarchy" was used to describe the perceived intent behind Gandhi's actions, suggesting a deliberate effort to destabilize political discourse and create disorder.
*   **"Nalayak Neta" Label:** The BJP's use of the derogatory term "'Nalayak neta'" against Rahul Gandhi marks a significant escalation in their rhetoric, reflecting the intensity of their disapproval of his political approach.

Poonawalla further elaborated on the BJP’s stance, questioning Gandhi's understanding and respect for India's constitutional framework. He highlighted the sanctity of the President's office as a symbol of national unity and urged all political actors to uphold its dignity. The BJP spokesperson also pointed to a pattern of alleged disrespect from the Congress party towards the President, citing previous instances where Congress leaders have been criticized for their remarks concerning President Murmu.

The incident underscores the growing acrimony in Indian politics, where exchanges between ruling and opposition parties often transcend traditional parliamentary decorum. The BJP's strong reaction indicates a firm resolve to defend the sanctity of constitutional offices from what it views as unwarranted politicization by the opposition. The use of sharp, personal labels like "'Nalayak neta'" by a ruling party against a prominent opposition figure signals a continued hardening of political rhetoric.

This latest round of accusations is expected to fuel further debate on political language, the boundaries of criticism, and the respect accorded to constitutional institutions. As the political landscape continues to evolve, the discourse surrounding the President's office and the conduct of political leaders remains a focal point of public and media scrutiny. Further responses from the Congress party are anticipated as this political confrontation unfolds.
