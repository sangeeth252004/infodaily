Opposition parties in India have recently voiced significant apprehension regarding legislative developments concerning the administration of the National Capital Territory of Delhi. Citing concerns over the central government's enhanced control, prominent opposition figures have publicly stated that the prevailing situation bears resemblances to the 'Kashmir model' and represents a state akin to an 'Emergency' in democratic functioning. These statements follow the passage of key legislation affecting the powers of Delhi's elected government.

The core of the controversy centers around the Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi (Amendment) Bill, 2023, which was enacted into law. This legislation grants the Lieutenant Governor (LG) significant authority over transfers and postings of civil servants in Delhi, effectively overriding a Supreme Court judgment from May 2023. The apex court had previously ruled that the elected government of Delhi held administrative control over services, with exceptions for land, police, and public order, aligning with principles of federalism and the mandate of an elected government.

Critics, primarily from the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) which governs Delhi, alongside other opposition parties, argue that the new law systematically undermines the democratic mandate of an elected state government. They contend that by centralizing control over the bureaucracy under the LG, who is an appointee of the central government, the legislation renders the elected government ineffective and accountable only to the Centre rather than to the citizens of Delhi.

The comparison to the 'Kashmir model' draws parallels with the governance structure of Jammu and Kashmir, particularly after the abrogation of Article 370 in 2019, which led to significant changes in its autonomous status and administrative control being largely vested with the central government. Opposition leaders have utilized this term to imply an erosion of local self-governance and an overreach of central authority into state affairs.

Furthermore, the declaration that the situation is "akin to an Emergency" evokes a period in India's history when civil liberties were suspended and powers were highly concentrated. This strong rhetoric is employed to underscore a perceived threat to democratic principles, constitutional balance, and the federal structure of the Indian Union. Opposition leaders assert that such legislative actions could set a precedent for weakening elected state governments across the country.

*   **Key Legislative Context:**
    *   **Government of National Capital Territory of Delhi (Amendment) Bill, 2023:** Passed by Parliament, it establishes a National Capital Civil Service Authority (NCCSA) for recommendations on transfers and postings of Group 'A' officers. The LG's decision is final in cases of disagreement.
    *   **Supreme Court's May 2023 Verdict:** Ruled that the Delhi government had legislative and executive power over administrative services, excluding land, police, and public order.
    *   **Ordinance Preceding the Bill:** An ordinance, promulgated in May 2023, effectively negated the Supreme Court's judgment, which was subsequently replaced by the Bill.

The central government has defended the legislation, stating it is necessary to ensure efficient and corruption-free administration in the capital, given its unique status as the seat of the national government. They argue that the bill clarifies ambiguities in governance and maintains the balance of power essential for the National Capital Region.

As the legislative process concludes, the political debate surrounding Delhi's administrative control is expected to continue. Opposition parties have indicated potential legal challenges to the new law, aiming to uphold what they describe as the constitutional powers of an elected state government. The long-term implications for Delhi's governance, federal relations, and the interplay between elected state bodies and centrally appointed authorities will remain a significant point of national political discourse.
