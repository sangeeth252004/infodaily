Kathmandu, Nepal – The Nepali government has approved a new policy to significantly reduce the waiting period for declaring individuals missing from the 2014 Sunkoshi River landslide as legally deceased. The decision, announced recently, will allow the families of 116 long-missing persons to have their loved ones officially declared dead after a 45-day public notice period. This measure aims to alleviate the protracted legal and administrative challenges faced by affected families for nearly a decade.

Previously, families of disaster victims often endured lengthy waits, sometimes exceeding traditional legal presumptions of death, before they could formally manage their deceased relatives' estates, claim insurance, or access government compensation. The absence of an expedited mechanism for mass disaster victims meant that families of the 2014 Sunkoshi River disaster had been in a state of limbo, unable to obtain essential documentation. This new directive from the Council of Ministers, issued under existing legal frameworks, provides a streamlined pathway to resolve their legal uncertainties.

The Sunkoshi River landslide, which occurred on August 2, 2014, in the Sindhupalchowk district, was a major natural disaster. The massive landslide blocked the Sunkoshi River, creating a large artificial lake, and swept away numerous homes and infrastructure. The incident led to the disappearance of 153 individuals. While 37 bodies were recovered in the aftermath, 116 people remained unaccounted for, leaving their families in prolonged uncertainty.

The inability to obtain a death certificate for a missing family member has created substantial difficulties for survivors. Without official documentation, families were unable to access critical financial support, including life insurance benefits, property inheritance, bank account transfers, and government-mandated relief packages. This prolonged uncertainty also impacted other legal and social aspects of family life, such as remarriage for spouses and the legal guardianship of children.

Under the new government procedure for declaring missing individuals as deceased:
*   **Family Application:** Relatives of the missing individuals must submit an application to their respective local government bodies.
*   **Local Verification:** The local body is mandated to conduct an investigation to verify the identity of the missing person and confirm their disappearance during the August 2014 Sunkoshi landslide event.
*   **Public Notice Period:** Following verification, a 45-day public notice will be issued. This notice invites any individual with verifiable information about the missing person to come forward within the specified timeframe.
*   **Official Declaration:** If no credible information leading to the missing person's reappearance is received and substantiated within the 45-day public notice period, the local body will proceed with the official declaration of death and issue the necessary certificate to the applicant.

This governmental action has been widely anticipated by the affected communities in Sindhupalchowk and other regions impacted by the 2014 disaster. It is expected to provide both closure and practical relief to families who have navigated complex bureaucratic systems while coping with the emotional toll of their loss for nearly a decade. The policy acknowledges the unique circumstances of disaster-related disappearances and aims to streamline administrative processes for victims' families. Further details regarding the commencement of applications under this new policy are expected to be released by authorities.
