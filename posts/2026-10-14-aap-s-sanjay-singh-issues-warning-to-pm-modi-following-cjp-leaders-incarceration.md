Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) leader Sanjay Singh issued a pointed warning to Prime Minister Narendra Modi, stating, "Don't mess with youth," following the recent incarceration of leaders from the Chhatra Yuva Sangharsh Samiti (CJP). The statement, made publicly, underscores escalating political tensions related to actions involving youth political figures in India.

Mr. Singh's remarks specifically addressed the jailing of CJP leaders, an event that appears to have catalyzed the strong condemnation from the AAP. While specific details surrounding the charges or circumstances of the CJP leaders' arrests were not immediately detailed in Mr. Singh's public address, his warning suggests a perceived challenge to the younger demographic's involvement in political processes. The AAP has often positioned itself as a party advocating for youth issues and their active participation in the nation's democratic framework.

The warning from a prominent AAP figure like Sanjay Singh carries significance within the current political landscape. It highlights a critical area of contention between the ruling party and opposition, specifically concerning the treatment and engagement of India's substantial youth population. Such statements often aim to mobilize public opinion and draw attention to government actions that are viewed as potentially suppressive or unjust towards younger generations.

Key aspects of this development include:
*   **Direct Confrontation:** Mr. Singh's warning was explicitly directed at Prime Minister Modi, signifying a high-level political challenge rather than a general critique.
*   **Youth Focus:** The phrase "Don't mess with youth" directly appeals to and warns on behalf of the country's youth, a crucial voter base and demographic for any political party.
*   **Catalyst Event:** The jailing of CJP leaders is presented as the immediate trigger for this public warning, indicating that the incident is viewed by the AAP as a significant act warranting such a strong response.
*   **Political Context:** The Aam Aadmi Party, an opposition force, frequently engages in debates with the central government over governance, civil liberties, and the rights of specific demographic groups.

This development follows a period of heightened political activity and debate across various sectors of Indian society. The jailing of political activists or youth leaders often ignites broader discussions about freedom of expression, political dissent, and the state's response to opposition movements. Mr. Singh's statement therefore places the issue of youth engagement and the handling of political opposition firmly on the national agenda, implying potential repercussions if the concerns raised are not addressed. The warning suggests that the AAP intends to continue to advocate for these specific leaders and, by extension, for the broader principle of protecting youth involvement in the political sphere.
