Odisha Governor Raghubar Das has ignited a political controversy with his recent remark stating he would "work for BJP." The statement, reportedly made while addressing party workers in the Mayurbhanj district, has drawn immediate and widespread criticism from opposition parties in the state, who allege a breach of the constitutional neutrality expected from the gubernatorial office.

The role of a Governor in India is constitutionally defined as a nominal head of the state, expected to function impartially and above party politics. This position, appointed by the President of India, acts as a bridge between the state and central governments, upholding the Constitution. Critics argue that a public declaration of allegiance to a specific political party, particularly the ruling party at the Centre, directly undermines the integrity and impartiality inherent to the Governor's office. Such remarks raise questions about the Governor's ability to act without bias, especially in a state governed by a different political party.

Following Governor Das's statement, various political parties in Odisha have voiced their objections and demanded action from higher authorities.

*   **Biju Janata Dal (BJD):** The ruling party in Odisha expressed "shock" over the remark. Senior BJD leader and spokesperson, Sasmit Patra, characterized the statement as "unfortunate" and contradictory to the constitutional mandate of the Governor. Patra urged the President of India to take appropriate action, emphasizing the need to uphold the sanctity of the gubernatorial post.
*   **Indian National Congress:** The principal opposition party in Odisha called for the immediate recall of Governor Das. State Congress President Sharat Pattanayak questioned the Governor's impartiality, stating that the remark confirmed their long-held suspicions about his political leanings. Pattanayak highlighted that a Governor is meant to represent the Constitution, not a political party.
*   **Communist Party of India (CPI):** The CPI also condemned Governor Das's statement. CPI state secretary, Ramakrushna Panda, described the comment as "unacceptable" and called for the President's intervention, stressing that Governors must maintain a non-partisan stance.

The controversy centers on the constitutional provisions that mandate the Governor to preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution and the law, as outlined in Article 159 of the Indian Constitution. Political commentators note that public expressions of party affiliation by an incumbent Governor can erode public trust in constitutional institutions and potentially lead to conflicts of interest, especially in politically sensitive scenarios.

As the political debate continues, the focus remains on potential actions by the President of India, who holds the authority to appoint and remove Governors. Whether any official clarification will be issued by the Governor's office in response to the criticism, or if the central government will address the concerns raised by opposition parties, remains to be seen. The incident has reignited discussions on the political neutrality and constitutional role of Governors within India's federal structure.
