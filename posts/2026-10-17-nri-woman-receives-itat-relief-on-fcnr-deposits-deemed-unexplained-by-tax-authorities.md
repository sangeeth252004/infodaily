The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Mumbai, has recently provided significant relief to a Dubai-based Non-Resident Indian (NRI) woman, overturning a tax notice issued by the Income Tax Department (ITD) concerning her Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits. The notice had initially classified her investment of 4,68,96,000 Japanese Yen, equivalent to approximately Rs 3.41 crore at the time, as unexplained funds for Assessment Year (AY) 2011-12.

The case originated when the assessing officer (AO) of the Income Tax Department issued a notice under Section 148 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, alleging that the substantial deposits made by the NRI woman and her husband in a joint FCNR account were unexplained cash credits. The AO subsequently added the entire amount to her taxable income, treating it as undisclosed income under Section 68 of the Act. This assessment was made despite the financial institution involved having provided documentation confirming the foreign remittance source of these funds.

A central point of contention in the dispute was the assessing officer's interpretation of the foreign currency amount. The deposits were originally made in Japanese Yen. The ITAT noted that the AO had incorrectly treated the figure of 4,68,96,000 Japanese Yen as if it were an equivalent amount in Indian Rupees, leading to a significant overestimation of the alleged unexplained income. This misinterpretation formed the basis of the tax demand, suggesting the funds were not legitimate foreign earnings.

Upon appeal, the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal meticulously reviewed the evidence and the assessment process. The ITAT observed that the bank had already furnished a certificate verifying the remittance of the foreign currency from abroad by both the NRI woman and her husband. The tribunal concluded that the assessing officer had failed in due diligence by not properly considering the bank's confirmation regarding the source of funds and had erred fundamentally in the conversion and treatment of the foreign currency deposit. Consequently, the ITAT ruled in favor of the taxpayer, removing the additions made to her income.

Key details of the case include:

*   **Taxpayer:** A Dubai-based Non-Resident Indian (NRI) woman.
*   **Assessment Year:** 2011-12.
*   **Deposit Type:** Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposit.
*   **Original Amount:** 4,68,96,000 Japanese Yen.
*   **Indian Rupee Equivalent:** Approximately Rs 3.41 crore.
*   **Issuing Authority:** Income Tax Department (Assessing Officer).
*   **Relief Granted By:** Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Mumbai.
*   **Core Issue:** Assessing Officer's misinterpretation of Japanese Yen amount as Indian Rupees and failure to accept bank-confirmed foreign remittance.

This ruling by the ITAT provides an important precedent for Non-Resident Indians managing their finances, particularly those utilizing FCNR accounts. It underscores the necessity for tax authorities to conduct thorough verification of documented foreign currency transactions and to accurately assess the nature and source of such deposits. The decision reinforces the protection for legitimate overseas earnings transferred to India and highlights the appellate channels available to taxpayers against potentially erroneous assessments.
