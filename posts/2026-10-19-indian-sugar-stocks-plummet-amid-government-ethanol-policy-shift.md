MUMBAI – Shares of major Indian sugar producers experienced a significant decline in recent trading sessions, with Bajaj Hindusthan Sugar Ltd. and Shree Renuka Sugars Ltd. leading the downturn, plummeting by up to 5% each. The broader sugar sector also saw substantial selling pressure following recent government directives impacting the use of sugarcane juice for ethanol production, a key diversification strategy for many mills.

The market reaction, observed on [Insert a recent date, e.g., Tuesday, December 5, 2023], stemmed from an announcement by the Indian government's Ministry of Food and Public Distribution. The directive instructed sugar mills to cease or significantly restrict the diversion of sugarcane juice and syrup for ethanol production, effective immediately. This measure is primarily aimed at ensuring sufficient domestic sugar availability and mitigating potential price increases, particularly in light of anticipated lower sugarcane yields due to erratic weather patterns.

Sugar companies had increasingly relied on ethanol production from sugarcane juice and syrup as a stable revenue stream and a way to manage sugar surpluses. The government’s Ethanol Blending Program (EBP) set ambitious targets, including achieving E20 (20% ethanol blending in petrol) by 2025. This policy had encouraged significant investment by sugar mills into ethanol distilleries, making the latest directive a substantial shift in operational strategy and expected profitability.

Key implications of the government's decision for the sugar industry include:

*   **Impact on Ethanol Production Mix:** Sugar mills will now primarily rely on B-heavy and C-heavy molasses for ethanol production. Producing ethanol from sugarcane juice and syrup is generally more efficient and profitable than from molasses. This shift is expected to impact the overall volume and cost-effectiveness of ethanol output.
*   **Revenue Diversification:** The directive directly affects the revenue diversification plans of sugar companies. Many had projected higher earnings from ethanol sales, particularly from the more lucrative sugarcane juice route. Reduced ethanol production from high-yield sources may necessitate a revision of their financial forecasts.
*   **Sugar Availability Focus:** The government's stated priority is to ensure adequate sugar supply for domestic consumption. Concerns about potential El Niño impacts on agricultural output and the need to maintain price stability for consumers are understood to be driving the policy.
*   **Stock Market Volatility:** The immediate market response reflects investor concerns regarding the sudden policy change and its potential impact on the profitability and growth prospects of the sugar sector.

Companies like Bajaj Hindusthan Sugar and Shree Renuka Sugars, which have invested heavily in ethanol production capacities, are particularly exposed to such policy shifts.
