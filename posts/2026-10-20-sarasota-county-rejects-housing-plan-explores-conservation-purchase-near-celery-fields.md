Sarasota County Commissioners recently voted to reject a proposed housing development near the popular Celery Fields and authorized staff to explore the acquisition of the 51-acre parcel for conservation purposes. The decision marks a shift from potential residential expansion to environmental preservation for a significant tract of land adjacent to a key ecological and recreational area in the county.

The action came after a developer, whose identity was not specified in public records relating to this specific decision, had initially proposed the construction of 864 apartments on the 51-acre site. Following public feedback and negotiations, the developer then offered a revised settlement plan to build 126 single-family homes on the same parcel. However, commissioners ultimately voted against the residential development, signaling a preference for maintaining open space in the rapidly developing region.

The land in question is strategically located near Sarasota's Celery Fields, a 360-acre stormwater treatment facility that has also become a renowned nature preserve and recreational hub. Managed by Sarasota County, the Celery Fields site is popular for birdwatching, walking, and biking, and plays a crucial role in regional flood control and water quality. Proximity to this environmental asset has made land use decisions in the surrounding areas particularly sensitive.

Key details of the commission's decision include:
*   **Proposed Development:** An initial plan for 864 apartments, later revised to 126 single-family homes.
*   **Land Area:** 51 acres, located adjacent to the Celery Fields.
*   **Commission Vote:** Rejection of the residential development proposal.
*   **Alternative Action:** Authorization for county staff to assess the feasibility and cost of acquiring the 51 acres for conservation.

The commission's vote to pursue a conservation purchase reflects ongoing discussions within the county regarding managed growth and the balance between development needs and environmental protection. As Sarasota County continues to experience population growth, land availability for both housing and conservation becomes a critical consideration for local governance. The decision underscores a commitment to protecting natural habitats and ensuring public access to green spaces, particularly around established environmental landmarks like the Celery Fields.

Following the vote, Sarasota County staff will now undertake due diligence, including property appraisals and environmental assessments, to determine the financial and logistical viability of acquiring the 51-acre parcel. This process will involve evaluating funding sources, potential costs to the taxpayer, and the long-term benefits of adding the land to the county's conservation holdings. Staff are expected to report their findings back to the County Commission for further discussion and a final decision on the potential purchase.
