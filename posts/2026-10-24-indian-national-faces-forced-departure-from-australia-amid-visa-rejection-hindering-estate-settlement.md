An Indian national, Meena Singh, is facing imminent forced departure from Australia following the rejection of her visa waiver application by the Department of Home Affairs (DHA). Ms. Singh, who arrived in Australia in November 2023, was seeking to remain in the country to settle the estate of her late brother, Sanjeev Singh, who passed away in Brisbane, Queensland. Her current visa status mandates her departure by Thursday, February 29, 2024.

Ms. Singh initially entered Australia on a Subclass 600 tourist visa, which expired on February 15, 2024. The purpose of her visit was to oversee her brother's funeral arrangements and manage his affairs. Upon the expiry of her visa, Ms. Singh applied for a waiver of the "no further stay" condition (Condition 8503), which would have allowed her to apply for a new visa onshore and extend her stay to complete the complex legal and administrative tasks associated with her brother's estate. This application was subsequently denied by Australian immigration authorities.

The rejection of the visa waiver has significant implications for the settlement of Sanjeev Singh's estate. Ms. Singh is the primary individual responsible for these matters, which include:

*   Accessing bank accounts and Superannuation funds.
*   Selling a property owned by her late brother.
*   Collecting various insurance policies.
*   Finalising other administrative and legal documents required for the estate.

Her forced departure prevents her from completing these critical tasks in person. Ms. Singh has publicly expressed the emotional toll of the situation, stating, "I'm on the brink of breaking down," due to the combined grief and bureaucratic challenges.

According to legal representatives assisting Ms. Singh, the "no further stay" condition is typically attached to certain temporary visas to ensure visitors depart Australia before their visa expires. While waivers can be granted under compelling and compassionate circumstances, particularly if a change in circumstances occurs after the visa grant, the DHA determined that Ms. Singh's situation did not meet the criteria for an exemption. Ms. Singh's legal team has highlighted that leaving Australia at this juncture would complicate the estate process significantly, potentially leading to the Public Trustee of Queensland managing the estate, a process that can incur substantial fees and delays.

As of now, Ms. Singh is scheduled to leave Australia by the stipulated deadline. Her legal and migration agents are reviewing all available options, including the possibility of further appeals or seeking alternative solutions for managing her brother's estate remotely or through local representation. The full impact of her departure on the timely and efficient settlement of the estate remains a key concern.
