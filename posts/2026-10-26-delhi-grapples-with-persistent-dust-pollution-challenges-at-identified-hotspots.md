Delhi, India's capital, continues to contend with significant dust pollution, a primary contributor to its deteriorating air quality, particularly at identified hotspots across the city. Despite the implementation of stringent measures by government agencies, compliance challenges persist, making sustained improvements difficult in these critical zones.

Dust from construction and demolition (C&D) activities, unpaved roads, and vehicular movement is recognized as a major source of particulate matter in Delhi's ambient air. These microscopic particles can exacerbate respiratory illnesses and other health conditions. The Delhi government, through agencies such as the Delhi Pollution Control Committee (DPCC), Public Works Department (PWD), and Municipal Corporations (MCD), has deployed various strategies to combat this issue.

Measures include the mandatory use of anti-smog guns and water sprinklers at large construction sites, regular mechanical road sweeping, and the proper covering of construction materials and debris. Specific areas consistently registering high dust levels, termed 'hotspots,' include ITO, Anand Vihar, Jahangirpuri, and Wazirpur. These locations often see a convergence of dense traffic, extensive infrastructure projects, and commercial activity, contributing to increased dust generation.

A key impediment to effective dust control remains the insufficient adherence to environmental guidelines by various construction and infrastructure agencies. Issues observed by monitoring teams include uncovered building materials, inadequate water sprinkling, and the improper disposal of construction and demolition waste. The re-suspension of dust from unpaved road shoulders and heavy traffic movement further complicates mitigation efforts, even on frequently swept roads.

Supporting details on actions and challenges include:
*   **Enforcement Actions:** The DPCC inspected over 1,700 construction sites in October alone, imposing fines totaling approximately Rs 1.5 crore for violations of dust control norms.
*   **Technological Interventions:** Anti-smog guns, which atomize water into fine mist, and water sprinklers are mandated at construction sites larger than 500 square meters to suppress dust.
*   **Road Maintenance:** Mechanical road sweepers operate across major arterial roads, complemented by water sprinkling to settle surface dust and prevent its re-suspension.
*   **Waste Management:** Ongoing efforts aim to ensure the proper processing of C&D waste at designated facilities to prevent open dumping, which is a significant source of dust.

Looking ahead, authorities emphasize continued rigorous enforcement and greater inter-agency coordination as crucial for long-term success. The government's Green War Room monitors pollution levels and compliance, facilitating real-time responses to identified violations. Delhi's ongoing campaign against dust pollution underscores a sustained commitment to improving air quality, though addressing the persistent challenges at critical hotspots requires continuous adaptation and stricter oversight.
