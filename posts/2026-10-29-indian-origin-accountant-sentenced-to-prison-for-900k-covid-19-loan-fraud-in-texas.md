An Indian-origin accountant, Sunil Mehta, 57, has been sentenced to 30 months in federal prison in Texas for orchestrating a $900,000 fraud involving COVID-19 relief loans. Mehta, notably the author of a book titled "Black Money and India: An Enquiry into its Nature, Growth and Management," was also ordered to pay $900,000 in restitution.

The sentencing follows Mehta's conviction for fraudulently obtaining funds from the Paycheck Protection Program (PPP), a government initiative designed to provide economic relief to small businesses during the pandemic. Investigations revealed that Mehta submitted false applications for loans through two entities, "Sunil Mehta & Co" and "Black Money LLC."

*   **Fraudulent Applications:** Mehta's scheme involved submitting altered bank statements, inaccurate employee numbers, and false payroll documentation to secure the PPP loans.
*   **Entities Involved:** Loans were sought under the names of "Sunil Mehta & Co," his accounting practice, and "Black Money LLC," a company name that starkly contrasts with his authored work.
*   **Loan Amount:** The total amount obtained through these fraudulent applications was $900,000, intended for payroll and operational costs.

The case was investigated by the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration (TIGTA). Special Agent in Charge Michael E. Davis commented on the case, stating, "I can only imagine his shock when federal agents showed up at his office." This remark underscores the investigative efforts that led to Mehta's apprehension and subsequent conviction.

The incident highlights the ongoing efforts by federal authorities to combat fraud related to pandemic relief programs. The PPP, designed to support businesses and retain jobs during economic uncertainty, became a target for fraudulent activities, leading to numerous prosecutions across the United States. Mehta's background as an author on financial transparency and illicit funds adds a layer of irony to his conviction for financial deceit.

Following his prison term, Mehta will also face a period of supervised release, during which he will be monitored by federal authorities. The restitution order mandates that he repays the full $900,000 defrauded from the program. This sentencing serves as a clear indication of the severe penalties for those found to have misused federal aid programs. Federal agencies continue to pursue individuals and entities that engaged in such fraudulent schemes.
