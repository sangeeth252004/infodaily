Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi recently called for the removal of Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar during an "anti-CEC rally," asserting that Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah would subsequently face similar demands. Mr. Gandhi's remarks were made as part of a broader protest, organized by opposition parties, expressing dissatisfaction with the functioning and appointments within the Election Commission of India.

The rally served as a platform for the opposition to voice its concerns over what it describes as a perceived erosion of institutional independence, particularly regarding the Election Commission. Mr. Gandhi specifically questioned the impartiality and decision-making processes within the electoral body, accusing it of failing to ensure a level playing field in the country's democratic framework. His statements represent an escalation of political rhetoric against the government and its appointed officials.

Key points raised by Mr. Gandhi during his address included:
*   A direct demand for the removal of Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar. This demand was not accompanied by specific allegations of misconduct in the provided context, but was framed within a general criticism of the EC's current composition.
*   An assertion that Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah would be "next" in facing calls for their removal, indicating a systemic challenge to the current administration.
*   Criticism of the process for appointing Election Commissioners, specifically highlighting the recent exclusion of the Chief Justice of India from the selection panel, which has been a contentious issue between the government and the opposition. The current selection committee comprises the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister, and the Leader of the Opposition.

The Election Commission of India is a constitutional body responsible for administering elections in the country. The process for removing an Election Commissioner is stringent, requiring a resolution passed by both Houses of Parliament with a two-thirds majority, similar to the impeachment of a High Court or Supreme Court judge, on grounds of proven misbehavior or incapacity. Mr. Gandhi's call therefore points towards a significant political challenge rather than an immediate procedural possibility without parliamentary consensus.

These statements are anticipated to intensify the ongoing political debate surrounding the Election Commission's autonomy and the integrity of electoral processes in India. Opposition parties have consistently raised questions regarding the EC's decisions and appointments, particularly in the lead-up to significant elections. The government has, in turn, defended its appointments and the independence of the Election Commission. The demand by a prominent opposition leader to remove an Election Commissioner and subsequently target top government functionaries signals a prolonged period of political contention over institutional checks and balances. What specific actions, if any, the opposition plans to take following this rally remain a subject of political observation.
