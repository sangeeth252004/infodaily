The Indian Navy successfully concluded an extensive anti-piracy operation in the Arabian Sea, rescuing 17 crew members and apprehending 35 Somali pirates from the hijacked Maltese-flagged bulk carrier MV Ruen. The operation, which involved "calibrated non-kinetic measures" followed by decisive action, unfolded over approximately 40 hours some 2,600 km (1,400 nautical miles) off the Indian coast.

The rescue operation culminated on March 16, 2024, after the Indian Navy intercepted the MV Ruen, which had been hijacked on December 14, 2023. The vessel, originally carrying 18 crew members, had been seized near Yemen's Socotra island by Somali pirates. One injured crew member was evacuated by the Spanish warship ESPS Victoria on December 26, leaving 17 on board at the time of the Indian Navy's final intervention. The pirates aboard the MV Ruen, which had been converted into a pirate mothership, fired upon the Indian Navy warship INS Kolkata during the encounter, prompting a calibrated response.

The Indian Navy's approach focused initially on non-kinetic methods, including continuous surveillance, sustained pressure, and communication attempts to secure the safe surrender of the pirates and the release of the crew. When these measures were met with resistance and hostile fire, Indian Navy assets engaged the vessel, leading to the eventual surrender of all 35 pirates. The operation underscored the Indian Navy's commitment to maritime security and its capability to project power in the Indian Ocean Region.

Key details of the operation include:
*   **Vessel:** MV Ruen, a Maltese-flagged bulk carrier.
*   **Hijacking Date:** December 14, 2023, near Socotra, Yemen.
*   **Rescue Date:** March 16, 2024.
*   **Location:** Approximately 2,600 km (1,400 nautical miles) from the Indian coast in the Arabian Sea.
*   **Indian Navy Assets:** INS Kolkata, INS Subhadra, P8I maritime patrol aircraft, drones, and MARCOS (Marine Commandos).
*   **Crew Rescued:** 17 crew members, comprising Bulgarian, Angolan, and Myanmar nationals.
*   **Pirates Apprehended:** 35.

The successful mission highlights a resurgence of Somali piracy, which had seen a decline in recent years but has witnessed a spike in incidents in late 2023 and early 2024. This development poses a significant threat to global maritime trade routes, particularly through the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden, which are critical arteries for international commerce. The Indian Navy, as a Net Security Provider in the Indian Ocean Region, has significantly enhanced its anti-piracy patrols and maritime surveillance in response to these evolving threats.

Following their apprehension, the 35 pirates are expected to be brought to India for legal proceedings, consistent with established protocols for such maritime interventions. The rescued crew members will undergo medical examinations and debriefing before arrangements are made for their repatriation. The Indian Navy maintains its robust presence in the Arabian Sea and the Gulf of Aden to ensure the safety and security of merchant shipping against piracy and other maritime threats.
