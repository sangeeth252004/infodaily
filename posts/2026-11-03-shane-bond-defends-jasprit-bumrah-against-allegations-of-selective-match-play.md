Former New Zealand fast bowler and current Mumbai Indians bowling coach, Shane Bond, has publicly come to the defense of Indian pace spearhead Jasprit Bumrah, refuting claims that the bowler is selectively participating in matches. Bond, who has worked closely with Bumrah during their time at the Indian Premier League (IPL) franchise, described such accusations as "harsh," emphasizing the complex realities of elite athlete management.

Bond's comments arrive amidst ongoing scrutiny regarding Bumrah's workload and availability for international and domestic fixtures, a topic that has frequently been debated since the bowler's return from a significant back injury. Bumrah, a pivotal component of the Indian bowling attack across all three formats, has experienced periods on the sidelines due to injury, prompting discussions about his long-term fitness and strategic match selection.

Speaking on the matter, Bond reportedly highlighted the immense physical demands placed on fast bowlers, particularly those operating at Bumrah's elite level. He underscored that an athlete's career involves meticulous planning, rehabilitation, and workload management, especially following serious injuries like the stress fracture Bumrah sustained in his back. Bond’s perspective suggests that external criticisms might not fully account for the internal processes and medical advice guiding a player’s return and ongoing participation.

Jasprit Bumrah’s journey back to competitive cricket has been closely monitored. After a prolonged absence that saw him miss major tournaments, including the Asia Cup and the ICC Men's T20 World Cup in 2022, he made a impactful return to the Indian squad. His performances since rejoining the team have consistently demonstrated his value, reaffirming his status as one of the world’s premier fast bowlers capable of delivering crucial breakthroughs in all phases of a game. His ability to perform at high intensity across Tests, One Day Internationals, and Twenty20 Internationals makes his fitness a critical concern for Indian cricket.

The debate around managing star athletes’ workloads and preventing injuries is a perennial subject in modern professional sports. Bond’s intervention provides an informed perspective from someone directly involved in Bumrah's professional development and conditioning. His insights draw attention to the often-unseen challenges faced by top-tier players and the comprehensive support systems required to maintain their performance and longevity.

As Jasprit Bumrah continues his career at the pinnacle of international cricket, discussions surrounding his workload, fitness, and availability are expected to remain a significant topic. The proactive management of his physical condition will undoubtedly be a key priority for both his franchise and the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) to ensure his sustained contribution to the sport.
