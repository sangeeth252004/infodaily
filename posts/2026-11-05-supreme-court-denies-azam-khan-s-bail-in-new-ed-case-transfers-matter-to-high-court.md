NEW DELHI – The Supreme Court of India on Tuesday declined to grant bail to prominent politician Azam Khan in a new case filed by the Enforcement Directorate (ED), opting instead to transfer the matter to the Allahabad High Court for further consideration. This decision marks a significant development in the ongoing legal challenges faced by the Samajwadi Party leader.

Mr. Khan, a former Member of Parliament and a prominent figure in Uttar Pradesh politics, had approached the apex court seeking relief in connection with the fresh charges. The Enforcement Directorate, a specialized financial investigation agency in India, typically investigates cases related to money laundering under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and foreign exchange violations. While the specifics of the current ED case were not detailed in the court's brief order, it represents another legal hurdle for Mr. Khan.

The Supreme Court's directive mandates that the Allahabad High Court now review Mr. Khan's bail application. This procedural step often occurs when the apex court deems it appropriate for the primary examination of facts, evidence, and legal arguments to be conducted by a lower appellate court. Such transfers allow the High Court to thoroughly assess the merits of the case before any potential subsequent review by the Supreme Court.

Mr. Khan has been embroiled in numerous legal battles over recent years, encompassing a range of allegations including land encroachment, hate speech, and other alleged financial irregularities. His legal team will now prepare to present their arguments before the Allahabad High Court, seeking bail in the newly initiated ED proceedings. The denial of bail by the Supreme Court in this specific instance means Mr. Khan must pursue his relief through the established channels of the High Court.

This development ensures that the legal scrutiny surrounding Azam Khan continues, with the focus now shifting to the Allahabad High Court to decide on the bail plea in the fresh Enforcement Directorate case. The legal process is expected to unfold in the coming weeks as Mr. Khan's counsel presents their arguments and the High Court deliberates on the merits of the application.
