The Supreme Court of India on Monday, June 3, 2024, declined to suspend the 10-year rigorous imprisonment sentence handed to former Tehelka editor-in-chief Tarun Tejpal. The ruling pertains to the 2013 sexual assault case where Tejpal was convicted for the rape of a junior colleague during a professional event in Goa. This decision upholds the previous verdicts by the trial court and the Bombay High Court's Goa bench.

The incident dates back to November 2013, when Tejpal, then editor-in-chief of the investigative news magazine Tehelka, was accused by a junior female journalist of sexual assault inside an elevator at a five-star hotel during the magazine's annual "Thinkfest" event in Goa. Following the complaint, Tejpal stepped down from his position at Tehelka. The Goa Police subsequently registered a First Information Report (FIR) against him, leading to his arrest in November 2013. He was later granted bail.

After years of legal proceedings, a trial court in Goa convicted Tejpal on May 21, 2021. The court found him guilty under various sections of the Indian Penal Code, including 376(2)(k) (rape by a person in a position of authority), 354 (assault or criminal force to woman with intent to outrage her modesty), 354-A (sexual harassment), 341 (wrongful restraint), 342 (wrongful confinement), and 506(2) (criminal intimidation). The court sentenced him to 10 years of rigorous imprisonment and imposed a fine of ₹25 lakh.

Tejpal subsequently appealed his conviction and sentence to the Bombay High Court's Goa bench. In December 2023, the High Court dismissed his appeal, affirming the trial court's verdict and the imposed sentence. Following this dismissal, Tejpal approached the Supreme Court of India, seeking a suspension of his sentence during the pendency of his appeal against the conviction.

During the Supreme Court hearing on Monday, a bench comprising Justices Aniruddha Bose and Bela M. Trivedi heard the arguments presented by Tejpal's legal counsel. The defence argued for the suspension of the sentence, citing various grounds typically raised in such appeals, including potential health issues and challenges to the evidence presented during the trial. However, the bench refused to grant the requested relief, thereby ensuring that Tejpal continues to serve his rigorous imprisonment.

The Supreme Court's refusal marks a significant development in the decade-long legal battle. It reinforces the judicial affirmation of Tejpal's conviction and sentence. As per legal protocol, this ruling means the sentence remains in effect. While Tejpal's main appeal against the conviction itself is still pending before the Supreme Court, the immediate application for suspension of sentence has been denied, concluding this specific legal recourse at the apex court.
