The Indian National Congress (INC) has reported a substantial 75% decline in its total donations for the financial year 2022-23 (FY23), accumulating approximately Rs 131 crore. This figure represents the lowest funding received by the party in the last three years, according to financial disclosures. The significant reduction in contributions highlights a notable shift in the funding landscape for one of India's major political parties.

The reported donation total for FY23 stands in sharp contrast to the previous financial year. For FY22, the Congress party had received donations totaling around Rs 524 crore. This comparison underscores the magnitude of the 75% plunge witnessed in the latest reporting period. Furthermore, the FY23 collection of Rs 131 crore is also lower than the Rs 283 crore reported for FY21, cementing its position as the lowest in a three-year cycle. These figures are typically compiled from party submissions to the Election Commission of India (ECI) and analyzed by independent bodies such as the Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR).

The financial health of political parties is a critical component of democratic functioning, directly impacting their operational capabilities, outreach programs, and election campaigning efforts. A significant drop in donations can influence a party's ability to contest elections effectively, particularly in a large and diverse electoral landscape like India. Political funding sources generally include individual contributions, corporate donations, and electoral bonds.

Key details regarding the Congress party's donations:
*   **Financial Year 2022-23 (FY23):** Approximately Rs 131 crore
*   **Percentage Decline:** 75% compared to FY22
*   **Financial Year 2021-22 (FY22):** Approximately Rs 524 crore
*   **Financial Year 2020-21 (FY21):** Approximately Rs 283 crore
*   **Trend:** Consistently lower than the preceding two financial years.

The precise reasons behind such a sharp decline are often multi-faceted and can include shifts in donor confidence, changes in electoral prospects, economic factors, or evolving political strategies. Parties rely on these funds for a range of activities, including cadre maintenance, policy research, public relations, and large-scale election campaigns, especially with the impending general elections.

As India approaches the next general elections, the financial resources of political parties will remain a subject of close scrutiny. The substantial reduction in donations for the Indian National Congress could necessitate a re-evaluation of its fundraising strategies and operational budgeting. Regulatory bodies like the ECI continue to monitor political funding to ensure transparency and accountability in the democratic process. Future financial disclosures from political parties will provide further insight into the evolving trends of political funding in the country.
