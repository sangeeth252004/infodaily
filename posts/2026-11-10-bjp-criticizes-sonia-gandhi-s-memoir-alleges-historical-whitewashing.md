New Delhi, India – The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) has strongly criticized former Congress President Sonia Gandhi's recently released memoir, "An Ordinary Life: Belonging," accusing the autobiography of omitting crucial historical events and presenting a "whitewashed" account of her life and the Congress party's political tenure. The criticisms emerged shortly after the book's official launch, sparking immediate political debate.

BJP IT Cell chief Amit Malviya led the charge, stating that the memoir should have been titled "Whitewashing" instead of "Belonging." Mr. Malviya's remarks, made public following the book's release, questioned the absence of detailed accounts or personal reflections on controversial periods and decisions that occurred during the Congress party's governance.

*   **Book's Content:** "An Ordinary Life: Belonging" is co-authored by Sonia Gandhi and A.O. Scott. The memoir details Gandhi's journey from Italy to India, her marriage into the Nehru-Gandhi family, and her eventual reluctant entry into politics following the assassinations of her husband, Rajiv Gandhi, and mother-in-law, Indira Gandhi. It aims to offer a personal perspective on her experiences and influences.
*   **BJP's Allegations:** The BJP's primary contention is that the book selectively addresses historical events, glossing over or entirely omitting incidents that have drawn significant public and political scrutiny. Specific concerns raised include:
    *   The 1975 Emergency period, during which civil liberties were suspended.
    *   The Bofors scandal, a major corruption allegation that rocked the Rajiv Gandhi government in the late 1980s.
    *   The 2G spectrum allocation scam, which surfaced during the United Progressive Alliance (UPA) government led by the Congress.
    *   Allegations of dynastic politics and decision-making within the Congress party.

Mr. Malviya asserted that a comprehensive autobiography, particularly from a figure of Gandhi's stature, should confront all aspects of her political life and the era she influenced, rather than focusing solely on personal narratives or favorable recollections. He characterized the book as an attempt to "repackage" history, portraying a sanitized version of events for public consumption.

The release of "An Ordinary Life: Belonging" comes at a politically charged time in India, with general elections approaching in the coming months. Memoirs and autobiographies by prominent political figures often become subjects of intense scrutiny, particularly from rival parties, as they can shape public perception and influence political narratives. The Congress party has yet to issue a formal response to the BJP's specific allegations regarding the book's content, though party spokespersons have defended Gandhi's right to tell her story.

The discourse surrounding the memoir is expected to continue as both political factions utilize the book's release to articulate their respective viewpoints on India's recent history and the leadership roles played by their parties. The ongoing debate highlights the persistent tension and differing interpretations of historical events within India's political landscape.
