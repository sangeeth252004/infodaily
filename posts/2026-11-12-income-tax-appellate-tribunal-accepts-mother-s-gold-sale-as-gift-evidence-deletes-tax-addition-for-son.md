The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) has provided relief to a taxpayer by accepting evidence presented by his mother regarding a gift derived from the sale of ancestral gold jewellery. This ruling led to the deletion of tax additions totaling Rs 33.27 lakh that had been imposed by the Income Tax Department (ITD) concerning a property purchase and cash deposits made by the son.

The case originated when the taxpayer received notices from the ITD regarding the source of funds for a property acquisition valued at Rs 20.23 lakh and cash deposits amounting to Rs 13.04 lakh. The ITD had considered these amounts as unexplained income, leading to an addition to the taxpayer's taxable income during assessment proceedings. The taxpayer contended that the funds were a gift received from his mother.

To substantiate his claim, the taxpayer's mother provided detailed documentation to the appellate authorities. This evidence included an affidavit confirming the gift, bank statements tracing the proceeds from the sale of her ancestral gold jewellery, and a formal gift deed. The mother demonstrated that she had sold a significant portion of her long-held gold ornaments, which were part of her ancestral inheritance, and subsequently gifted the proceeds to her son. Furthermore, the mother presented evidence of her own independent financial capacity, including income from a family pension and agricultural land, demonstrating her ability to make such a gift.

The ITAT, after reviewing the submitted evidence, concluded that the mother had adequately explained the source of the funds used for the gift. The Tribunal highlighted that the documentation, including the gold sale proceeds deposited into the mother's bank account and the subsequent transfer to the son, corroborated the taxpayer's claim. The ITAT's decision underscores the importance of proper documentation for inter-family financial transactions and gifts, particularly when such transactions come under tax scrutiny.

*   **Key Figures:**
    *   Property purchase: Rs 20.23 lakh
    *   Cash deposits: Rs 13.04 lakh
    *   Total tax addition deleted: Rs 33.27 lakh

*   **Evidence Accepted:**
    *   Mother's affidavit
    *   Bank statements detailing gold sale proceeds
    *   Gift deed
    *   Evidence of mother's independent income sources (family pension, agricultural income)

The Tribunal's acceptance of the mother's explanation and supporting documents led to the deletion of the disputed tax additions, concluding the taxpayer's appeal. This ruling reinforces the legal precedent that gifts between family members, when adequately sourced and documented, are permissible and can stand up to scrutiny from tax authorities. It also emphasizes the role of appellate bodies like the ITAT in adjudicating disputes between taxpayers and the Income Tax Department based on presented evidence and legal principles.
