New Delhi – The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) on Monday, May 20, 2024, reiterated its position that visa issuance remains a sovereign matter for the Indian government. The statement came in response to reports that approximately 115 foreign speakers had been denied visas to attend the "Samvidhan Sammelan" (Constitutional Convention) event organized by the Indian National Congress.

MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal addressed the issue during a regular press briefing, stating, "Visa matters are sovereign." He emphasized that the government makes decisions regarding visa applications based on various factors, including the purpose of travel, specific event requirements, and national interests. This assertion underscored India's prerogative in regulating entry of foreign nationals into the country.

The "Samvidhan Sammelan," a two-day event held on May 20-21, 2024, in New Delhi, was organized by the Congress to discuss constitutional values and contemporary challenges. Reports from the Congress party indicated that invitations had been extended to a diverse group of international academics, activists, and parliamentarians from over 20 countries. However, a significant number of these invitees were reportedly unable to secure visas, leading to their absence from the convention.

Key details surrounding the situation include:

*   **Number of Speakers:** The Congress claimed approximately 115 foreign speakers were denied visas or did not receive them in time.
*   **Event Focus:** The "Samvidhan Sammelan" aimed to foster discussions on India's constitutional framework and democratic principles.
*   **Organizer:** The event was organized by the Indian National Congress, a prominent opposition political party.
*   **Government Stance:** The MEA maintained that each visa application is evaluated individually, and the decision to grant or deny a visa rests solely with the government.

The incident has drawn attention to the procedures for foreign participation in privately organized political or academic events in India. While the government consistently cites national security and sovereign rights as primary considerations in visa policy, opposition parties occasionally raise concerns about potential restrictions on international engagement.

As of now, the MEA has not provided specific reasons for the denial of individual visa applications for the Congress event, adhering to its policy of not commenting on specific visa cases. The Congress party has expressed disappointment over the perceived lack of cooperation from the government, arguing that international participation would have enriched the discourse at the convention. The situation highlights the ongoing dynamics between the government's immigration policies and the aspirations of political entities to engage with global counterparts. No further official statements or changes in visa policy are immediately anticipated following the MEA's definitive pronouncement on the matter.
