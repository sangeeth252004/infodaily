Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro and his wife, Lori Shapiro, are engaged in a legal dispute with their neighbors, Andrew and Jennifer Crompton, concerning an 8-foot security fence and approximately 2,900 square feet of land adjacent to the Governor's private residence in Montgomery County. Both parties have filed separate lawsuits, each asserting ownership of the disputed property and seeking legal remedies.

The core of the dispute revolves around a strip of land and an 8-foot security fence erected by the Shapiros. This construction occurred in 2023, following an arson attack on the Governor's official residence in Harrisburg earlier that year, which prompted heightened security measures at his private home. The Cromptons contend the fence encroaches upon their property, while the Shapiros maintain it is situated entirely on their land, which they claim through adverse possession and their property deed.

Key details of the competing legal actions include:

*   **Shapiros' Lawsuit:** Filed on May 30 in Pennsylvania's Commonwealth Court, the Shapiros' complaint seeks a declaratory judgment affirming their ownership of the 2,900-square-foot parcel. They further allege trespass by the Cromptons, citing landscaping elements, including a shed and playground equipment, that previously occupied portions of the disputed area. Their claim is supported by a 2023 property survey.
*   **Cromptons' Lawsuit:** Filed on June 4 in the Montgomery County Court of Common Pleas, the Cromptons' lawsuit alleges trespass by the Shapiros' fence. They contend the fence's 8-foot height violates local township zoning ordinances, which typically limit residential fences to six feet. The Cromptons' claim is supported by a 2011 property survey, which reportedly identifies the disputed land as belonging to them. They seek the removal of the fence and damages.

The Shapiros purchased their property in 2005, and the Cromptons acquired their adjacent property in 1999. Prior to the fence's construction, the disputed area was reportedly used by the Cromptons for various landscaping purposes, including the aforementioned shed and playground. The conflicting surveys, one from 2011 commissioned by the Cromptons and another from 2023 commissioned by the Shapiros, present differing conclusions regarding the precise property line, forming a central point of contention in the ongoing legal battle.

This property dispute involving a sitting governor underscores the complexities inherent in land ownership and boundary definitions, especially when personal security considerations for public officials intersect with private property rights. The legal proceedings are now underway, with both the Commonwealth Court and the Montgomery County Court slated to hear arguments and evidence to determine the rightful ownership of the land and the legality of the security fence. The outcomes will hinge on judicial interpretation of property deeds, survey results, and applicable zoning regulations.
