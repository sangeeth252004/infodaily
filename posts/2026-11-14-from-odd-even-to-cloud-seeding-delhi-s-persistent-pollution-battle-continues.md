New Delhi continues to grapple with severe air pollution, particularly during the winter months, prompting authorities to implement a range of measures from vehicular rationing to experimental atmospheric interventions. Despite sustained efforts over several years, including the "odd-even" road space rationalization scheme and recent proposals for cloud seeding, the National Capital Region (NCR) faces an ongoing challenge in achieving consistently healthy air quality levels. The multi-faceted nature of the pollution sources and the complexity of their mitigation contribute to the unfinished status of this critical environmental battle.

The annual pollution crisis typically escalates from October to February, driven by a confluence of factors including vehicular emissions, industrial discharge, construction dust, stubble burning in neighbouring states, and adverse meteorological conditions like low wind speeds and temperature inversions. During these periods, concentrations of fine particulate matter, specifically PM2.5, frequently exceed the World Health Organization's safe limits by significant margins, posing substantial health risks to millions of residents, including respiratory illnesses, cardiovascular problems, and reduced life expectancy. Public health advisories and emergency measures have become a recurring feature of the city's winter.

In an attempt to curb vehicular emissions, a major contributor to urban air pollution, the Delhi government introduced the "odd-even" scheme on multiple occasions. This initiative restricted private vehicles from operating on alternating days based on the last digit of their license plates.
*   **First phase:** Implemented from January 1 to January 15, 2016.
*   **Second phase:** Conducted from April 15 to April 30, 2016.
*   **Subsequent implementations:** Activated during severe pollution peaks, such as in November 2019, to alleviate emergency situations.
While the scheme aimed to reduce the number of cars on the road and subsequently emissions, its overall long-term impact on air quality has been subject to varied analyses, with some studies suggesting temporary relief while others pointed to challenges in achieving sustained improvements.

More recently, attention has shifted towards advanced technological solutions, with cloud seeding emerging as a proposed measure. Cloud seeding involves introducing substances like silver iodide or dry ice into clouds to induce precipitation, with the aim of washing away atmospheric pollutants.
*   **Proposal:** The Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) in Pune, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences, has been involved in feasibility studies and proposed trials for cloud seeding in Delhi.
*   **Objective:** To generate artificial rain during periods of high pollution, effectively cleaning the air.
*   **Current Status:** As of late 2023, trials were being considered or planned, subject to necessary environmental clearances and suitable meteorological conditions for effective cloud formation.
The efficacy of cloud seeding in dense urban environments specifically for pollution mitigation, and the logistical challenges associated with its deployment, remain subjects of scientific research and debate.

Despite these varied interventions, including bans on specific industrial activities, restrictions on construction, and the implementation of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) which mandates escalating anti-pollution measures based on air quality levels, Delhi's air quality routinely deteriorates to "severe" or "hazardous" categories annually. The ongoing nature of the crisis underscores the need for a comprehensive, sustained, and regionally coordinated strategy that addresses all major pollution sources effectively. Further research and policy interventions are expected as authorities continue to seek lasting solutions to this critical public health and environmental challenge.
