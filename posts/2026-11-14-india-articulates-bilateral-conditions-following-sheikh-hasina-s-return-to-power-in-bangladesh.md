New Delhi has communicated two specific conditions to Dhaka, outlining its expectations for bilateral cooperation following Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina's recent return to power in Bangladesh. The conditions, which underscore India's strategic priorities in the region, are understood to center on enhanced security cooperation and the facilitation of regional connectivity projects. These developments come as both nations seek to maintain and strengthen their established diplomatic and economic ties.

The articulation of these conditions by India emphasizes the ongoing strategic importance New Delhi places on its relationship with Bangladesh. Following the general elections in Bangladesh in January 2024, which saw Prime Minister Hasina's Awami League secure another term, diplomatic channels have been active. India's external affairs minister and other senior officials have engaged with their Bangladeshi counterparts, reiterating the significance of a stable and cooperative bilateral framework for regional stability and prosperity.

The first condition, according to sources familiar with the discussions, relates to comprehensive security cooperation. India has reportedly sought renewed assurances and concrete actions from Dhaka regarding the prevention of cross-border illicit activities, including terrorism and insurgency. This includes intelligence sharing, coordinated border management, and ensuring that no hostile elements exploit Bangladeshi territory for activities detrimental to India's security interests. Both nations share a long, porous border, making robust security collaboration a cornerstone of their relationship. Past cooperation has yielded positive results in curbing extremist activities, and India is keen to maintain and expand this level of engagement.

The second condition focuses on accelerating regional connectivity initiatives. India has reportedly pressed for continued and expanded access to Bangladeshi infrastructure for transit and trade, specifically emphasizing waterways, railways, and roadways. Projects aimed at improving cross-border logistics, such as the operationalization of multi-modal transit routes and port usage agreements, are central to this demand. Enhanced connectivity is seen by India as vital for economic development in its landlocked northeastern states and for bolstering sub-regional trade under initiatives like the Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal (BBIN) Motor Vehicle Agreement. Facilitating seamless movement of goods and people is expected to unlock significant economic potential for both countries.

These articulated conditions are part of the ongoing diplomatic discourse between the two South Asian neighbors, reflecting India's consistent foreign policy objectives regarding its eastern frontier. While the specific details of Dhaka's response remain part of private diplomatic exchanges, the general trajectory of India-Bangladesh relations suggests a continued commitment to addressing mutual concerns through dialogue. Future engagements are expected to focus on mechanisms for implementing these conditions, ensuring that bilateral ties remain strong and mutually beneficial.
