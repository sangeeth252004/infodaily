India's Supreme Court on Wednesday, June 12, 2024, expressed strong dissatisfaction with the lack of progress in cleaning the Yamuna River, admonishing various administrative bodies for persistent pollution despite substantial financial outlays. The court highlighted that over 500 drains continue to discharge untreated wastewater directly into the river, rendering clean-up efforts ineffective.

During a hearing, a bench comprising Justices P.S. Narasimha and Prasanna B. Varale observed that approximately 520 drains flow into the Yamuna, releasing untreated sewage and industrial effluents. This continued discharge undermines investments and efforts aimed at river rejuvenation. The court underscored that despite directions issued in its 2020 judgment for a dedicated monitoring committee, the ground reality shows little improvement.

The Supreme Court noted that more than Rs 6,000 crore has been spent on Yamuna cleaning initiatives since 2020. However, this expenditure has not translated into tangible results, with the river's pollution levels remaining a serious concern. The court criticized the Delhi Jal Board and other responsible administrations for their inability to curb the direct flow of pollutants.

*   **Key Issues Identified by the Court:**
    *   **520 Drains:** A significant number of drains are still discharging untreated wastewater into the Yamuna.
    *   **Rs 6,000 Crore Spending:** Over Rs 6,000 crore has been allocated and spent on cleaning projects since 2020.
    *   **No Tangible Improvement:** The river's pollution status has not improved despite these substantial financial and administrative efforts.
    *   **Failure of Monitoring:** Previous directives for a dedicated monitoring committee following a 2020 judgment have not led to effective oversight or accountability.

The court's observations come amidst long-standing public health and environmental concerns surrounding the Yamuna, which serves as a vital water source for millions. The river flows through several states, including Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Delhi, making inter-state cooperation and coordinated administrative action critical for its revival. The Delhi Jal Board, as the primary water utility in the national capital, bears significant responsibility for sewage treatment and preventing pollutant discharge in its jurisdiction.

The Supreme Court has indicated that it will continue to monitor the situation closely. Further directions are anticipated, potentially focusing on stricter enforcement mechanisms, accountability for administrative failures, and concrete timelines for the treatment and diversion of wastewater from the remaining polluting drains. The ongoing judicial scrutiny aims to compel administrative bodies to implement effective measures to restore the ecological health of the Yamuna River.
