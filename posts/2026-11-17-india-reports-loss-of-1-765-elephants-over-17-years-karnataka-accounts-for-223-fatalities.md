New Delhi, India – Official data released by the Indian government indicates that a total of 1,765 elephants have died across the country over a 17-year period, spanning the financial years 2009-10 to 2023-24. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) presented these figures, highlighting the ongoing challenges in elephant conservation. Karnataka, a southern state known for its significant elephant population, recorded 223 of these fatalities, marking it as one of the states with a notable number of elephant deaths during this period.

The reported mortality figures underscore the pressures faced by India's elephant population, a species listed under Schedule I of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, granting it the highest level of protection. The data reflects a continuous struggle to safeguard these animals amidst increasing human-wildlife interface and various environmental factors. The long timeframe of the report provides a comprehensive overview of mortality trends and the persistent threats to elephant survival.

Analysis of the official report details several primary causes contributing to these elephant deaths. These causes frequently stem from interactions with human infrastructure and activities, as well as natural factors. Wildlife authorities and the MoEFCC have identified a range of incidents leading to these fatalities, often categorizing them to inform mitigation strategies.

Key causes of elephant mortality cited in government reports include:
*   **Electrocution:** Often resulting from illegal electric fences erected by farmers to protect crops, or sagging power lines.
*   **Train Accidents:** Collisions with trains, particularly in corridors that intersect elephant migratory routes.
*   **Poaching:** Despite stringent anti-poaching measures, incidents of elephants being killed for their ivory persist.
*   **Poisoning:** Retaliatory killings or accidental poisoning often linked to human-elephant conflict.
*   **Road Accidents:** Collisions with vehicles on national highways passing through forest areas.
*   **Natural Causes:** Including disease, old age, and conflicts between elephants.

In response to these challenges, the Indian government has implemented several initiatives aimed at reducing elephant mortality and promoting conservation. Project Elephant, launched in 1992, is a centrally sponsored scheme focused on protecting elephants, their habitats, and corridors. The project also addresses human-elephant conflict and the welfare of captive elephants. Efforts include habitat improvement, anti-poaching strategies, and the creation of elephant reserves and corridors. Furthermore, railway authorities have been instructed to implement measures such as speed restrictions in elephant zones and the construction of underpasses or overpasses to facilitate safe passage.

The release of this data serves as a factual account of elephant mortality trends in India. While conservation efforts continue, the reported figures highlight the persistent threats and the necessity for ongoing, coordinated actions by government agencies, local communities, and conservation organizations to ensure the long-term survival of India's wild elephant population. Monitoring of these trends is expected to continue, informing future policy and conservation strategies.
