NEW DELHI – The National Health Authority (NHA), the implementing agency for the Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY), has announced significant revisions to its Health Benefit Package (HBP) master. Effective November 1, 2023, the NHA has increased the rates for 23 existing medical procedures and introduced 112 new services under the flagship health insurance scheme. This initiative aims to enhance the financial viability of hospitals providing services under AB PM-JAY and improve access to quality healthcare for beneficiaries.

The revisions mark an average increase of approximately 20% in the package rates for the selected existing procedures. Some packages have seen substantially higher revisions, with increases up to 260% for specific treatments. The overall scheme encompasses more than 1,900 medical and surgical packages, covering a wide range of health conditions and treatments. These adjustments are a response to long-standing demands from healthcare stakeholders, including private sector providers, and are intended to ensure sustainable and quality treatment options.

Key aspects of the revised packages include:

*   **Average Rate Increase:** An approximate 20% average hike across 23 existing health benefit packages.
*   **Maximum Revision:** Certain packages have witnessed rate increases of up to 260%.
*   **New Procedures:** 112 new medical and surgical procedures have been incorporated into the scheme.
*   **Effective Date:** The revised rates and new packages are applicable from November 1, 2023.

Specific examples of the revised package rates illustrate the scale of the adjustment:

*   **Radiotherapy for Head & Neck Cancer:** The package rate has increased by 260%, from ₹8,000 to ₹28,800.
*   **Medical Management of Dengue:** The rate for treating dengue has seen a 50% increase, moving from ₹5,000 to ₹7,500.
*   **Appendectomy:** The package for appendectomy has been revised upwards by 33%, from ₹12,000 to ₹16,000.

These adjustments are projected to provide better reimbursement to hospitals, particularly public health facilities, encouraging them to offer a wider array of services and improve the quality of care. The NHA emphasized that a primary objective of this revision is to motivate public sector hospitals to participate more actively in the scheme, thereby strengthening the public health infrastructure and reducing out-of-pocket expenditure for eligible beneficiaries.

The enhancements are part of a continuous effort by the NHA to review and update the health benefit packages under AB PM-JAY. This process involves regular consultations with medical experts, state health agencies, and other stakeholders to ensure that the scheme remains responsive to healthcare needs and economic realities. The aim is to bridge the gap between package rates offered by public and private hospitals, promoting equitable access to treatment across the network.

The NHA will continue to monitor the implementation of these revised packages to assess their impact on healthcare delivery and beneficiary access. The ongoing evolution of the AB PM-JAY scheme reflects a commitment to providing comprehensive and affordable health coverage to vulnerable populations across India.
