New Delhi, India – India's Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) on Thursday, June 13, 2024, categorically denied recent reports suggesting it had sought permission from Nepal to utilize its airspace for military flights. MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal stated during a weekly press briefing in New Delhi, "No such proposal has been made by the government of India to the government of Nepal." The direct denial came in response to assertions that had circulated within sections of the Nepali media earlier in the week regarding an alleged formal request.

The reports, which gained traction within Nepali news outlets, had posited that India had approached Kathmandu for military overflight rights. Such claims carry significant weight due to the sensitive nature of national airspace sovereignty and bilateral relations between the two neighboring countries. Mr. Jaiswal's unequivocal statement aimed to clarify the Indian government's official position, directly refuting the speculative narratives that had sparked discussions in Nepal concerning security and diplomatic protocols. The spokesperson emphasized that India maintains regular, cordial engagement with Nepal on a spectrum of bilateral issues, but confirmed unequivocally that no military airspace request had been part of these diplomatic dialogues.

Concurrently, Nepal's Civil Aviation Authority (CAAN) also addressed the emerging reports. CAAN officials confirmed that while India had not submitted any request for the use of Nepali airspace for military purposes, discussions between the two nations regarding new flight routes for civilian aircraft were indeed ongoing. This distinction is crucial, serving to separate unsubstantiated reports about military access from confirmed bilateral talks focused on enhancing civil aviation connectivity and air traffic management.

The ongoing discussions for civilian flight routes include proposals for new air entry points, particularly over western Nepal. These routes are considered vital for facilitating more direct and efficient air traffic to Nepal's expanding international aviation infrastructure, such as the newly constructed Gautam Buddha International Airport in Bhairahawa. Enhancing these routes is seen as key to boosting tourism and economic development in Nepal. Negotiations concerning such civil aviation pathways have been a subject of bilateral dialogue for several years, aimed at improving air travel efficiency and safety for commercial flights. Historical context reveals continuous efforts, with previous agreements having led to the establishment of additional entry points for civilian aircraft over areas including Janakpur and Bhairahawa, underscoring the long-standing commitment to streamline regional air travel.

As of this report, discussions between India and Nepal regarding the expansion and optimization of civilian flight routes are expected to continue through established diplomatic channels. The categorical denial from both Indian and Nepalese authorities regarding military airspace requests serves to mitigate speculation and reinforce that the primary focus of current bilateral aviation dialogue remains firmly on enhancing civilian connectivity and mutual benefit for both nations.
