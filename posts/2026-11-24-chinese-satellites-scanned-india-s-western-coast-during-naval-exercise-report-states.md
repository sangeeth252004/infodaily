An Indian space surveillance company, Digantara, has reported that three Chinese Yaogan series satellites frequently scanned India's western coast in April 2024. The scans coincided with the Indian Navy's large-scale operational exercise, "Op Sindoor," according to Digantara's analysis.

The report, issued by Bengaluru-based Digantara, utilized its "Pathfinder" platform for space domain awareness to identify the specific Chinese satellites involved. The identified assets were Yaogan-34A, Yaogan-34B, and Yaogan-34C. These satellites, part of China's extensive Yaogan constellation, are widely understood to possess capabilities for advanced remote sensing, including ocean reconnaissance and electronic intelligence gathering.

According to Digantara's findings, the Yaogan-34 series satellites conducted frequent overflights of India's western seaboard. The analysis indicated that these satellites executed scans daily or once every few days during the period when Op Sindoor was underway. Op Sindoor is a significant naval exercise regularly conducted by the Indian Navy, designed to test operational readiness, validate maritime strategies, and enhance inter-fleet coordination in the Arabian Sea. The exercise typically involves extensive deployment of naval vessels, aircraft, and personnel.

The Yaogan constellation, to which Yaogan-34A, Yaogan-34B, and Yaogan-34C belong, comprises a diverse group of Earth observation satellites. While officially designated for civilian purposes such as land resources survey, urban planning, crop yield estimation, and disaster prevention, various international defense and space analysts often categorize elements of the Yaogan series as having military or dual-use applications. These capabilities include providing high-resolution imagery and electronic signals intelligence, making them valuable assets for surveillance and reconnaissance. Yaogan-34A was launched in 2021, followed by Yaogan-34B and -34C in 2022.

Digantara's report underscores the growing imperative for robust Space Situational Awareness (SSA) capabilities. The ability to track, identify, and analyze the activities of other nations' satellites is becoming a critical component of national security for states globally. For India, an indigenous capability to monitor space assets provides a strategic advantage in understanding potential threats and maintaining domain awareness in an increasingly congested and contested outer space environment.

The findings from Digantara focus on the factual reporting of observed satellite behavior and its context within known Indian naval operations. The report does not offer speculation regarding the specific data gathered by the Chinese satellites or their intent. However, it highlights the routine nature of satellite-based surveillance conducted by global powers and the strategic importance of monitoring such activities. This development also emphasizes the increasing contributions of private Indian companies to enhancing the nation's strategic space capabilities.

As space continues to evolve into a vital domain for both civilian and military applications, continuous monitoring and analysis of orbital activities are expected to remain a key focus for national security agencies worldwide. This observation by Digantara represents a public instance of an Indian private entity reporting on foreign satellite activities over sensitive Indian zones, contributing to a broader understanding of space-based surveillance dynamics.
