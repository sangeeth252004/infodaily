Former President Donald Trump has publicly advised Jensen Huang, CEO of Nvidia, against invoking the Fifth Amendment of the U.S. Constitution. This statement comes amid reports that the U.S. Department of Justice is exploring potential antitrust violations by the artificial intelligence (AI) chip manufacturing giant.

Mr. Trump conveyed his advice during a recent public appearance, specifically addressing Mr. Huang. He stated, "I hope that Jensen Huang, the head of Nvidia... doesn't take the Fifth Amendment," adding his view that "The innocent don't take the Fifth Amendment." These remarks were made as Nvidia faces increasing scrutiny over its dominant position in the rapidly expanding AI hardware market.

The Fifth Amendment to the U.S. Constitution provides several key protections for individuals, most notably the right against self-incrimination. This provision allows a person to refuse to answer questions or provide testimony that might incriminate them in a criminal proceeding. It is a fundamental component of due process rights within the American legal system. Invoking this right does not constitute an admission of guilt.

*   **Context of Scrutiny:** Nvidia, founded in 1993, has become a pivotal player in the global technology sector, particularly for its specialized graphics processing units (GPUs) essential for AI development. The company's market capitalization has soared, recently reaching approximately $3 trillion, positioning it as one of the world's most valuable companies. Its near-monopoly in high-end AI chips has reportedly drawn the attention of antitrust regulators.
*   **Justice Department Inquiry:** While no formal charges have been filed, the Justice Department's exploration of potential antitrust violations signals a heightened level of regulatory oversight. This inquiry is part of a broader governmental focus on the market power of major technology companies.
*   **Trump's Own History:** Notably, Mr. Trump himself invoked the Fifth Amendment multiple times during a civil fraud investigation in New York. In August 2022, he confirmed that he had invoked his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination more than 400 times during a deposition related to the investigation into his business practices by the New York Attorney General's office.

Nvidia has not publicly commented on the reported antitrust inquiry or Mr. Trump's specific remarks regarding Mr. Huang. The company continues to operate as the leading supplier of AI chips, a critical component in the ongoing technological advancement of artificial intelligence across various industries.

The Justice Department's inquiry into Nvidia's business practices remains ongoing. Future developments will depend on the findings of this investigation and any subsequent legal actions that may or may not be pursued.
