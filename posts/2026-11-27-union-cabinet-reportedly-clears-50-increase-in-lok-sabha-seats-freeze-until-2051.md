The Union Cabinet has reportedly cleared a proposal to increase the number of Lok Sabha seats by 50% across states, with a new ceiling on further increases to remain until 2051. Reports on December 10, 2024, indicate this move aims to address long-standing disparities in parliamentary representation across India.

This significant electoral reform would potentially raise the total number of parliamentary constituencies from the current 543 elected seats to approximately 815. The initiative is intended to update the representation framework, which has largely remained static for decades despite substantial population growth and demographic shifts.

Currently, the freeze on delimitation, the process of redrawing electoral boundaries and allocating seats, is based on the 1971 Census data and was extended until 2026. The proposed expansion and subsequent delimitation exercise are anticipated to utilize data from the 2021 Census once its final figures are released. This would mark a substantial recalibration of political weight across states.

Key details of the reported cabinet decision include:
*   **Constitutional Amendment:** The proposal necessitates a constitutional amendment to facilitate both the expansion of seats and the subsequent freeze.
*   **Population Basis:** The new allocation would be based on the latest finalized population data from the 2021 Census.
*   **New Parliament Capacity:** The recently inaugurated Parliament building has been designed with increased seating capacity, capable of accommodating a larger number of Members of Parliament (MPs), aligning with such an expansion.
*   **Addressing Disparities:** The initiative seeks to reconcile the disproportionate representation that has emerged due to varying population growth rates across states since the 1970s. States, particularly in the South, that implemented successful population control measures have historically expressed concerns about potential reductions in their political weight if delimitation were based solely on more recent population figures. The proposed framework, which includes an initial increase followed by a new freeze until 2051, aims to provide a revised and stable representational structure.

Following the initial 50% increase, the number of Lok Sabha seats would be frozen at the new level until 2051. This mechanism mirrors the existing freeze, providing a predictable framework for political representation over the next quarter-century. The proposal's formalization and passage would involve parliamentary debate and approval, leading to the actual delimitation exercise and seat reallocation after the 2026 deadline and based on the finalized 2021 Census data.
