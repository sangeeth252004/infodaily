### Encryption of Internet Traffic

When you connect to a VPN, your device establishes a secure, encrypted tunnel between itself and the VPN server. All data that travels between your device and the VPN server is then scrambled using cryptographic algorithms. This means that even if someone were to intercept your internet traffic, such as your Internet Service Provider (ISP) or someone on the same public Wi-Fi network, they would only see unintelligible code instead of your actual data. This encryption is crucial for protecting sensitive information like passwords, financial details, and personal communications from prying eyes.

### Masking Your IP Address

Your IP address is a unique numerical label assigned to your device when it connects to the internet, acting like a digital address for your online activity. A VPN hides your real IP address by routing your traffic through one of its own servers. When your data exits the VPN server to reach its final destination, it uses the VPN server's IP address. This makes it appear as though your internet activity originates from the VPN server's location, not your actual physical location. This anonymity helps prevent websites, advertisers, and other entities from tracking your online behavior and building profiles based on your IP address.

**Example:**
Imagine you are sending a postcard through the regular mail. Anyone handling the postcard can read your message and see your return address. Now, imagine you put that postcard inside a sealed, opaque envelope with only a generic return address that belongs to a trusted intermediary. The intermediary receives the envelope, takes out your postcard, places it in their own envelope with their return address, and then sends it to the final recipient. The final recipient sees the intermediary's address, and no one along the way can read the original message.

### Limitations and Edge Cases

While VPNs offer significant privacy and security benefits, they are not an absolute guarantee of anonymity. The VPN provider itself can potentially see your internet activity, depending on their logging policies. Therefore, choosing a reputable VPN provider with a strict no-logs policy is important. Additionally, if you log into websites or services while using a VPN, those services will still know who you are through your account information, regardless of your masked IP address. Browser fingerprinting techniques can also sometimes be used to identify users even when their IP addresses are hidden.
