### Immutable Record-Keeping

Blockchain utilizes cryptography to create a chain of blocks, where each block contains a batch of transactions. Once a block is added to the chain, it is extremely difficult to alter or delete its contents without the consensus of the network. This immutability means that records of product origin, handling, and transfers are permanent and tamper-proof.

### Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT)

Instead of relying on a single, centralized database, blockchain distributes the ledger across multiple computers (nodes) within the network. This decentralization eliminates a single point of failure and makes it significantly harder for malicious actors to compromise the entire system. All participants holding a copy of the ledger can verify the accuracy of transactions.

### Enhanced Transparency and Traceability

Every step a product takes in the supply chain, from raw material sourcing to final delivery, can be recorded on the blockchain. This creates a transparent audit trail, allowing all authorized stakeholders to track the product's provenance, authenticity, and condition in near real-time. This visibility helps in identifying bottlenecks, verifying compliance, and combating counterfeit goods.

### Smart Contracts for Automation

Smart contracts are self-executing contracts with the terms of the agreement directly written into code. They can be deployed on a blockchain to automate various supply chain processes. For instance, a smart contract could automatically trigger payment to a supplier once the goods are verified as received and their quality is confirmed on the blockchain.

### Example: Pharmaceutical Supply Chain

Consider the pharmaceutical industry, where counterfeit drugs pose a significant risk. A blockchain can track each batch of medication from the manufacturing plant, through distributors and pharmacies, to the patient. Each handover and temperature check can be recorded. If a drug is suspected of being counterfeit, its entire history can be quickly verified on the blockchain, distinguishing it from legitimate products.

### Limitations and Edge Cases

While blockchain offers robust security, its effectiveness depends on the integrity of the data entered initially. If inaccurate information is recorded on the blockchain (e.g., incorrect product details or false origin information), the blockchain itself will faithfully store that incorrect data. Furthermore, the scalability of some blockchain networks can be a concern for high-volume supply chains, and the cost of implementation and integration can be substantial. The adoption rate and standardization across different entities within a supply chain are also crucial factors.
