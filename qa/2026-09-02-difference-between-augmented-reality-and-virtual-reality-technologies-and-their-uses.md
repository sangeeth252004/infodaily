### Augmented Reality (AR)

Augmented reality blends digital elements with the physical world. This is typically achieved through devices like smartphones, tablets, or specialized AR glasses. The user can still see and interact with their real environment while digital content, such as images, sounds, or text, is superimposed onto it. The goal is to provide context-aware information or enhance the user's experience of their existing reality.

**Uses of AR:**
*   **Navigation:** AR apps can overlay directions onto a street view, making it easier to find your way.
*   **Retail:** Customers can virtually "try on" clothes or place furniture in their homes before buying.
*   **Education:** Students can see 3D models of historical artifacts or anatomical structures appear in their classroom.
*   **Gaming:** AR games often involve interacting with digital characters or objects that appear in the user's real-world space.

**Example:**
Using a smartphone app to see how a new couch would look in your living room, with the app overlaying a 3D model of the couch onto the live camera feed of your room.

### Virtual Reality (VR)

Virtual reality completely replaces the user's real-world surroundings with a computer-generated environment. This is achieved using a head-mounted display (HMD) that blocks out external visual input and presents a stereoscopic 3D view. VR aims to create a sense of presence, making the user feel as if they are actually within the virtual world.

**Uses of VR:**
*   **Gaming and Entertainment:** VR offers highly immersive gaming experiences and virtual cinema.
*   **Training and Simulation:** VR is used for realistic training in fields like surgery, aviation, and manufacturing, allowing for practice in safe, controlled environments.
*   **Therapy and Rehabilitation:** VR can be used to treat phobias, PTSD, and to aid in physical rehabilitation by creating engaging exercises.
*   **Design and Architecture:** Professionals can walk through virtual models of buildings before they are constructed.

**Example:**
Wearing a VR headset to experience a simulated flight over a mountain range, where your view and any interaction are entirely within the digital landscape.

### Key Differences and Limitations

The primary distinction lies in the degree of immersion and the relationship with the real world. AR enhances reality; VR replaces it.

**Limitations:**
*   **AR:** Requires specific hardware (smartphones, AR glasses) and can be limited by the device's processing power and camera capabilities. The integration can sometimes feel clunky if not perfectly aligned.
*   **VR:** Can cause motion sickness or eye strain in some individuals. It requires dedicated, often expensive, hardware and completely isolates the user from their physical environment, posing safety risks if the user is not aware of their surroundings.
