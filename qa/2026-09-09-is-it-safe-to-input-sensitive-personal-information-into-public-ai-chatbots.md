### Data Handling and Storage

Public chatbot services are designed to collect and process user inputs to provide responses and improve their systems. This means that any text or data you type into these chatbots is typically transmitted to and stored on the service provider's servers. This data retention is often necessary for the system to learn, recall past conversations (if applicable), and refine its performance over time.

### Privacy and Security Risks

When sensitive personal information is entered into a public chatbot, it becomes part of the stored data. This creates several risks:

*   **Potential Access:** The service provider's employees or authorized third parties may have access to this data for various purposes, such as quality control, system maintenance, or research.
*   **Data Breaches:** Stored data is vulnerable to cyberattacks and data breaches. If a breach occurs, your sensitive information could be exposed to unauthorized individuals.
*   **Training Data Inclusion:** Many chatbots use user interactions as part of the dataset to train and improve their underlying models. Information provided, even if sensitive, could inadvertently become part of the training data and potentially be exposed in unforeseen ways.

### Example of Information to Avoid

Examples of sensitive personal information that should not be shared with public chatbots include:

*   Full names in conjunction with other identifying details
*   Social Security numbers or equivalent national identification numbers
*   Bank account numbers, credit card details, or other financial information
*   Medical records or private health information
*   Passwords or login credentials for any service
*   Home addresses or other precise location data

### Terms of Service and Limitations

While each public chatbot service has its own terms of service and privacy policy, these documents often state that users should not input sensitive or confidential information. Users typically bear the responsibility for the data they choose to share. It is prudent to assume that any information entered into a publicly accessible digital service may not remain entirely private.
