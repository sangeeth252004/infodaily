### What is Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)?
Two-factor authentication (2FA) is a security process that requires users to provide two different authentication factors to verify their identity. It combines something the user knows (like a password) with something the user has (like a mobile device or a physical key) or something the user is (like a fingerprint). This method adds a substantial barrier to unauthorized access.

### Why Enable 2FA?
Passwords alone are often susceptible to various threats, including data breaches, phishing attempts, and brute-force attacks. If a password is stolen or guessed, an unauthorized person could gain full access to an account. 2FA mitigates this risk by requiring a second, distinct piece of information that the attacker likely does not possess, even if they have the correct password. It significantly reduces the chances of account compromise.

### Accounts That Benefit Most from 2FA
It is advisable to enable 2FA on any online account containing sensitive information or that could be used to access other accounts. High-priority accounts include:
*   **Email accounts:** Often serve as recovery mechanisms for other services.
*   **Financial accounts:** Banking, investment, and payment services.
*   **Social media platforms:** To protect personal identity and privacy.
*   **Cloud storage and productivity suites:** Where personal and professional documents are stored.
*   **Shopping accounts:** Especially those with stored payment information.

### How it Works: A Simple Example
When 2FA is enabled, the login process typically involves two steps:
1.  **First Factor:** The user enters their username and password as usual.
2.  **Second Factor:** The service then prompts for a second verification. This might be a unique code sent via text message to the user's registered phone, a code generated by an authenticator app (e.g., Google Authenticator, Authy), or a confirmation prompt sent to a trusted device. The user must enter this second factor to successfully log in.

### Important Considerations and Limitations
While 2FA greatly improves security, it is not entirely foolproof. Some considerations include:
*   **Phishing for 2FA codes:** Sophisticated phishing attacks can sometimes trick users into revealing their one-time codes. Users should always verify the legitimacy of the login page.
*   **SIM-swapping attacks:** In some cases, attackers can transfer a user's phone number to a new SIM card, intercepting SMS-based 2FA codes. Using authenticator apps or physical security keys can offer stronger protection against this.
*   **Loss of the second factor:** If the device used for the second factor (e.g., smartphone) is lost, stolen, or damaged, users may be locked out of their accounts. It is crucial to save backup codes provided by services during 2FA setup and store them securely, or configure alternative recovery methods.
