### Augmented Reality (AR)

Augmented reality technology integrates digital information with the user's real-world environment in real-time. It augments the existing reality by superimposing virtual objects, sounds, or other sensory input onto live views. The user remains present and aware of their physical surroundings while interacting with digital enhancements.

*   **Experience:** Users view the actual world through a device (like a smartphone camera or specialized glasses), and digital elements appear as if they are part of that real world. This enhances perception and interaction with the physical environment.
*   **Hardware:** Common AR experiences are delivered via smartphones and tablets. Dedicated AR glasses, such as Microsoft HoloLens, also exist, offering a hands-free experience with transparent lenses that allow the real world to remain visible.
*   **Example:** Applications like Pokémon GO overlay digital characters onto real-world locations seen through a phone's camera. The IKEA Place app allows users to virtually place furniture in their homes to visualize how it would look.
*   **Limitations:** AR's effectiveness can be impacted by factors like lighting conditions, the processing power of the device, and the accuracy of environmental tracking. Achieving realistic occlusion (where digital objects correctly appear behind real ones) can be technically challenging for seamless integration.

### Virtual Reality (VR)

Virtual reality technology creates a fully immersive, synthetic environment that replaces the user's perception of the real world. Users are transported into a completely digital space, disconnecting them from their physical surroundings. The goal is to generate a strong sense of presence within this simulated world.

*   **Experience:** Users wear a head-mounted display (HMD) that covers their eyes and often ears, blocking out all external sensory input. This display shows a computer-generated 3D environment, creating the illusion of being elsewhere.
*   **Hardware:** VR experiences require specialized headsets, such as the Meta Quest, HTC Vive, or PlayStation VR. These headsets typically include screens, lenses, and motion sensors to track head and body movements, allowing users to navigate and interact within the virtual space.
*   **Example:** Playing an immersive video game where the user feels they are inside the game world. VR is also used for training simulations, such as surgical procedures or flight training, and for virtual tourism or architectural walkthroughs.
*   **Limitations:** Some users may experience motion sickness (cybersickness) due to the discrepancy between visual input and physical sensations. High-fidelity VR systems can be expensive and often require a dedicated, clear physical space for safe movement.

### Key Distinctions

The core difference lies in their approach to reality: AR adds to it, while VR replaces it. AR maintains the user's connection to the physical world, augmenting it with digital layers. VR completely isolates the user within a fabricated digital world, aiming for full immersion and a sense of "being there" in the simulated environment.
