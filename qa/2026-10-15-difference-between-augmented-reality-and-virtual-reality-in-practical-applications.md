### Augmented Reality (AR)

Augmented reality integrates digital content and information with the user's real-world environment in real-time. It typically uses devices like smartphones, tablets, or smart glasses to display digital overlays directly onto a live view of the physical world. Users remain aware of and can interact with their actual surroundings.

*   **How it Works:** AR systems identify physical objects or locations and then project relevant digital information, 3D models, or interactive elements onto them. This can be achieved through camera-based tracking or spatial mapping.
*   **Practical Applications:**
    *   **Navigation:** Displaying directions directly onto a live view of the road through a car windshield or a smartphone camera.
    *   **Maintenance and Repair:** Technicians can see digital overlays of schematics or repair instructions directly on a piece of machinery, guiding them step-by-step.
    *   **Retail:** Customers can virtually "try on" clothes or place virtual furniture in their homes to visualize how it looks before purchase.
    *   **Gaming:** Games like Pokémon GO overlay digital characters onto real-world locations, encouraging physical exploration.
*   **Limitations:** AR experiences are often limited by the display capabilities and field of view of the device being used. The digital content can sometimes appear less integrated with the physical world compared to more advanced mixed reality systems.

### Virtual Reality (VR)

Virtual reality creates a completely artificial, computer-generated environment that simulates physical presence in a way that blocks out the real world. Users typically wear head-mounted displays that provide a stereoscopic view, often accompanied by headphones for auditory immersion and controllers for interaction.

*   **How it Works:** VR systems render a 3D digital world that the user can explore and interact with, often with 360-degree vision. Head tracking technology allows the user's view within the virtual environment to change as they move their head, enhancing the sense of presence.
*   **Practical Applications:**
    *   **Training Simulations:** Pilots, surgeons, and soldiers can practice complex procedures in safe, realistic virtual environments without real-world risks or costs.
    *   **Entertainment:** Immersive video games and virtual tours allow users to experience fantastical worlds or travel to distant locations from their homes.
    *   **Design and Architecture:** Architects and designers can walk through virtual prototypes of buildings or products before physical construction, identifying flaws and refining designs.
    *   **Therapy:** VR is used for exposure therapy for phobias or to manage pain by distracting patients in calming virtual spaces.
*   **Limitations:** VR can sometimes cause motion sickness or disorientation due to the disconnect between visual input and physical movement. It requires specialized hardware and can be isolating from the real world, making multi-user experiences in the same physical space challenging without additional technology.

### Key Distinction

The core practical difference is the degree of immersion and presence within the real world. AR enhances reality by adding digital layers, allowing users to remain present in their physical surroundings. VR replaces reality entirely with a digital one, aiming for complete immersion and a sense of presence within the simulated environment.
