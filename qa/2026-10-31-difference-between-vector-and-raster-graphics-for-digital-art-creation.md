### Vector Graphics

Vector graphics are built using mathematical formulas that describe points, lines, curves, and polygons. These formulas dictate the position, direction, and length of these elements. Because they are based on mathematical calculations rather than a fixed number of dots, vector images can be scaled to any size – from a small icon to a large billboard – without any degradation in sharpness or clarity. When you resize a vector image, the software recalculates the mathematical equations to render the image at the new dimensions.

**Example:** A logo designed in vector format can be used on a business card, a website, or a large banner with the same crisp appearance. Common file formats include SVG, AI, and EPS.

### Raster Graphics

Raster graphics, also known as bitmap graphics, are composed of a grid of individual colored squares called pixels. Each pixel has a specific color value. The overall image is formed by the arrangement and color of all these pixels. The quality of a raster image is tied to its resolution, which is typically measured in pixels per inch (PPI) or dots per inch (DPI). When a raster image is scaled up beyond its original resolution, the pixels become larger and more visible, resulting in a blurry or "pixelated" appearance.

**Example:** Photographs taken with digital cameras are raster images. Editing a photograph in software like Photoshop involves manipulating these individual pixels. Common file formats include JPG, PNG, and GIF.

### Key Differences in Digital Art Creation

*   **Scalability:** Vector graphics are infinitely scalable; raster graphics are resolution-dependent and lose quality when enlarged.
*   **File Size:** Vector files are generally smaller as they store mathematical instructions, while raster files can become very large, especially at high resolutions.
*   **Editing:** Vector art is ideal for editing shapes, lines, and solid colors, making it suitable for logos, illustrations, and typography. Raster art is best for detailed images with complex color gradients and textures, such as photographs and digital paintings.
*   **Creation Tools:** Vector graphics are created with programs like Adobe Illustrator or Inkscape, while raster graphics are created with programs like Adobe Photoshop or GIMP.

### Limitations and Edge Cases

For very complex, photorealistic images, creating them in vector format can be extremely computationally intensive and may not achieve the same level of detail as raster. Conversely, attempting to create intricate, painterly artwork in a vector program can be cumbersome compared to pixel-based editing. Sometimes, designers will convert raster images to vector (tracing), but this process is an approximation and may not perfectly replicate the original detail.
