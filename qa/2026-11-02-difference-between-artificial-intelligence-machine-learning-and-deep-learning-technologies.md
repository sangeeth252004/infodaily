### Artificial Intelligence (AI)
Artificial intelligence is a broad scientific field focused on developing machines capable of simulating or exhibiting intelligent behavior. Its goal is to enable computers to perform tasks that typically require human intellect, such as problem-solving, decision-making, learning, language understanding, and pattern recognition. AI encompasses various methodologies, from symbolic, rule-based systems to modern statistical approaches.

*   **Example:** A classic chess-playing computer, a self-driving car managing navigation, or a virtual assistant understanding voice commands all fall under the umbrella of AI.

### Machine Learning (ML)
Machine learning is a core component within the field of AI, specifically focused on enabling systems to learn from data rather than being explicitly programmed for every scenario. ML algorithms analyze vast datasets, identify underlying patterns, and then use these learned insights to make predictions or decisions on new, unseen data. This iterative learning process allows models to improve their performance over time.

*   **How it works:** An ML algorithm might be fed thousands of pictures labeled "cat" or "not cat." Over time, it learns the distinguishing features of cats and can then accurately identify cats in new images.
*   **Limitations:** Machine learning models require significant amounts of high-quality data for training. They can also struggle to generalize effectively if the new data differs significantly from their training data, and the decision-making process of complex models can sometimes lack transparency.

### Deep Learning (DL)
Deep learning is a specialized subfield within machine learning that utilizes artificial neural networks with multiple hidden layers—hence the term "deep." Inspired by the structure and function of the human brain, these deep neural networks are highly adept at automatically discovering intricate features and hierarchical representations directly from raw, unstructured data like images, audio, and text.

*   **How it works:** In a deep neural network, each layer processes the input from the previous layer, transforming it into a more abstract and refined representation. This multi-layered processing allows the network to learn increasingly complex patterns, such as recognizing edges, then shapes, then objects in an image.
*   **Example:** Facial recognition systems that identify individuals in photos, natural language translation services, and speech recognition powering voice assistants are common applications of deep learning.
*   **Limitations:** Deep learning models demand extremely large datasets and considerable computational resources (like powerful GPUs) for training. Their complexity can also make them "black boxes," meaning it is difficult for humans to fully understand or interpret the specific reasoning behind their predictions.
