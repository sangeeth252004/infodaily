### Inherent Risks of Public Wi-Fi
Public Wi-Fi networks, such as those found in cafes, airports, or libraries, are designed for easy access and often have minimal security protocols. Many of these networks do not use strong encryption, or they may lack any encryption at all, making the data transmitted over them vulnerable.

### Specific Threats to Online Transactions
When a user connects to an unsecured public Wi-Fi network, their internet traffic can be exposed to various threats:
*   **Eavesdropping/Sniffing:** Attackers can use software to "listen in" on unencrypted data transmissions, potentially capturing sensitive information like credit card numbers, login credentials, and personal details as they are entered.
*   **Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attacks:** An attacker can position themselves between a user's device and the website they are trying to access. This allows them to intercept, read, or even alter the communication without the user or the website being aware.
*   **Malicious Hotspots:** Cybercriminals may set up fake Wi-Fi networks with names similar to legitimate public hotspots to trick users into connecting. Once connected, the attacker has direct access to the user's data.

*Example:* If a user enters credit card details on an e-commerce website while connected to a public Wi-Fi network that does not secure the connection, an attacker on the same network could potentially intercept this data before it reaches the secure server of the website.

### Enhanced Security Measures and Best Practices
To mitigate these risks, it is advisable to:
*   **Use Mobile Data:** Your smartphone's cellular data connection (4G/5G) is typically more secure than public Wi-Fi because it creates a direct, encrypted connection between your device and your mobile provider.
*   **Utilize a Virtual Private Network (VPN):** A reputable VPN encrypts all internet traffic from your device, creating a secure tunnel regardless of the underlying network. This makes it much harder for anyone on a public Wi-Fi network to intercept or decipher your data.
*   **Verify HTTPS:** Always ensure the website you are visiting uses HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure), indicated by "https://" in the URL and a padlock icon in the browser's address bar. While HTTPS encrypts the connection between your browser and the website, protecting the content of your data, it is still best combined with a VPN on public networks.

### When Security is Improved (Limitations)
While HTTPS offers a significant layer of security for the data exchanged with a website, using it on an insecure public Wi-Fi network still carries some risk. A strong VPN provides comprehensive encryption for *all* traffic from your device over the public network, offering a higher level of protection against network-level eavesdropping and MitM attacks. Therefore, for any sensitive transactions, combining a VPN with HTTPS on a trusted website is the most secure approach when a private network is unavailable.
